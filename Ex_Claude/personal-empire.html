How Napoleon's System Translates to Your Life

Napoleon governed France the way a CEO governs a company — with clear ministries, fixed review cadences, and metrics for every domain. His ministers submitted structured reports ("tableaux") at weekly sessions. He ran War on Mondays, Interior on Thursdays, Finance on Sundays. Nothing was left to mood or chance.

The Critical Shift in Thinking
Napoleon didn't manage "tasks" or "habits." He managed institutions with domains, budgets, and metrics. You are not trying to be more productive. You are governing an empire of one — where every domain has a mandate, a resource allocation, and a review cadence. The goal is not self-improvement. It is institutional development.
How Ministries Feed Each Other

Napoleon's empire was described as a "spider's web" — Paris at the centre, each ministry a node, with flows of resources, intelligence, and power between them. Neglect one node and the web weakens everywhere. Your domains work the same way.

I · Culture & Identity — The Constitution
Feeds ALL 7 other ministries. Your Ikigai and principles are the mandate every other ministry operates under. Without this, ministries make decisions without a governing law. Napoleon's state ideology gave every prefect a shared direction. Yours must do the same.
II · Infrastructure & Systems — The Roads
Feeds ALL 7 other ministries. Napoleon's roads and bridges were what made military campaigns, tax collection, and trade possible. Your health, habits, and routines are what make every other ministry operable. A broken infrastructure means all other domains run at reduced capacity.
III · Career Defense — The Standing Army
Feeds Treasury (current income), Intelligence (what skills matter), Alliances (professional network). Napoleon maintained his armies even in peacetime. You must maintain your career even when not under threat. A neglected army is defeated before the battle begins.
IV · Commerce & Enterprise — The Navy
Feeds Treasury (future independence), Intelligence (forces real learning), Alliances (new partnerships). Napoleon's Navy opened new territories and trade routes. Your business project opens new income lines — it is how your empire expands beyond its current borders.
V · Intelligence & Learning — The Lycées
Feeds Career Defense (skill edge), Commerce (business insight), Culture (expands your worldview). Napoleon's Lycées produced every minister, general, and administrator. Your learning builds the talent that runs your empire's other ministries.
VI · Treasury & Finance — The Bank of France
Feeds Base Operations (stable home), Commerce (capital to launch), Infrastructure (resources for health). Napoleon created the Bank of France in 1800 to end financial chaos. Your safety bucket does the same — when Treasury is stable, background anxiety drops and every other ministry operates more clearly.
VII · Alliances & Relations — Foreign Affairs
Feeds Commerce (customers, partners), Intelligence (mentors, access), Career Defense (referrals, opportunities). Napoleon famously said he who fights alone loses. Every major expansion in your empire will come through people. Neglect this ministry and the other six operate in isolation.
VIII · Base Operations — The Prefects
Feeds Infrastructure (a stable base enables good habits), Culture (your environment shapes your thinking). Napoleon's prefects were called "little emperors" — they maintained local order so Paris could think at the level of empire. Your daily structure and home environment must do the same for your mind.
The Imperial Notebook — Governance Structure

Napoleon's ministers submitted structured "tableaux" — status tables for each domain — at fixed weekly sessions. Each ministry page in your notebook is your personal tableau. Updated on its own cadence. Reviewed at Council Sessions.

Masses de Granit — The Unshakeable Foundations
Napoleon called his core institutions "masses of granite" — the bedrock everything else rested on. Three of your ministries function the same way. Without them, the others crumble.

Council Session Cadence
Daily — 5-Minute Morning Session
DUMP · QUESTION · INSIGHT · INTENTION
Which ministry needs a directive today?
Weekly — Council of State (Sunday · 20 min)
Rate each ministry 1–5. One sentence on status. Set the week's campaign objective. Identify which ministry is most at risk this week.
Monthly — Imperial Review (Last Sunday · 45 min)
Full tableau review: all 8 ministries. Update the empire map. Set next month's priority ministry. Where are the cross-ministry gaps?
Quarterly — Constitutional Review (3 months)
Return to Ministry I — Culture & Identity. Has your Ikigai shifted? Do your principles still hold? Rewrite the Constitution if needed. Everything else follows from here.
