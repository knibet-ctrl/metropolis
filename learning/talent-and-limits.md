## The core distinction, stated before anything else

**Heritability (h²) is a population-level variance statistic. It is the
proportion of *variance in a trait, in a particular population, at a particular
time, under that population's range of environments*, that is statistically
associated with genetic variance.**

**It is therefore NOT:**

| Common misreading | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| "60% of my intelligence is genetic" | Heritability says nothing about any individual. It is a property of a population's variance, not of a person's trait. There is no coherent way to divide one person's ability into genetic and environmental portions |
| "h² = 0.6 means the trait can only change by 40%" | **Flatly false.** Height is roughly 80% heritable and Dutch mean height rose by around 20 cm in 150 years — an entirely environmental change to a highly heritable trait. Phenylketonuria is essentially 100% heritable and completely controlled by diet |
| "High heritability means education can't help" | Heritability is estimated *within the existing range of environments*. Introduce a new environment and the estimate changes. It has no implications for the effect of interventions not yet tried |
| "If a trait is heritable within groups, differences between groups are genetic" | **This is a logical error, not a subtle empirical dispute.** Lewontin's (1970) seed illustration: take genetically variable seed, split it, grow one half in rich soil and one half in poor. Heritability *within* each pot is high; the difference *between* pots is entirely environmental. Within-group heritability places no constraint on between-group causes. This holds regardless of what one believes about any particular group difference |
| "Heritability is fixed" | It changes with age (rising for cognitive ability through development), with cohort, and with environmental variance. If you equalise environments, heritability goes **up**, not down — which is the opposite of what most people intuit |

**Every one of these misreadings appears in popular writing on both sides of
the debate.** The second-to-last is the one with a body count.

## What the behavioural genetics actually shows

### Cognitive ability

| Finding | Figure | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Twin and adoption estimates of h² for general cognitive ability, childhood | ~0.2-0.4 | Well established |
| Same, adulthood | ~0.6-0.8 | Well established |
| **The Wilson effect**: heritability *increases* with age, shared-environment influence falls toward ~0 by adulthood | Haworth et al. (2010), multi-cohort, n ≈ 11,000 | Robust, replicated, and counterintuitive |
| Heritability of educational attainment (years of schooling) | ~0.4 | Reasonably established |
| Shared family environment effect on adult IQ | Near zero in most Western samples | Established and deeply unpopular; the honest caveat is that these samples exclude severe deprivation |

**Turkheimer's Three Laws of Behaviour Genetics (2000)** — the best one-page
summary in the field:
1. All human behavioural traits are heritable.
2. The effect of being raised in the same family is smaller than the effect of
   genes.
3. A substantial portion of the variation in complex human behavioural traits
   is not accounted for by the effects of genes or families.

**Turkheimer added a fourth point himself: none of this tells you much, because
the causal pathways are so diffuse.** He is a critic of hereditarian
interpretation while being the author of the laws. That combination is worth
sitting with.

### Molecular genetics — the reality check

| Study | Result |
|---|---|
| **Lee et al. (2018)**, *Nature Genetics*, EA3, n ≈ 1.1 million | Polygenic score predicted roughly **11-13%** of variance in educational attainment† |
| **Okbay et al. (2022)**, EA4, n ≈ 3 million | Roughly **12-16%** in-sample; **within-family prediction is roughly half the population estimate**† |

**That within-family attenuation is the most important number in this file.**
The population-level polygenic score partly captures *environmental* effects
correlated with parental genotype — the family a child is born into — not just
direct genetic effects on the child. This is called genetic nurture (Kong et
al., 2018, *Science*, using Icelandic data: **non-transmitted parental alleles
predicted offspring educational attainment**, which is only possible via
environment). Anyone quoting a polygenic score's population R² as a measure of
individual genetic potential is overstating it by roughly a factor of two.

**Missing heritability:** twin studies say 0.6-0.8; measured DNA gets to
roughly 0.1-0.2 predictive and around 0.2-0.3 SNP-heritability. The gap is
unexplained and is itself evidence that the twin estimates rest on assumptions
(most contentiously the equal-environments assumption) that may not fully hold.

### The Scarr-Rowe / Turkheimer interaction

**Turkheimer, Haley, Waldron, D'Onofrio & Gottesman (2003)**: in a US sample of
7-year-old twins, heritability of IQ was near **zero in the poorest families
and around 0.6 in the most affluent**. Interpretation: in deprived
environments, environmental variance dominates and genetic potential is not
expressed.

**Replication status is mixed and this matters.** Tucker-Drob & Bates (2016)
meta-analysed the interaction: found it in US samples, **did not find it in
Western Europe or Australia**. The plausible reading is that the interaction
tracks the strength of the welfare floor — in countries with less deprivation,
there is less deprivation-driven variance to detect. That reading is coherent
and is not proven.

## Physical constraints — where the ceiling is undeniable

Ericsson conceded height and body size. The honest position concedes more.

| Constraint | Evidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Height in basketball** | The frequently cited figure is that roughly **17% of American men aged 20-40 who are 7 feet tall are in the NBA**† | The number is widely repeated and poorly sourced; treat the magnitude, not the digit. The direction is not in doubt |
| **ACTN3 R577X "speed gene"** | The XX genotype (no functional α-actinin-3) is essentially absent among elite sprinters; roughly 18% of Europeans carry it | Real but modest as a single locus. It is a filter, not a predictor. Do not let it stand in for a general genetic-determinism argument |
| **VO₂max trainability** | **HERITAGE Family Study** (Bouchard et al.): identical 20-week training programme, mean VO₂max gain ~19%, individual range from **essentially zero to over 50%**. Trainability itself clusters in families† | **This is the most important result in the file for practical purposes: response to identical training varies enormously and heritably** |
| **East African distance running** | Kalenjin and Oromo overrepresentation is extreme and not plausibly explained by a single factor | Best account is multi-causal: body morphology (long thin lower limbs — low moment of inertia), altitude of upbringing, running as childhood transport, and above all overwhelming economic incentive and a dense selection funnel. **Attributing it to genes alone is unsupported; attributing it to culture alone is also unsupported** |
| **Absolute pitch** | Strong critical-period effect. Very rare in those who begin music after ~6; far more common with early tonal-language exposure plus early training (Deutsch) | A clean example of gene-environment interaction with a developmental window |

**The HERITAGE result deserves emphasis because it generalises the argument
without invoking anything contentious.** Even holding practice *exactly*
constant — same programme, same duration, supervised — outcomes differ by an
order of magnitude, and the differences run in families. Whatever one believes
about cognition, differential trainability is not in serious doubt in
physiology.

## The Flynn effect and its reversal

**James Flynn (1984, 1987)**: measured IQ rose by roughly **3 points per
decade** across the developed world for most of the twentieth century, with the
largest gains on the most abstract, least knowledge-loaded tests (Raven's
Progressive Matrices), and the smallest on vocabulary and arithmetic.

**This is the strongest single piece of evidence for large environmental
malleability of measured cognitive ability.** A ~30-point rise over a century
in a trait with adult heritability of 0.6-0.8 demonstrates, without any further
argument, that high heritability does not imply fixity.

**Candidate causes** (none individually sufficient): nutrition; reduced
infectious disease and parasite load; smaller family size; more and longer
schooling; the increasing abstraction and "scientific spectacles" of modern
work and media (Flynn's own preferred explanation); reduced lead exposure;
test-taking familiarity. Dickens & Flynn (2001) proposed a **multiplier model**
in which small genetic advantages get amplified by environmental feedback —
which simultaneously explains high heritability and large environmental
effects. It is the most elegant reconciliation available and remains a model,
not a measurement.

**The reversal.** Gains have stopped or reversed in several countries:

- **Bratsberg & Rogeberg (2018), *PNAS***: Norwegian military conscription data,
  covering birth cohorts from 1962 to 1991. Scores rose then fell, roughly 7
  points per generation at the turn.† **Critically, the decline was observed
  *within families* — brothers born later scored lower — which rules out
  compositional explanations such as immigration or differential fertility, and
  points to environmental causes.**
- Similar reversals reported in Denmark, Finland, and the UK; Sundet et al.
  (2004) found the Norwegian plateau earlier.

**The within-family design is what makes this convincing.** It is also awkward
for everyone: it undercuts hereditarian compositional explanations of decline
*and* it undercuts naive optimism about educational progress.

## Steelmanning both readings of the same data

### The hereditarian reading, at its strongest

Adult heritability of cognitive ability is high and replicated across twin,
adoption and sibling designs, and across countries. Shared-environment effects
on adult outcomes are near zero in the studied range — meaning that within the
normal Western range, differences in parenting and schooling explain far less
of adult cognitive variance than people assume. Adoption studies show adoptees'
adult IQ correlating with biological rather than adoptive parents. Polygenic
scores predict outcomes and do so partly within families, so the signal is not
purely confounding. Interventions to raise IQ have a long history of fadeout
(Head Start effects on test scores attenuate substantially by adolescence,
though non-cognitive and life outcomes persist — a genuinely important
qualification). Refusing to consider genetic contributions has itself caused
harm: it produced decades of blaming parents for conditions now known to be
substantially genetic, autism and schizophrenia being the clearest cases.

### The environmentalist reading, at its strongest

The Flynn effect is a ~30-point secular shift in a trait with h² of 0.7 — that
alone falsifies any inference from heritability to fixity, and it is not
disputed by anyone. Within-family polygenic prediction is roughly *half* the
population estimate, meaning a large part of the apparent genetic signal is
genetic *nurture* — parents' genotypes acting through the environments they
create (Kong et al. 2018). Missing heritability means twin estimates rest on
assumptions that molecular data has not vindicated. Twin studies' equal-
environments assumption is contested and identical twins are treated more
similarly. Heritability estimates come from populations with restricted
environmental variance; by construction, they cannot capture the effects of
environments not present in the sample. Schooling causally raises IQ: Ritchie &
Tucker-Drob's (2018) meta-analysis of three quasi-experimental designs found
roughly **1-5 IQ points per additional year**, which is a large lever. Adoption
into higher-SES homes raises IQ substantially in childhood (Duyme et al. 1999
reported large gains for late-adopted deprived children).† And the entire
research programme has a documented record of premature, harmful conclusions.

### Where I think a fair reading lands

- **Substantial, heritable individual differences in rate of skill acquisition
  exist.** TIER 1. Denying this requires ignoring HERITAGE, Gobet & Campitelli,
  and the whole behavioural genetics literature.
- **Those differences do not establish individual ceilings.** TIER 1 in the
  logic. Nothing in a variance statistic licenses a prediction about how far a
  given person can go with a training regime not present in the sample.
- **The environmental share is large and partially manipulable, and we do not
  know which levers work.** TIER 1 on the first clause, TIER 2 on the second.
  The Flynn effect proves the levers exist; nobody can name them with
  confidence.
- **Between-group differences cannot be inferred from within-group
  heritability.** TIER 1, and it is a matter of logic, not of politics.
- **Any strong claim about the genetic basis of group differences in cognitive
  ability.** TIER 3 at best, and in most formulations TIER 4 — because the
  environments being compared are not equalised and cannot currently be
  equalised, so the required experiment does not exist.

## Why this area attracts bad reasoning

| Failure | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Motivated reasoning at maximum strength** | Both political directions have prior commitments. Kahan's cultural cognition work predicts this is the *worst* case: more numerate people become more polarised, not less |
| **A genuine asymmetry of consequence** | Overclaiming genetic determinism has historically underwritten sterilisation, immigration restriction and segregation. Overclaiming environmentalism has historically underwritten refrigerator-mother theories of autism and coercive re-education. **Both errors have victims; they are not symmetric in scale, and pretending otherwise is its own distortion** |
| **The technical vocabulary invites misreading** | "Heritability" means something very different from its ordinary-language sense. This is not the public's fault |
| **Selection into the field** | Researchers with strong priors are the ones who choose to work here. Adversarial collaboration is almost entirely absent — in contrast to expertise.md, where it produced the cleanest result in the notebook |

## Where the popular story is wrong

| Popular story | Reality |
|---|---|
| **"Talent is a myth; anyone can do anything"** | Not supported. HERITAGE alone shows an order-of-magnitude spread in response to identical training. Gobet & Campitelli show an eightfold spread in hours to chess mastery |
| **"It's all genetic; effort is at the margins"** | Not supported. The Flynn effect is a ~30-point secular change. Within-family polygenic prediction is about half the headline figure. Schooling causally raises IQ |
| **"Heritability tells you your ceiling"** | It tells you nothing about any individual. This is the single most common error and it is a category error, not a quantitative one |
| **"High heritability means intervention is futile"** | Height: h² ≈ 0.8, +20 cm in 150 years. PKU: h² ≈ 1.0, fully managed by diet. The inference does not go through |
| **"If a trait is heritable, group gaps are genetic"** | Lewontin's two pots. Within-group heritability constrains nothing about between-group causes. This holds symmetrically — it also means group gaps cannot be assumed environmental on this evidence |
| **"The Flynn effect proves IQ is meaningless"** | It proves measured IQ is environmentally sensitive. It does not show the construct lacks validity; IQ remains among the best-validated predictors in psychology, which is why the debate matters |
| **"IQ gains from preschool prove early intervention works"** | Test-score gains from early intervention typically fade. **But the life outcomes — earnings, incarceration, health — often persist anyway (Perry Preschool, Abecedarian, and Chetty et al. on teacher value-added).** The fadeout is real and the pessimistic conclusion drawn from it is not |
| **"Scientists have settled this"** | They have not, and the loudest claims of settlement in both directions come from outside the primary literature |

## Open questions

- [ ] Why does test-score fadeout coexist with persistent life-outcome effects? This is the most interesting unresolved puzzle in applied education research.
- [ ] What is the correct within-family polygenic score attenuation factor, and does it stabilise with larger GWAS?
- [ ] Does the Scarr-Rowe interaction exist outside the US, and if not, is the welfare-floor explanation right?
- [ ] What caused the Flynn effect, and what caused its reversal? Nobody knows, and the within-family reversal finding makes the second question sharper.
- [ ] Is differential trainability (HERITAGE) present in cognitive as well as physical domains? Almost nobody has looked with an adequate design, and it would be the most decisive evidence available.
- [ ] Is there any ethically runnable study that could settle between-group questions? I do not believe there currently is, which is why I rate those claims TIER 4 rather than TIER 3.
- [ ] Does the equal-environments assumption survive direct testing? The existing tests are old and small.
