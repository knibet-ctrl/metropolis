# What We Actually Know — The Confidence Ledger

> **The key file.** Every significant claim in this notebook, sorted by how much
> weight it can bear. Read this before citing anything from the other files.

**The tiers:**

- **TIER 1 — Established.** Multiple independent lines of evidence. Overturning it would require extraordinary evidence.
- **TIER 2 — Contested but tractable.** Serious people disagree; evidence and argument are relevant; progress is possible.
- **TIER 3 — Speculative.** Coherent, unfalsified, unevidenced. Hold loosely.
- **TIER 4 — Unanswerable as posed, or malformed.** No procedure could settle it in its current form.

**Standing caveat:** every figure below was written from general knowledge with
no live verification. Tier assignments reflect my confidence in the *claim*, not
in my recall of the *citation*. Figures most worth checking are flagged with a
dagger (†).

**Field-level caveat, which downgrades everything:** education research has a
replication problem and a severe effect-size-inflation problem. Small samples,
unblinded assessors, researcher-designed outcome measures, and publication bias.
Lortie-Forgues & Inglis (2019): mean effect across 141 large NCEE/EEF trials
around **0.06 SD**.† Kraft (2020): for a well-run trial with a standardised
outcome, **0.20 SD is large**. Cheung & Slavin (2016): researcher-made outcome
measures produce roughly **double** the effect of standardised ones (~0.30 vs
~0.16).† **Every effect size below should be read against those benchmarks, not
against Cohen's.**

## TIER 1 — Established

| Claim | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Retrieval practice beats restudying for long-term retention** | Roediger & Karpicke (2006); Karpicke & Roediger (2008), *Science*. Meta-analytic g ≈ 0.50-0.61 (Rowland 2014; Adesope et al. 2017).† The effect grows with retention interval |
| **The advantage of testing over restudy REVERSES at short delay** | Restudy wins after 5 minutes; testing wins decisively after a week. This inversion is why students choose wrong |
| **Distributed practice beats massed practice** | Cepeda et al. (2006): 254 studies, >14,000 participants.† One of the oldest and most replicated findings in psychology |
| **The optimal spacing gap scales with the retention interval** | Cepeda et al. (2008), roughly 10-20% of the interval. The function is broad near the optimum |
| **The most-used study techniques are among the least effective** | Dunlosky et al. (2013): rereading and highlighting rated LOW; practice testing and distributed practice rated HIGH. Karpicke et al. (2009): ~84% of students use rereading; ~11% self-test† |
| **Learners' judgements of their own learning are systematically wrong, and sometimes inverted** | Kornell & Bjork (2008); Rhodes & Castel (2008); Carpenter et al. (2013); **Deslauriers et al. (2019), PNAS — more learning, lower perceived learning.** Convergent across paradigms |
| **Interleaving beats blocking for retention and discrimination** | Rohrer & Taylor (2007); Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic (2015); Kornell & Bjork (2008). And ~78% of learners believe the opposite† |
| **Working memory is sharply capacity-limited; the unit is the chunk** | Miller (1956); Cowan (2001), ~4±1 chunks. Chunk size is unbounded, which is the hinge of expertise |
| **Worked examples beat unguided problem solving for novices** | Sweller & Cooper (1985) and many replications |
| **The expertise reversal effect** | Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003). Instructional support that helps novices impairs experts. **There is no method-level answer to "what is good teaching"** |
| **Expert memory advantage is domain-structure-specific, not general** | Chase & Simon (1973): master advantage on real chess positions, none on random ones. Ericsson & Chase's SF: digit span ~79, letter span unchanged† |
| **Experts represent problems by deep structure; novices by surface features** | Chi, Feltovich & Glaser (1981) |
| **Intuitive expertise requires a regular environment AND feedback** | Kahneman & Klein (2009). **Reached by two researchers with opposite priors via adversarial collaboration — the cleanest result in the notebook** |
| **Mechanical prediction equals or beats expert clinical judgement** | Meehl (1954); Grove et al. (2000): ~94% of 136 comparisons.† Goldberg (1968): a linear model of the expert beats the expert |
| **Far transfer is rare and small** | Thorndike & Woodworth (1901) through Simons et al. (2016). Refuted in every generation against every generation's prior |
| **Brain training does not transfer beyond trained tasks** | Owen et al. (2010), n = 11,430, *Nature*; Melby-Lervåg & Hulme (2013); Redick et al. (2013). FTC fined Lumosity $2m in 2016 |
| **Effect size in cognitive-training studies is inversely proportional to control-group quality** | Holds across working-memory training, chess, music and video games (Sala & Gobet). **This signature is itself strong evidence of expectancy effects** |
| **Learning styles (the meshing hypothesis) have no support** | Pashler et al. (2008); Massa & Mayer (2006); Rogowsky et al. (2015, 2020). The required crossover interaction has never been found |
| **~90% of teachers believe in learning styles anyway** | Dekker et al. (2012); Howard-Jones (2014); Newton & Salvi (2020) ~89%.† The transmission failure is the finding |
| **Over one third of feedback interventions REDUCE performance** | Kluger & DeNisi (1996), 607 effect sizes.† Feedback aimed at the self rather than the task is the mechanism |
| **The forgetting curve is real and steep for meaningless material** | Ebbinghaus (1885); replicated by Murre & Dros (2015) |
| **Well-learned meaningful material is far more durable than the curve suggests** | Bahrick (1984): school Spanish essentially flat from ~6 to ~30 years after a steep initial drop† |
| **Memory is reconstructive, not reproductive** | Bartlett (1932); Loftus & Palmer (1974); Roediger & McDermott (1995); Neisser & Harsch (1992) |
| **Confidence and accuracy decouple in low-validity environments** | Flashbulb memory studies; Tetlock (2005). Confidence is not a signal of skill outside regular domains |
| **Substantial heritable individual differences in cognitive ability exist** | Twin, adoption, sibling designs; adult h² ≈ 0.6-0.8. The Wilson effect: heritability rises with age (Haworth et al. 2010) |
| **Heritability does not imply immutability** | Height h² ≈ 0.8, +20cm Dutch in 150 years. PKU h² ≈ 1.0, diet-controlled. **A matter of logic, not of politics** |
| **Within-group heritability licenses NO inference about between-group causes** | Lewontin (1970). A logical point, symmetric in both directions |
| **Response to identical training varies enormously and heritably** | HERITAGE Family Study: identical 20-week programme, VO₂max gains from ~0% to >50%† |
| **The Flynn effect: measured IQ rose ~3 points/decade for most of the 20th century** | Flynn (1984, 1987). The strongest single evidence for large environmental malleability |
| **The Flynn effect has reversed in several countries, and the reversal is environmental** | Bratsberg & Rogeberg (2018), *PNAS*, Norwegian conscription data, **observed within families** — which rules out compositional explanations |
| **Schooling causally raises IQ** | Ritchie & Tucker-Drob (2018): ~1-5 points per additional year across three quasi-experimental designs.† The best counterexample to a strong no-transfer position |
| **Elite performance requires very large amounts of practice** | No disputed counterexamples in any studied domain. The ten-year rule (Simon & Chase 1973; Hayes 1989) |
| **"10,000 hours" is Gladwell's framing, not Ericsson's, and Ericsson rejected it** | *Outliers* (2008) vs *Peak* (2016). The 10,000 figure was a group mean at age 20 for Berlin violinists |
| **Systematic phonics instruction outperforms whole language** | National Reading Panel (2000); Rose Review (2006); Seidenberg (2017). Largest benefit for at-risk readers |
| **Scared Straight increases offending** | Petrosino et al., Cochrane. **Interventions can be worse than nothing** |
| **Self-esteem does not cause achievement** | Baumeister et al. (2003), *PSPI*. The causal arrow runs the other way |
| **The Mozart effect as popularly stated does not exist** | Chabris (1999) d ≈ 0.09; Nantais & Schellenberg (1999) — a Stephen King audiobook works equally well. The mechanism is arousal |
| **The learning pyramid percentages are fabricated** | No traceable source. NTL could not produce the research. Dale's 1946 Cone contains no numbers |
| **There are no left-brained or right-brained people** | Nielsen et al. (2013), 1,011 individuals. Lateralisation of specific functions is real; the personality typology is not |
| **Education effect sizes are systematically inflated** | Cheung & Slavin (2016); Lortie-Forgues & Inglis (2019); Kraft (2020). **This downgrades the whole applied literature including parts of this notebook** |

## TIER 2 — Contested but tractable

| Question | State of play |
|---|---|
| **How much of expert performance does practice explain?** | Macnamara et al. (2014): ~26% games, ~21% music, ~18% sports, ~4% education, <1% professions.† Hambrick et al. (2014): ~34% chess, ~30% music. Ericsson: the coding was invalid and range restriction attenuates everything. **Both sides are right about the other's error. Unresolved** |
| **Is "deliberate practice" operationalisable prospectively?** | The unfalsifiability charge (Macnamara) has real force. Ericsson's reply was never formalised into a codeable protocol. This is settleable and nobody has settled it |
| **Is the practice-attainment link partly genetically confounded?** | Mosing et al. (2014) on Swedish twins suggests substantially yes.† If robust, it is the most damaging single finding for a pure practice account |
| **How large is growth mindset's real effect?** | Sisk et al. (2018): r ≈ 0.10, d ≈ 0.08. Yeager et al. (2019), n ≈ 12,490: ~0.10 GPA points for lower achievers, contingent on school norms. EEF (2019): null. Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023): near zero in the best studies. **Small and real, or small and noise, depending on which subset you weight** |
| **Does grit add anything to conscientiousness?** | Credé et al. (2017): r ≈ 0.84 with conscientiousness, incremental validity small.† Duckworth partly conceded. The perseverance facet does the work |
| **Where is the block-to-interleave crossover?** | Interleaving helps once basic competence exists and hurts before. Nobody has mapped the boundary. **The most practically consequential unmapped parameter in the notebook** |
| **Do the lab-scale effects of retrieval practice survive at classroom scale over a year?** | McDaniel et al. (2011) suggests yes and smaller. The magnitude of attenuation is unknown |
| **Is the overjustification effect large or narrow?** | Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999) vs Cameron & Pierce (1994). Cerasoli et al. (2014) reconciles: intrinsic motivation predicts quality, incentives predict quantity |
| **Does the Scarr-Rowe interaction (heritability lower in poverty) generalise?** | Turkheimer et al. (2003) found it in the US; Tucker-Drob & Bates (2016) did not find it in Europe or Australia. Welfare-floor explanation is plausible and unproven |
| **What caused the Flynn effect and its reversal?** | Nutrition, disease load, family size, schooling, abstraction of modern work, lead. Dickens & Flynn's (2001) multiplier model is the most elegant reconciliation and remains a model |
| **Why does test-score fadeout coexist with persistent life outcomes?** | Head Start / Perry / Abecedarian. Cognitive gains fade; earnings, incarceration and health effects often persist. **The most interesting unresolved puzzle in applied education research** |
| **Human capital vs signalling in the returns to education** | Caplan (2018) says ~80% signalling — an advocate's upper bound. Within-twin and compulsory-schooling evidence supports real human capital. Unresolved and load-bearing for policy |
| **Does sleep-dependent consolidation have the effect sizes claimed?** | Direction supported; magnitudes contested (Cordi & Rasch). **The most-read popular source, Walker's *Why We Sleep*, contains documented errors** |
| **Is cognitive load theory's three-type decomposition measurable?** | Sweller withdrew germane load around 2010. The empirical *effects* are robust; the *theory* is harder to test than its use implies |
| **Does handwritten note-taking beat typing?** | Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014) said yes; **Morehead, Dunlosky & Rawson (2019) failed to replicate.** Treat as unsupported |
| **Can you manufacture feedback and thereby create real expertise in a low-validity domain?** | Forecasting tournaments and surgical simulation are the natural experiments. Promising, not decisive |
| **Can critical thinking be taught transferably?** | Willingham says largely no without domain knowledge; Abrami et al. (2008, 2015) find positive effects when instruction is explicit and embedded. **Both can be true: transfer must be designed in, not assumed** |

## TIER 3 — Speculative

| Claim | Why it stays here |
|---|---|
| **Interest follows competence rather than preceding it** | Widely observed clinically, coherent with SDT, almost no prospective experimental evidence |
| **Desirable difficulty has an optimal level that could be computed per learner** | Attractive, matches adaptive-algorithm intuitions, no validated formula. The "85% success rate" heuristic circulating from machine-learning work is not established for humans |
| **Deliberate practice is impossible in domains without established pedagogy** | Follows from the definition; almost no direct test. Consistent with the professions result |
| **Sleep timing relative to study meaningfully changes retention in real learning** | Lab evidence exists; applied evidence is thin and the popular claims outrun it |
| **Teaching others is an especially powerful learning method** | The "protégé effect." Plausible, mechanistically explained by retrieval and generation, poorly isolated in controlled designs |
| **The multiplier model reconciles high heritability with large secular gains** | Dickens & Flynn (2001). Elegant, formally coherent, not directly measured |
| **Journaling / decision logs improve calibration in learning** | Cross-reference risk/practices.md, where it is also TIER 3. Components sound, no controlled trial |
| **Deep-work-style attention protection is necessary for expertise** | Consistent with cognitive load and with Ericsson's session structure. Popular literature far outruns the evidence |

## TIER 4 — Malformed or currently unanswerable

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| **"How long does it take to become an expert?"** | Underspecified across acquisition, retention, transfer and standard. Both "10,000 hours" and "20 hours" answer an unasked question |
| **"What percentage of ability is genes vs environment?"** | Heritability is a population variance decomposition, not an individual partition. The question has no referent as posed |
| **"Is she a natural or did she work for it?"** | Presupposes a partition that gene-environment correlation makes incoherent. Genes act partly *through* practice propensity |
| **"What is my learning style?"** | The construct does not predict outcomes. The question is well-formed and the answer is not useful |
| **Genetic bases of between-group differences in cognitive ability** | The required experiment — comparison under equalised environments — does not exist and cannot currently be run. **TIER 4 rather than TIER 3 for that reason, not for a political one** |
| **"What is the single best way to teach?"** | Expertise reversal makes this incoherent. Optimal method is a function of current expertise, so there is no method-level answer |
| **"Would this student have succeeded with different teaching?"** | The individual counterfactual is unobservable. This is the same structure as the absent-feedback problem in expertise.md |

## The things people most commonly get wrong

**The highest-value table in the notebook.**

| Belief | Reality | Tier of the correction |
|---|---|---|
| Rereading and highlighting are good study methods | Both rated LOW by Dunlosky et al. (2013). They are the two most-used methods | 1 |
| Testing measures learning | Testing **causes** learning, more than the studying it replaces | 1 |
| If it feels like it's working, it's working | Subjective fluency is anti-correlated with durable learning over a wide range. Deslauriers et al. (2019) measured it directly | 1 |
| Practise one topic to mastery, then move on | Blocking flatters today and damages retention and discrimination | 1 |
| 10,000 hours makes you an expert | Gladwell's phrase, not Ericsson's, rejected by Ericsson. Chess masters at ~3,000 hours; non-masters past 23,000 | 1 |
| Talent is a myth | Not held by anyone with data. HERITAGE alone shows order-of-magnitude spread in response to identical training | 1 |
| It's mostly genes; effort is marginal | Flynn effect: ~30 points in a century. Within-family polygenic prediction is half the population figure. Schooling raises IQ | 1 |
| Heritability tells you your ceiling | Category error. It is a population variance statistic and says nothing about individuals | 1 |
| Chess/music/coding makes you generally smarter | Effect size falls to near zero with active controls | 1 |
| Brain training improves intelligence | Owen et al., n = 11,430: no transfer. Lumosity fined $2m | 1 |
| Learning styles matter | Tested with the correct design; failed. Believed by ~90% of teachers | 1 |
| Critical thinking is a portable skill | Largely domain-knowledge-dependent. Wason selection task is the standard demonstration | 1-2 |
| Facts don't matter, you can look them up | Long-term knowledge expands effective working memory. You cannot look up a chunk | 1 |
| Feedback is always helpful | Over a third of feedback interventions reduce performance | 1 |
| Growth mindset transforms outcomes | d ≈ 0.08 overall; ~0.10 GPA points for lower achievers in the best trial. Real, small, oversold | 1 on the overselling, 2 on the residual |
| Grit is a distinct predictor of success | r ≈ 0.84 with conscientiousness. Little incremental validity | 2 |
| Experts think harder and consider more options | De Groot: they consider **fewer**. The expertise is in candidate generation | 1 |
| Experience makes you an expert | Only with a regular environment and feedback. Physician performance often declines with years since training | 1 |
| Trust the expert's intuition | Only under Kahneman and Klein's two conditions. Otherwise trust the checklist | 1 |
| Learning by doing is the best way to learn | For novices, unguided doing is among the worst uses of time. This reverses with expertise | 1 |
| Education research effect sizes mean what they do in medicine | They are inflated. 0.20 SD is a large education effect (Kraft 2020) | 1 |
| Hattie's *Visible Learning* settles what works | The common-language effect size transformation is mathematically incoherent (Bergeron & Rivard 2017) | 2 |

## Strongest convergences

Where independent lines of evidence, using different methods, reach the same
place. These deserve the most weight.

| Convergence | Lines that meet |
|---|---|
| **Difficulty during learning predicts durable learning** | Testing effect; spacing; interleaving; generation effect; pretesting; variability of practice in motor learning. Six independent paradigms, one direction |
| **Expertise is domain-bound** | Chase & Simon on chess; Chi on physics; Thorndike on formal discipline; brain-training nulls; the Nobel disease; encoding specificity. Cognitive, educational, historical and sociological evidence all agree |
| **Intuition needs regularity plus feedback** | Kahneman & Klein (adversarial collaboration); Shanteau (1992, independently); Meehl and Grove on clinical prediction; Tetlock on forecasting. **Four independent starting points, one criterion** |
| **Subjective judgement of learning is unreliable and often inverted** | Metacognition lab studies; student surveys; teaching evaluations; the Deslauriers PNAS field experiment |
| **Heritability is real AND does not constrain intervention** | Twin studies say the first; the Flynn effect, PKU, height and schooling effects say the second. **They are not in tension and are constantly presented as though they were** |
| **Popular educational interventions fail with active controls** | Learning styles, brain training, chess, music, Brain Gym, Mozart. Same signature every time |

## Where to spend attention

Ranked by expected value of an hour spent.

| Rank | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Switch from rereading to retrieval practice** | Largest documented gain per unit effort available. Costs nothing but discomfort |
| 2 | **Space your reviews** | Second-largest, also free. Gap ≈ 10-20% of desired retention interval |
| 3 | **Stop trusting the feeling of fluency** | Prerequisite for 1 and 2 to survive contact with your preferences |
| 4 | **Manufacture feedback where the domain gives none** | Converts a no-expertise domain into a possible-expertise domain. See practices.md |
| 5 | **Learn the domain deeply instead of a general skill** | Transfer is rare. If you want the capability, train the capability |
| 6 | **Interleave once you have the basics** | Large effects, large discomfort, and the crossover point is unmapped |
| 7 | **Check whether your domain permits valid intuition** | Kahneman and Klein's two conditions. Five minutes, and it determines whether to trust yourself at all |
| 8 | **Ignore everything in the-control-group.md** | Pure savings. No upside forgone |
