# Reading — Sources, With Reliability Marked

> **Status: this is a bibliography written from memory.** Titles, years and
> author lists have not been verified against any catalogue. Several are
> probably slightly wrong. Treat this as a search list, not a citation list.

> **Reliability key:**
> **[A]** Primary literature or a rigorous review. Read it directly.
> **[B]** Good popular or synthetic treatment by a competent author. Trustworthy
> in direction, occasionally loose in detail.
> **[C]** Influential and substantially wrong or oversold. Read to know what
> people believe, not to learn what is true.
> **[D]** Actively misleading. Listed because you will encounter it.

## Start here — if you read only three things

| Source | Why |
|---|---|
| **[A] Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques,"** *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1) | The single most useful document in the field. Ten techniques, systematically rated. Free to read, roughly 50 pages, and it will change what you do |
| **[A] Kahneman & Klein (2009), "Conditions for Intuitive Expertise: A Failure to Disagree,"** *American Psychologist* 64(6) | Twenty pages. The cleanest result in the notebook, produced by adversarial collaboration. A model of how disagreement should be conducted |
| **[A] Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork (2008), "Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence,"** *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 9(3) | Short, decisive, and an object lesson in what a correct experimental design looks like and why almost nobody used one |

## Talent, genetics and limits

**Read this section with more care than the others. The area has a documented
history of premature and harmful conclusions, and popular sources on both sides
are unreliable.**

| Source | Grade | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Turkheimer (2000), "Three Laws of Behavior Genetics and What They Mean" | **[A]** | Three pages. The best entry point, by an author who is simultaneously the field's summariser and a critic of its interpretation |
| Lewontin (1970), "Race and Intelligence" | **[A]** | The two-pots argument. Short, and settles the within/between-group inference question logically |
| Haworth et al. (2010), heritability increasing with age | **[A]** | The Wilson effect |
| Turkheimer, Haley, Waldron, D'Onofrio & Gottesman (2003) | **[A]** | Heritability by socioeconomic status. Read Tucker-Drob & Bates (2016) for the mixed replication |
| Kong et al. (2018), *Science*, genetic nurture | **[A]** | Non-transmitted parental alleles predict offspring outcomes. Deeply clarifying about what polygenic scores measure |
| Okbay et al. (2022), EA4 | **[A]** | And note the within-family attenuation |
| Flynn, *What Is Intelligence?* (2007) | **[B]** | Flynn's own account of his effect. Thoughtful and non-triumphalist |
| Bratsberg & Rogeberg (2018), *PNAS* | **[A]** | The within-family Flynn reversal. Methodologically the strongest thing in this section |
| Ritchie & Tucker-Drob (2018), "How Much Does Education Improve Intelligence?" | **[A]** | 1-5 IQ points per year of schooling. The best evidence for large environmental effects |
| Bouchard et al., HERITAGE Family Study papers | **[A]** | Differential trainability. Underused in the learning literature |
| Plomin, *Blueprint* (2018) | **[C]** | Well-informed and overreaching in its policy conclusions. Read with Turkheimer's critique |
| Any popular book confidently settling group differences, in either direction | **[D]** | The required experiment does not exist. Confident popular treatments are advocacy |

## What I would read first, in order

1. Dunlosky et al. (2013) — changes what you do this week.
2. Kahneman & Klein (2009) — changes what you trust.
3. Pashler et al. (2008) — inoculates you against a whole genre.
4. Kraft (2020) — recalibrates every number you will read afterwards.
5. Willingham, *Why Don't Students Like School?* — the synthesis.
6. Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993) and Macnamara et al. (2014) together,
   in that order, in one sitting. The dispute is more instructive than either
   paper alone.
