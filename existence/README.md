WHAT THIS IS
===============================================================================
An honest attempt at the questions that don't go away:

  What am I? What is life? What happens when I die? Why is there anything?
  What is consciousness? Am I free? Why do I live among others?
  Does any of it mean anything?

Written the same way as the leaders notebook: positions presented fairly,
disputes flagged as disputes, confidence marked, and a standing refusal to
pretend that something is settled when it isn't.

**No live sources were used.** This is compiled from general knowledge. Treat
it as a structured map of the territory, not as scholarship. See epistemics.md.

===============================================================================
THE ONE IDEA THAT ORGANISES EVERYTHING
===============================================================================
Almost every unanswerable question here is **several questions wearing one
costume.** The bulk of the confusion in this whole domain comes from asking one
and accepting an answer to another.

"What is life?" is at least four questions:
  (a) What distinguishes living matter from non-living? -> BIOLOGY, answerable
  (b) Why does anything exist at all?                   -> METAPHYSICS, open
  (c) What makes a life worth living?                   -> ETHICS, arguable
  (d) What am I supposed to do with mine?               -> PERSONAL, yours only

"Who am I?" is at least three:
  (a) What makes me the same person over time?          -> METAPHYSICS, tractable
  (b) What is it like to be me / why is there a "me"?   -> HARD PROBLEM, open
  (c) Which of my roles, values, stories is the real me?-> PSYCHOLOGY + choice

A biologist answering (a) has told you nothing about (d). A physicist has
nothing to say about (c). A therapist cannot resolve the hard problem.
**Sort the question first. Most of the anguish is a category error.**

HEADLINE POSITIONS (compressed, and each one contestable)
===============================================================================
 1. **"What is life" is scientifically embarrassing.** There is no agreed
    definition. Viruses break every one. This is not a gap in knowledge — it
    is a sign the category may be a human convenience, not a natural kind.

 2. **Consciousness is the one genuinely open scientific problem.** We can
    correlate brain states with experience in enormous detail and still cannot
    say why there is experience at all. This is not "not yet." Nobody has a
    accepted account of what an answer would even look like.

 3. **There is probably no persisting self.** The strongest positions in both
    Western philosophy (Hume, Parfit) and Buddhist analysis converge: what you
    call "I" is a process, not a thing. Neuroscience has found no place where
    the self lives. This is one of the few genuine convergences in the file.

 4. **Free will as usually imagined almost certainly doesn't exist.** Free will
    as compatibilists define it plausibly does. The disagreement is largely
    about which one deserves the name — but the practical consequences for
    responsibility are real and are argued in free-will.md.

 5. **Death is annihilation, on the best available evidence.** Every known
    mental function depends on brain tissue and degrades with damage to it.
    No credible mechanism for survival has been demonstrated. Say this plainly
    and then take the question of how to live with it seriously.

 6. **Meaning is not discovered; it is not arbitrary either.** The strongest
    position is hybrid: meaning arises where subjective engagement meets
    something objectively worth engaging with. Pure "choose your own meaning"
    is too weak. Cosmic purpose is unevidenced.

 7. **Society is not optional and not merely instrumental.** Humans are an
    obligately social species. Isolation damages us physiologically. The self
    itself is substantially constructed in relation to others.

 8. **The absurd is real and is not a tragedy.** The gap between our demand for
    meaning and the universe's silence is genuine. Camus's response — live
    fully inside the gap without resolving it — survives scrutiny better than
    most alternatives.

 9. **The traditions disagree, and papering over it is dishonest.** Buddhism
    denies a permanent self; Vedanta affirms an eternal one. Both cannot be
    right. "All religions say the same thing" is false and lazy.

10. **Nobody knows why there is something rather than nothing.** This is the
    genuine bedrock question and there is no accepted answer, including from
    physics, including from theology. Anyone who tells you otherwise is
    selling something.

THE HONEST PART
===============================================================================
- These questions have been worked on by extraordinarily intelligent people for
  roughly 2,500 years without resolution. **Anyone offering you certainty is
  either confused or lying.** That includes confident atheism and confident
  religion about matters neither can access.
- **Feeling certain is not evidence.** Mystical, psychedelic and near-death
  experiences are real experiences and are not self-validating as reports about
  reality. Their intensity is a fact about brains, not about metaphysics.
- **Most existential distress is not solved by philosophy.** If the question
  "why am I here" arrives with dread, exhaustion or hopelessness attached, the
  useful intervention is usually sleep, connection, exercise, sunlight or a
  professional — not an argument. Philosophy is for the question when it
  arrives with curiosity. Both are legitimate; they are different problems.
  See practices.md.
- **This notebook will not tell you what to do with your life.** No file can.
  What it can do is clear away the badly-formed versions of the question so
  the answerable parts become visible.
