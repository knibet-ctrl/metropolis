## The claim

**Attention is the substrate of experienced life.**

Not a metaphor. Your life, as you experience it, consists of what you attended
to. Events you did not attend to did not happen to you in any experiential
sense. **William James: "My experience is what I agree to attend to."**

This has an uncomfortable consequence. If memory is what constitutes your sense
of having lived (personal-identity.md), and memory encodes only what was
attended to, then **what you paid attention to is what your life was.** Not what
happened around you. Not what you intended. What you attended to.

**This makes attention allocation the highest-order practical question in the
notebook**, and it connects to nearly every other file.

## What attention actually is (Tier 1–2)

- **It is a selection mechanism with hard limits.** Vastly more information reaches your senses than reaches awareness. Attention is the filter, and the filter is narrow.
- **Inattentional blindness is real and dramatic.** The gorilla-in-the-basketball-game study is the famous demonstration; the effect replicates well. **You do not see what you are not attending to, even when it is directly in front of you and obvious in retrospect.**
- **Change blindness.** Large changes to a scene go unnoticed across brief interruptions. Your sense of a rich, complete, continuously updated visual world is substantially constructed.
- **Multitasking is switching, and switching is expensive.** For demanding tasks, apparent multitasking is rapid alternation with a measurable switch cost in time and error rate. *(The popular claims about exact percentage costs are inflated; the direction is solid.)*
- **Attention residue.** After switching tasks, part of your attention remains on the previous one for a period. This is why fragmented days feel exhausting despite low output.

**The convergence with consciousness.md is direct:** Global Workspace Theory
holds that consciousness *is* what happens when information is broadcast
globally. **Attention is the gate.** On that theory, controlling attention is
controlling the content of consciousness — which is as close as this notebook
gets to a lever on experience itself.

## The industrial problem

This section is contemporary and the evidence is genuinely mixed, so it's worth
being careful.

**The structural fact is not contested.** A large share of the digital
environment is funded by advertising. Advertising revenue is a function of
engagement. Engagement is optimised by systems with far more data about your
responses than you have. **The economic incentive is to capture attention, not
to serve you** — this is a description of the business model, not a conspiracy
claim.

**Herbert Simon identified the underlying dynamic in 1971:** a wealth of
information creates a poverty of attention. When information becomes abundant,
attention becomes the scarce resource, and whatever is scarce gets extracted.

**What is well supported:**
- Variable-ratio reward schedules (unpredictable payoffs) produce persistent, hard-to-extinguish behaviour. This is basic operant conditioning and it describes pull-to-refresh feeds accurately.
- Mere presence of a phone appears to reduce available cognitive capacity in some studies. *(Effect sizes contested.)*
- Self-reported difficulty with sustained reading and deep focus has risen. *(Self-report; confounded.)*

**What is genuinely contested and frequently overstated:**
- **Causal claims about social media and adolescent mental health.** The correlational data is real; the causal question is actively disputed among researchers, with serious people on both sides. **Do not treat this as settled in either direction.**
- **"Attention spans are shrinking."** The commonly cited figures are not well supported and the "goldfish" comparison is fabricated.
- **"Screens rewire your brain."** All experience changes the brain. The claim needs specificity to mean anything.

**The honest framing:** the incentive structure is real and adversarial by
design. The magnitude of the harm is contested. **You can act on the first
without settling the second**, because the asymmetry — professionalised systems
optimising against unaided individual willpower — is sufficient reason for
structural rather than willpower-based responses.

## Attention in the traditions

Every contemplative tradition independently identified attention as the point
of leverage. **This is another of the notebook's cross-tradition convergences,
and it is under-noticed.**

**Buddhism.** *Sati* — usually translated "mindfulness," closer to
"recollection" or "keeping in mind." The entire practice architecture is
attention training: *samatha* (stabilising attention) and *vipassanā* (using
stabilised attention to investigate experience). **The claim is that attention
trained past a certain threshold reveals things about the mind that are
otherwise invisible** — including the absence of a self (personal-identity.md).

**Stoicism.** *Prosochē* — continuous self-attention. Watching your own
judgements as they form, which is the only place the dichotomy of control can
be applied.

**Christian contemplative practice.** *Nepsis* (watchfulness) in the Eastern
tradition; the examen in the Ignatian. Simone Weil went further: *"Attention is
the rarest and purest form of generosity,"* and she treated attention as
essentially identical to love.

**Weil's claim deserves emphasis** because it connects this file to society.md:
**giving someone your full attention may be the most substantive thing you can
give them**, and it is exactly what a fragmented attentional environment makes
impossible.

## What follows practically

**The core principle: structure beats willpower.** You are one unaided person
with a depleting resource facing systems designed by teams with your behavioural
data. **Treating this as a discipline problem is a category error**, and it
reliably produces self-blame instead of change.

**Structural interventions, roughly in order of evidence and effect:**

1. **Change the environment, not the intention.** Remove the app rather than resolving to use it less. Friction works; resolve doesn't.
2. **Protect a contiguous block.** Deep work requires uninterrupted duration because of attention residue and switching costs. Four fragmented hours are not equivalent to two whole ones.
3. **Single-task deliberately.** The cost of switching is real and is invisible while you're paying it.
4. **Notice what your attention is on** without immediately changing it. This is the shared first move of every contemplative tradition and it's the prerequisite for the rest.
5. **Treat undivided attention to another person as a substantive act**, because it is. Phone out of the room, not face-down.
6. **Audit where the hours went, not where they were supposed to go.** Most people have never done this once, and the result is usually shocking.

**The measurement that matters:** at the end of a week, what do you remember? A
week with no retrievable content was a week that, experientially, did not
happen. That's the time.md finding and it's the sharpest available feedback
signal.

## Where the popular story is wrong

- **"Attention spans have shrunk to 8 seconds."** Fabricated statistic, no credible source, and the goldfish comparison is invented.
- **"Multitasking makes you more productive."** For demanding tasks, no. For genuinely automatic tasks, fine.
- **"Social media causes depression" / "has no effect."** Both stated with more confidence than the evidence supports. Genuinely contested.
- **"Dopamine hits / dopamine detox."** Popular neuroscience misuse. Dopamine's role concerns prediction error and motivation, not pleasure in the way the popular story implies. "Dopamine detox" has no scientific basis.
- **"You just need more willpower."** The asymmetry between individual willpower and industrial optimisation makes this near-useless advice, and it converts a structural problem into a moral failing.
- **"Mindfulness is a neutral productivity tool."** It's a component extracted from a soteriological system, stripped of its ethics and its goal. It works for what it works for; the framing is a marketing artefact.
