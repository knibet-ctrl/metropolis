# The Questions — Disaggregated

> **Start here.** Most of these questions are unanswerable *as asked* and
> tractable once split. This file does the splitting.

The diagnostic that runs through everything below:

**Which discipline could, in principle, settle this?** If the answer is "none,"
you are not looking at a hard question — you are looking at a malformed one, or
at a genuine mystery, and it matters enormously which.

## "What is life?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Status |
|---|---|---|
| What distinguishes living from non-living matter? | Biology, chemistry | **Contested but tractable.** No agreed definition; see what-is-life.md |
| How did life begin? | Origin-of-life chemistry | **Open, actively researched.** Several plausible partial routes |
| Why does anything exist at all? | Metaphysics, physics | **Unanswered. Possibly unanswerable** |
| What makes a life go well? | Ethics, psychology | **Arguable.** Real positions, real evidence, no proof |
| What should *I* do with mine? | You | **Not a knowledge question.** No expert can answer it |

**The failure mode:** hearing a biologist's answer to row 1 and feeling that row
5 has been dismissed. It hasn't been addressed.

## "Who am I?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Status |
|---|---|---|
| What makes me the same person over time? | Metaphysics | **Tractable.** Several coherent answers; probably no fact of the matter |
| Why is there something it's like to be me? | Philosophy of mind | **The hard problem. Genuinely open** |
| What is my personality, history, disposition? | Psychology | **Empirically answerable** |
| Which of my values is the "real" me? | Partly discovery, partly decision | **Not purely factual** |
| Is there a soul? | Theology / metaphysics | **Unevidenced. Not disproven either** |

**The failure mode:** treating row 4 as though it has a hidden true answer
waiting to be uncovered. There is substantial evidence that people *construct*
the answer and then experience it as discovery.

## "What happens when I die?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Status |
|---|---|---|
| What physically happens? | Medicine | **Well established** |
| Does experience continue? | Neuroscience + metaphysics | **Best evidence: no.** Not strictly provable |
| Is being dead bad for me? | Philosophy | **Genuinely argued.** Epicurus vs the deprivation account |
| How should I live given it? | You | **The only one that changes anything** |

## "Why is there something rather than nothing?"

The bedrock question, and it deserves its own treatment because **every
proposed answer fails in the same way.**

- **"God created it."** Then why is there God? Either God is a brute fact — in which case you have not explained existence, you have relocated it — or God is self-explaining, and it is unclear what that means.
- **"The universe is a brute fact."** Same structure without the extra entity. Simpler, equally unexplanatory.
- **"Quantum fluctuation from nothing."** The physics here (Krauss and others) describes fluctuation in a quantum vacuum with laws and fields. **That is not nothing.** This is the most common bait-and-switch in popular science writing.
- **"Nothing is unstable / impossible."** Interesting, and it moves the question to why *that* is so.
- **"The question is malformed."** Possibly the strongest position — that "why" presupposes a causal frame that may not apply to existence as a whole. But this is a diagnosis, not an answer.

**Honest status: no accepted answer exists.** Not from physics, not from
theology, not from philosophy. This is the clearest case in the notebook where
the correct response is to say so and stop.

## "Am I free?"

| Sub-question | Status |
|---|---|
| Is the physical world deterministic (or random)? | **Largely settled physics.** Either way, neither gives you libertarian free will |
| Could I have done otherwise, holding everything fixed? | **Almost certainly no** |
| Do my deliberations cause my actions? | **Yes — demonstrably** |
| Am I morally responsible? | **This is the actual dispute.** See free-will.md |

**The failure mode:** concluding from "no libertarian free will" that
deliberation is pointless. Your deliberating is part of the causal chain. A
person who reasons carefully produces different outcomes than one who doesn't,
determinism notwithstanding.

## "Does life have meaning?"

Three distinct claims get conflated constantly:

1. **Cosmic meaning** — the universe has a purpose and you have a role in it. **Unevidenced.**
2. **Objective value** — some things are genuinely worth doing regardless of whether anyone wants to. **Seriously argued both ways.**
3. **Subjective meaning** — you experience your life as significant. **Obviously real, and empirically studied.**

Someone who says "life is meaningless" almost always means (1) and is often
heard as denying (3). Someone who says "you create your own meaning" is
asserting (3) and quietly assuming (2).

## "Why do I need other people?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Are humans obligately social? | Biology, anthropology | **Established.** Isolation causes measurable physiological harm |
| Why did cooperation evolve? | Evolutionary biology | **Well developed:** kin selection, reciprocity, group-level effects |
| Why is society organised as it is? | History, economics, sociology | **Partly answerable** |
| Is the self socially constructed? | Philosophy, developmental psych | **Substantially yes, and contested in degree** |

## The four tiers — apply this to any claim in this notebook

**TIER 1 — Established.** Multiple independent lines of evidence; would require
extraordinary evidence to overturn. *Example: mental function depends on brain
tissue.*

**TIER 2 — Contested but tractable.** Serious people disagree; evidence and
argument are relevant and progress is possible. *Example: the definition of
life; compatibilism.*

**TIER 3 — Speculative.** Coherent, unfalsified, unevidenced. Reasonable people
hold these; nobody should be confident. *Example: panpsychism; simulation
hypothesis; multiverse.*

**TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed.** No procedure could settle
it. *Example: why there is something rather than nothing; whether another being
is conscious.*

**The single most useful habit in this entire domain: label the tier before you
argue.** Most existential arguments are two people at different tiers shouting
past each other — one demanding evidence for a Tier 4 question, the other
offering feeling as evidence for a Tier 1 one.

## What actually changes anything

Brutally: of everything above, only a small number of questions change how you
live tomorrow.

- Whether there is a persisting self → changes how you hold grievance, ambition and fear. **Practically large.**
- Whether death is annihilation → changes urgency. **Practically large.**
- Whether meaning is made or found → changes where you look. **Practically large.**
- Whether the universe has a purpose → **practically small**, oddly. People who believe opposite things here often live near-identically.
- Whether free will is libertarian or compatibilist → **practically small** for your choices, **large** for how you judge others.
- Why there is something rather than nothing → **practically zero.** The most profound question in the file is the least consequential.

**Spend your attention accordingly.** The questions that feel deepest are not
the ones that pay.
