# Reading — Primary Sources Worth The Time

> Prefer primaries where they're readable. Most of what's wrong in popular
> discussion comes from people arguing about books they've only read summaries
> of.

---

## Start here — genuinely readable, high return

| Book | Why |
|---|---|
| **Marcus Aurelius — *Meditations*** | A working emperor's private notebook, not written for publication. The most useful ancient text for a modern reader. Hays or Waterfield translations |
| **Epictetus — *Discourses* / *Enchiridion*** | Sharper and more systematic than Marcus. The dichotomy of control, stated by a man born enslaved |
| **Camus — *The Myth of Sisyphus*** | The absurd, and the best answer to "why not give up." Short |
| **Frankl — *Man's Search for Meaning*** | Testimony, not clinical evidence. Read as the former |
| **Nagel — *What Does It All Mean?*** | ~100 pages, covers the whole field, written for beginners by a first-rank philosopher. **The single best entry point** |

## Consciousness

- **Chalmers — *The Conscious Mind*** (1996). The hard problem stated properly. Hard going, worth it.
- **Nagel — "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"** (1974). A paper, freely available, and the clearest single statement of the problem.
- **Dennett — *Consciousness Explained*** (1991). The strongest opposing case. Critics call it "consciousness explained away," which he'd partly accept.
- **Dehaene — *Consciousness and the Brain*** (2014). Best account of what neuroscience has actually shown. Global workspace, empirically grounded.
- **Godfrey-Smith — *Other Minds*** (2016). Octopuses and the evolution of experience. Unusually good, and enjoyable.

---

## Self and identity

- **Parfit — *Reasons and Persons*** (1984). Part III on personal identity is the landmark. Difficult and genuinely important.
- **Hume — *A Treatise of Human Nature***, Book I, Part IV, Section VI. A few pages. The bundle theory, 1739.
- **Metzinger — *The Ego Tunnel*** (2009). Neuroscience-informed no-self, accessible.
- **Gazzaniga — *Who's in Charge?*** Split-brain work and the interpreter module, from the person who did it.

---

## Free will

- **Dennett — *Elbow Room*** / ***Freedom Evolves***. The best compatibilist case.
- **Pereboom — *Living Without Free Will***. The best hard-incompatibilist case.
- **Strawson (Galen) — "The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility."** The basic argument, in paper form.
- **Sapolsky — *Determined*** (2023). Aggressive, readable, hard-determinist. Philosophers dispute a lot of it; read alongside Dennett.
- **Caruso — *Rejecting Retributivism***. What actually follows for criminal justice.

---

## Life and biology

- **Schrödinger — *What Is Life?*** (1944). A physicist's 1944 lectures that anticipated the informational nature of the gene. Short and remarkable.
- **Nick Lane — *The Vital Question*** (2015). Energy, hydrothermal vents, and origin of life. The best modern popular treatment.
- **Maturana & Varela — *The Tree of Knowledge***. Autopoiesis, from the people who coined it.
- **Prigogine & Stengers — *Order Out of Chaos***. Dissipative structures. Uneven but the ideas matter.

---

## Death

- **Epicurus — *Letter to Menoeceus***. A few pages. The original argument.
- **Nagel — "Death"** (1970), in *Mortal Questions*. The deprivation reply.
- **Becker — *The Denial of Death*** (1973). Culture as mortality defence.
- **Gawande — *Being Mortal*** (2014). What dying is actually like now, and how medicine handles it badly. **Arguably the most practically useful book on this list.**
- **Kagan — *Death***. Yale lecture course in book form. Careful and clear.

---

## Meaning

- **Wolf — *Meaning in Life and Why It Matters*** (2010). Short. The hybrid view, and the strongest position in the file.
- **Camus — *The Myth of Sisyphus***.
- **de Beauvoir — *The Ethics of Ambiguity*** (1947). Better than Sartre on the same ground — freedom as situated rather than abstract.
- **Sartre — *Existentialism Is a Humanism***. A lecture, accessible. *Being and Nothingness* is not.
- **Tolstoy — *A Confession***. A man at the height of success finding it hollow. Painfully honest.

---

## Society

- **Rawls — *A Theory of Justice*** (1971). The veil of ignorance. Long; the first ~100 pages carry most of it.
- **Henrich — *The WEIRDest People in the World*** (2020). Why most psychology describes an unusual population.
- **Durkheim — *Suicide*** (1897). Anomie. Founding text of empirical sociology.
- **Honneth — *The Struggle for Recognition***. The recognition tradition, developed.
- **Putnam — *Bowling Alone***. Social capital and its decline. Contested, influential.

## Traditions — primaries where possible

**Buddhism**
- ***Dhammapada*** — short, aphoristic, the accessible entry.
- **Bhikkhu Bodhi — *In the Buddha's Words*** — the best organised anthology of the Pāli canon.
- **Nāgārjuna — *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*** — emptiness, rigorously. Very hard.
- *Avoid starting with Western secular repackagings. Read what the tradition says first, then decide what to keep.*

**Vedanta**
- ***Upanishads*** (Easwaran or Olivelle translation).
- ***Bhagavad Gita*** (Easwaran is readable; Zaehner is scholarly).
- **Shankara — *Vivekachudamani***.

**Daoism**
- ***Dao De Jing*** — translations vary enormously. Try more than one.
- ***Zhuangzi*** — funnier and more radical than the *Dao De Jing*. Watson or Ziporyn.

**Abrahamic**
- **Augustine — *Confessions***. The restless heart. Also the first real autobiography.
- **Maimonides — *Guide for the Perplexed***.
- **Al-Ghazali — *Deliverance from Error***. A crisis of doubt, from inside.
- **Kierkegaard — *Fear and Trembling***. Faith as something that cannot be justified by reason.
- **Boethius — *The Consolation of Philosophy***. Written awaiting execution.

## Correctives — read to stay honest

- **Kahneman — *Thinking, Fast and Slow***. How your reasoning actually works. *(Note: some cited studies have replication problems, which Kahneman acknowledged.)*
- **Ritchie — *Science Fictions***. The replication crisis. Necessary before trusting any psychology cited anywhere, including in this notebook.
- **Taleb — *Fooled by Randomness***. Survivorship bias.
- **Sagan — *The Demon-Haunted World***. Why extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence, written generously rather than contemptuously.

## On reading this material

**Read primaries, slowly, and fewer of them.** Ten pages of Epictetus attended
to properly is worth more than three popular books about Stoicism.

**Read the strongest opponent of whatever you find convincing.** If Dennett
persuades you, read Chalmers. If Chalmers does, read Dennett. This is the single
highest-return habit available here.

**Notice when a book is doing emotional work for you** and increase scrutiny
rather than decreasing it. See epistemics.md.

**Check the translation.** *Dukkha*, *wu wei*, *ātman*, *logos* — a great deal
of confident Western commentary rests on mistranslations.
