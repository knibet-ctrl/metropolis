PURPOSE
===============================================================================
Answer one question rigorously:

  "What did outlier leaders actually DO differently from an ordinary,
   equally-talented person living in the same era, with the same tools,
   under the same constraints?"

Not hagiography. Not 'they worked hard'. Everyone worked hard.
We are hunting for the *mechanisms* — the repeatable, describable moves.

HEADLINE FINDINGS (compressed)
===============================================================================
After going through ~60 figures across 2,500 years, the differentiators
cluster into 12 mechanisms. None of them are "charisma".

 1. ASYMMETRIC INFORMATION POSITION
    They systematically knew something true that the consensus did not.
    Usually earned by going somewhere others wouldn't go (the front line,
    the factory floor, the prison, the lab bench, the customer's house).

 2. TIME-HORIZON ARBITRAGE
    They played a 20-year game in a room full of people playing 1-year games.
    This is the single most repeated pattern in the whole dataset.

 3. COMPOUNDING A NARROW EDGE
    One unfair advantage, pressed relentlessly, rather than five modest ones.

 4. DECISION VELOCITY UNDER UNCERTAINTY
    They decided at ~60-70% information while peers waited for 95%.
    They paired this with cheap reversibility and fast error correction.

 5. NARRATIVE CONTROL
    They defined the frame, the vocabulary, and the enemy. Whoever names
    the situation usually wins the argument about it.

 6. SELECTION OVER MANAGEMENT
    Obsessive about *who*. They spent outrageous time on picking people and
    comparatively little on supervising them.

 7. INSTITUTION-BUILDING (the "machine that outlives me" move)
    Ordinary high-performers optimise output. Outliers build the mechanism
    that produces output without them.

 8. STRATEGIC PATIENCE + SUDDEN CONCENTRATION
    Long quiet accumulation, then all-in at the decisive moment.
    Not constant aggression — punctuated aggression.

 9. COSTLY SIGNALS OF COMMITMENT
    They did something irreversible and expensive that proved they meant it,
    which changed everyone else's calculations.

10. PROXIMITY TO THE REAL CONSTRAINT
    They found the actual bottleneck (logistics, legitimacy, cashflow,
    a single physical parameter) and attacked it instead of the symptom.

11. ASYMMETRIC MORAL LEVERAGE
    The non-violent tradition especially: absorb harm publicly, force the
    opponent to reveal their nature, convert bystanders into participants.

12. RECRUITING THE FUTURE
    Deliberately built successors and disciples. Influence outlived the body.

===============================================================================
THE UNCOMFORTABLE PART (do not skip)
===============================================================================
- SURVIVORSHIP BIAS is enormous here. For every outlier who bet big and won,
  a graveyard of identical people bet big and vanished. We only interview
  the winners. Treat every "strategy" below as *necessary but not sufficient*.
- LUCK / TIMING is doing heavy lifting. Being 26 when the printing press,
  the railway, the microprocessor, or the internet arrives is not a skill.
  The transferable part is: they were POSITIONED to exploit the shock.
- MANY OF THESE PEOPLE WERE MONSTROUS. Effectiveness is not virtue.
  Napoleon, Caesar, Mao, and Genghis Khan are studied here as case data,
  not as role models. See failure-modes.md.
- Biographies are written by winners and their friends. Discount accordingly.
