A structured, honest map of collapse studies — the field that tries to explain
why Rome, the Maya lowlands, Ugarit, Angkor, the USSR and a hundred others

THE ONE IDEA THAT ORGANISES EVERYTHING
===============================================================================
**"Collapse" is almost always a claim about elites, and almost never a claim
about people.**

What the archaeological and textual record actually registers is the cessation
of *elite signalling behaviour*: monument dedication stops, palace archives
stop, long-distance luxury trade stops, coin quality drops, tax records end.
Those are the things that fossilise. They are also the things a small ruling
stratum produces.

So when we say "the Maya collapsed," what we have evidence for is: **the
institution of divine kingship in the southern lowlands stopped commissioning
dated stone monuments between roughly 800 and 900 CE.** Six to seven million
Maya people are alive today. Both facts are true. Only one of them is what the
phrase "the Maya collapse" conveys to a general audience.

This is not a debunking. Sometimes the population really did fall, and fall
hard. But **the burden of proof for "everyone died" is completely different
from the burden of proof for "the palace stopped keeping records," and the
literature — especially the popular literature — routinely conflates them.**

Second organising idea, from `existence/`: the big question is several
questions wearing one costume. See the-questions.md.

HEADLINE FINDINGS (compressed, each contestable)
===============================================================================
 1. **"Collapse" measures elite institutions, not human welfare.** The signal
    we detect — monuments, archives, coinage, trade goods — is produced by a
    tiny stratum. Population decline is a *separate* claim needing separate
    evidence, and it is often absent. TIER 1 as a methodological point.

 2. **There is no general theory of collapse and probably cannot be one.**
    Tainter, Diamond, Turchin and Ibn Khaldun are not really competitors;
    they describe different mechanisms operating at different timescales.
    The field's search for a single master variable has failed. TIER 1.

 3. **Rome did not fall in 476.** Romulus Augustulus was deposed by Odoacer,
    who then sent the imperial regalia to Constantinople and governed Italy in
    the emperor's name. Contemporaries did not record it as an epoch. The date
    is a 6th-century-and-later convention. **The Eastern Empire ran another
    977 years.** TIER 1.

 4. **But the West's material collapse was real and severe.** Ward-Perkins is
    right against the softest versions of "transformation": wheel-thrown
    pottery, roofing tile, large-scale coinage and long-distance bulk trade
    genuinely disappear from Britain and much of the West for centuries.
    Standards of living for ordinary people fell. TIER 1–2.

 5. **The Easter Island ecocide story is probably wrong.** Deforestation
    happened; the claim it caused a pre-contact societal collapse does not
    survive the radiocarbon revision, the rat-predation evidence, the lithic
    mulching evidence, or the 2024 ancient-DNA finding of no pre-contact
    bottleneck. Contact-era slaving and disease did the population killing.
    TIER 2, moving toward Tier 1 against Diamond.

 6. **The Late Bronze Age collapse c. 1200–1150 BC is the real thing.** Multiple
    palace civilisations across the eastern Mediterranean failed within about
    fifty years, literacy was lost in Greece for centuries, and an integrated
    trade network dependent on long-distance tin came apart. Multi-causal,
    genuinely catastrophic, and the best analogue for interconnection failure.
    TIER 1 that it happened; TIER 2–3 on cause weighting.

 7. **Elite overproduction is the most promising single mechanism.** More
    credentialed claimants to elite positions than positions available →
    intra-elite competition → factionalism → institutional capture → conflict.
    Goldstone and Turchin formalised it; Ibn Khaldun had the core of it in
    1377. It is testable and partly tested. TIER 2, unusually strong for Tier 2.

 8. **Ibn Khaldun is the most under-credited theorist in the field.** The
    *Muqaddimah* (1377) contains a mechanistic, cyclical, non-teleological
    theory of cohesion decay with a fiscal sub-model, four centuries before
    Vico and Gibbon. Western collapse literature routinely omits him. TIER 1
    as a fact about the historiography.

 9. **Collapse is one of history's few reliable engines of equality.** Scheidel's
    uncomfortable finding: state collapse flattens wealth distributions as
    effectively as plague or revolution, because inequality requires a state
    to enforce property claims. This is not an argument for collapse. It is an
    argument against assuming collapse is uniformly bad. TIER 2.

10. **We study the societies that left monuments.** Perishable-material,
    non-literate, non-monumental and wet-climate societies are systematically
    under-sampled. LiDAR in Amazonia and Mesoamerica has already forced major
    upward revisions. **The sample is biased in a direction that inflates the
    apparent importance of monumental states.** TIER 1.

THE HONEST PART — what's wrong with this notebook
===============================================================================
**1. No source verification.** Every date, figure and attribution here is from
memory. I am confident about structure and argument, less confident about
numbers, least confident about attributions of specific claims to specific
scholars. Check anything load-bearing.

**2. Numbers in this field are terrible and I have flagged, but not fixed,
this.** Specific offenders:
  - Pre-contact American population: published estimates range from ~8 million
    to ~112 million. This is a factor of fourteen. It is the single most
    contested demographic number in history and it drives the entire debate
    about the Columbian exchange's death toll.
  - Roman Empire population (~50–80 million?), city of Rome at peak (~500,000
    to 1.2 million), Classic Maya lowland density — all are model outputs, not
    counts. They are derived from settlement survey, house-mound counts and
    assumed occupancy rates, each of which is arguable.
  - Late Bronze Age destruction dates rest on chronologies that are themselves
    disputed by decades.
  - Death tolls for the Yugoslav wars, the 1990s Russian mortality crisis and
    Venezuelan emigration are all live political questions with partisan
    estimates on both sides.

**3. Survivorship bias runs through everything.** See the-control-group.md.

**4. The field is politically loaded.** Collapse studies attracts people who
want a scientific-looking vehicle for a prior political conclusion — left
(capitalism/colonialism), right (decadence/migration), green (limits to
growth), and tech-accelerationist (we'll engineer our way out). I have tried to
mark where an argument's popularity exceeds its evidence, but I am not immune.

**5. Anglophone bias.** The cases I know best are Mediterranean, Mesoamerican
and modern-European. Coverage of the Indian subcontinent (Harappan decline),
Africa (Great Zimbabwe, Aksum, Jenne-jeno), Southeast Asia (Angkor) and the
Andes (Wari, Tiwanaku, Inca) is thin here and should be treated as a gap, not
as an absence of relevant cases.

**6. "Civilisation" is a compromised word.** It has an ugly history as a ranking
device. I use it because it is the topic's name, and I try in
what-is-a-civilisation.md to say what work it is and isn't doing.
