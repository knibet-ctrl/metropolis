# What We Actually Know — The Confidence Ledger

> **The key file.** Everything in this notebook, sorted by how much weight it
> can bear. Read this before arguing with anyone, including yourself.

**Written from general knowledge without live source verification.** The tier
assignments are my judgement of the state of the field. The specific numbers
are the least reliable content and are flagged individually.

**The tiers:**

- **TIER 1 — Established.** Multiple independent lines of evidence. Overturning it would require extraordinary evidence.
- **TIER 2 — Contested but tractable.** Serious people disagree; evidence and argument are relevant; progress is possible.
- **TIER 3 — Speculative.** Coherent, unfalsified, unevidenced. Hold loosely.
- **TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed.** No procedure could settle it.

## TIER 1 — Established

| Claim | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Complex societies have repeatedly lost complexity, sometimes rapidly** | The core phenomenon is real. Destruction layers, abandonment, monument cessation, material simplification |
| **476 CE is a historiographical convention, not an event** | No contemporary treats it as epochal. Odoacer governed in the eastern emperor's name; the Senate continued |
| **The Roman Empire continued in the east until 1453** | Its inhabitants called themselves *Rhomaioi*. "Byzantine" was coined in 1557 |
| **The Maya did not disappear** | 6–7 million living Maya, ~30 languages. Independent Maya states survived to 1697 |
| **Material living standards fell sharply in the post-Roman West** | Pottery quality, roofing tile, coinage, building technique, cattle size, casual literacy, Greenland ice-core lead — multiple independent proxies, same direction |
| **A multi-polity collapse occurred in the eastern Mediterranean c. 1200–1150 BC** | Destruction layers at many independent sites; Hattusa, Ugarit, the Mycenaean palaces; Linear B literacy lost for ~400 years |
| **Justinianic plague was *Yersinia pestis*** | Confirmed by ancient DNA from burials. Pathogen identity is settled; demographic scale is not |
| **Roman currency was massively debased in the third century** | Directly measurable in surviving coins |
| **Deforestation occurred on Rapa Nui** | Not in dispute. The cause and the consequence are |
| **Rapa Nui's documented population crash followed European contact** | Disease, the 1862–63 Peruvian slave raids, subsequent epidemics; a recorded low of 111 people in 1877 |
| **Sedentism preceded agriculture, and agriculture preceded states by millennia** | Natufian sedentism, Göbekli Tepe, and a ~4,000-year gap between farming and states |
| **Writing was invented for accounting** | Earliest proto-cuneiform and Linear B corpora are overwhelmingly administrative |
| **The surviving sample of past societies is filtered toward monumental, literate, dry-climate, stone-building cases** | Survivorship bias is demonstrated, not merely suspected — LiDAR revisions in three regions |
| **Elite institutional failure and population decline are separate phenomena requiring separate evidence** | Methodological, and routinely violated in popular writing |
| **No modern state collapse has produced immediate mass mortality** | USSR, Yugoslavia (war deaths, not collapse deaths), Venezuela. Delayed excess mortality is a different matter |
| **The Sovietology profession failed to predict 1991** | With vastly better data than any ancient historian has |
| **Ibn Khaldun (1377) produced a mechanistic cyclical theory four centuries before European equivalents** | A fact about the historiography, and Western surveys routinely omit him |

## TIER 2 — Contested but tractable

| Question | State of play |
|---|---|
| **Why did the Western Roman Empire fall?** | Multi-causal, weighting disputed. Heather (exogenous Hunnic-driven pressure), Ward-Perkins (material catastrophe), Wickham (fiscal-to-land structural shift), Harper (climate and disease) are the live positions. **Fiscal-military feedback after the loss of Africa in 439 is the most legible single chain** |
| **Was post-Roman change catastrophe or transformation?** | Both, regionally. Catastrophic in Britain and northern Gaul; continuity or growth in Egypt and Syria to the 7th century. The dispute is substantially about which metric counts |
| **How much did climate cause the Maya Terminal Classic?** | Drought is the best-supported single factor and is not sufficient. Warfare, density and an ideology hostage to rainfall all contribute |
| **Did Rapa Nui experience a pre-contact societal collapse?** | **The revisionist case (Hunt and Lipo, plus 2024 ancient-DNA evidence of no pre-contact bottleneck) is now stronger than Diamond's.** Not yet Tier 1 |
| **What caused the Late Bronze Age collapse?** | Occurrence is Tier 1. Weighting among drought, seismicity, migration, internal revolt and network failure is Tier 2 |
| **Is elite overproduction a real driver of instability?** | The strongest single mechanism candidate. Countable in principle; the historical series are reconstructions |
| **Do secular cycles of ~200–300 years exist?** | Turchin's strongest empirical claim. Overfitting and case non-independence are unresolved objections |
| **How large was the Justinianic plague's demographic impact?** | The minimalist revision is a serious challenge to the traditional catastrophic reading. **Currently the sharpest live dispute in ancient demography** |
| **How large was the Antonine plague?** | Estimates from 2% to 25% of the empire. That range makes downstream arguments untestable |
| **Was collapse better for ordinary people?** | Post-Black-Death real wages: strong. Post-Roman skeletal improvement: contested. Scheidel's levelling thesis: serious and unwelcome |
| **Did early farmers have worse health than foragers?** | Stature, dental and dietary-diversity evidence points that way. The osteological paradox is a real qualification |
| **Was the Norse Greenland collapse ecological or economic?** | The ivory-market explanation is a live competitor to Diamond's ecocide reading, and it matters because Greenland is his best case |
| **Did the Chinese examination system absorb elite overproduction?** | Partly, and it also deferred it — the Ming-Qing degree-holder glut and the Taiping catastrophe suggest deferral more than absorption |
| **Does interconnection net out as fragility or resilience?** | Redundancy appears to be the deciding variable. Nobody has located the threshold |
| **How much did US sanctions deepen Venezuela's collapse?** | Empirically answerable; nobody is answering it neutrally |
| **How much of pre-Columbian American depopulation was pathogens versus violence and disruption?** | The "virgin soil" framing is under sustained challenge |

## TIER 3 — Speculative

Hold loosely. Reasonable people hold these; nobody should be confident.

| Claim | Note |
|---|---|
| **Declining marginal returns on complexity as a general law** | Tainter's mechanism is illuminating; it has never been measured for any ancient society. It may be a metaphor with unusually good explanatory reach |
| **Early-warning signals (critical slowing down) in societies** | Works in ecology and lake systems. Applying it to societies requires time series we do not possess for the ancient cases |
| **Zomia as deliberate state avoidance** | The *possibility* is established; that it explains the highland cultures in detail is not. Lieberman's chronological objections are serious |
| **Strategic illiteracy** | Scott's most provocative claim. Interesting, thin |
| **The 4.2 ka BP event as a global climate forcing** | The regional signals are real; the global synchronous "event" framing is contested |
| **Asabiyyah as a measurable variable** | Currently diagnosed from the outcome it explains |
| **"Empires last ~250 years"** | Glubb's essay. No method, ad hoc definitions, sample fitted to conclusion. **Should arguably be Tier 4 as malformed rather than Tier 3** |
| **Spengler's organic life-cycle of cultures** | Historically influential, methodologically abandoned |
| **Toynbee's challenge-and-response** | Unfalsifiable as stated. His "dominant minority" prefigures elite overproduction and deserves that much credit |
| **Modern civilisation is uniquely fragile / uniquely robust** | Both are asserted confidently. Neither has a base rate. See applications.md |
| **Graeber and Wengrow's seasonal political dual-morphology as widespread** | Provocative, argued substantially from absence of evidence |
| **The Indus script encodes language** | Genuinely open; some researchers argue it may not |

## TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed

| Question | Why it can't be settled |
|---|---|
| **"What counts as a civilisation?"** | No fact settles it. Family-resemblance term. Pick a definition and the downstream questions become tractable |
| **"When exactly did Rome fall?"** | Depends entirely on the chosen unit — state, economy, church, language, empire — each with a different answer spanning a millennium |
| **"Was the collapse inevitable?"** | Counterfactual history has no settling procedure. You cannot rerun the experiment |
| **"Are we next?"** | No defined referent for "we," no specified sense of "collapse," and no usable base rate from ~30 non-independent, badly measured cases |
| **"Which civilisation was greatest?"** | A value judgment wearing a research question's clothes |
| **"Could they have prevented it?"** | Requires knowing what they knew, which for most cases we cannot recover |
| **"Is civilisation good?"** | Depends on the welfare metric — median individual, total population, capability, knowledge — and the choice among these is not empirical |

## The things people most commonly get wrong

Sorted roughly by how often the error appears.

| Belief | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Rome fell in 476" | A retrospective convention. The eastern empire ran to 1453 |
| "The Maya vanished" | 6–7 million living Maya today. **The most consequential error in the topic** |
| "Easter Islanders destroyed themselves" | Contested and probably wrong. Rats, disease and slave raiding are strong alternatives |
| "Collapse means everyone dies" | Usually it means elite institutions stopped. Population effects are a separate claim |
| "Christianity caused Rome's fall" | Gibbon. The Christian half survived a further millennium |
| "Barbarians destroyed Rome" | Most wanted Roman land, titles and salaries. Odoacer and Theodoric ruled through Roman institutions |
| "Empires last about 250 years" | Glubb's essay, endlessly recirculated. Rome, China, Venice, Byzantium and the Ottomans all falsify it |
| "The Maya predicted the world would end in 2012" | A calendar cycle rolling over. Maya inscriptions reference dates far beyond it |
| "Moral decay causes collapse" | Every society's moralists say this, always. It is evidence about moralists |
| "Lead pipes poisoned the Romans" | Hard water calcified the pipes; Vitruvius warned about lead. Not a civilisational cause |
| "Reagan collapsed the Soviet Union" | Specialists weight internal stagnation, the 1986 oil shock, reform dynamics and elite defection far higher |
| "Yugoslavia collapsed from ancient hatreds" | Rejected by specialists. Elite manufacture of conflict is far better supported |
| "The Dark Ages were dark" | Uneven, and partly an artefact of losing the records we detect activity with. Lefkandi is the standing counterexample |
| "Iron replaced bronze and caused the Bronze Age collapse" | Iron adoption followed the collapse of the tin trade. Consequence, not cause |
| "Diamond proved societies destroy their environments" | Five-factor framework including response; his headline case is probably wrong; his best case (Greenland) has a live economic competitor |
| "Agriculture was straightforwardly progress" | Individual health indicators worsened; population and total capability rose. Both are true and they answer different questions |
| "Historians know why Rome fell" | Over 200 published hypotheses. The number is the finding |
| "Complexity is bad" | Byzantium simplified deliberately and lasted 800 more years; China re-complexified repeatedly and it worked |
| "We have a representative sample of past societies" | Seven filters, all pointing the same way |

## Where the strongest convergences are

Independent methods reaching the same conclusion is the best signal available
in a field this soft. There are five.

**1. Collapse is multi-causal.** Textual history (Rome), archaeology (Maya),
palaeoclimatology (Bronze Age), and the fully documented modern cases (USSR)
independently produce the same shape: a conjunction of stresses hitting a system
with pre-existing vulnerability. **No well-documented case is monocausal.
Four different evidence types, same result.**

**2. Elite institutions fail before, and more visibly than, populations.**
Monument records, tax records, coin quality, palace archives and modern
statistics all show the same asymmetry.

**3. Legitimacy and cohesion are load-bearing.** Ibn Khaldun (1377, from
dynastic observation), Weber (sociology), the Maya drought-and-divine-kingship
case (archaeology), and Yurchak on the late USSR (ethnography) converge on the
same variable from four unrelated directions and four different centuries.

**4. Succession failure is a first-order cause and is under-rated.** Rome's
third century, Alexander's Diadochi, the Mongol *kurultai* of 1259–60, the
Ottoman *kafes*, the Soviet gerontocracy. Meanwhile every long-runner in
what-makes-them-last.md had a working succession technology. **The presence in
the failures and the absence in the survivors is as close to a controlled
comparison as this field gets.**

**5. Detection improvements always revise upward.** LiDAR in Mesoamerica, at
Angkor and in Amazonia; ancient DNA in disease history; ice cores in climate.
**The direction of revision has been consistent, which means current figures
should be read as floors.**

## Where to spend attention

If you have limited time, these are the highest-yield items.

| Priority | Why |
|---|---|
| **1. Learn the trigger/vulnerability distinction** | It reorganises every argument you will encounter and immediately disqualifies most popular accounts |
| **2. Always ask "collapse of what, for whom, measured how?"** | Catches the definitional switch that most popular collapse writing depends on |
| **3. Read one revisionist case fully** | Rapa Nui is the best. It shows how a compelling parable outran its evidence and how the correction worked |
| **4. Understand elite overproduction** | The most transferable mechanism, and the most testable |
| **5. Study the long-runners, not the collapses** | The literature ratio is ~10:1 the wrong way. Ostrom is the single most useful body of work |
| **6. Internalise the survivorship filter** | It discounts everything else in the notebook by an unknown but non-trivial amount |
| **7. Treat every ancient number as an order-of-magnitude estimate** | Antonine plague at 2–25%; pre-Columbian America at 8–112 million. These are not measurements |
