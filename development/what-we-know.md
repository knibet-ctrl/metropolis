# What We Actually Know — The Confidence Ledger

> **The key file.** Every significant claim in this notebook, sorted by how much
> weight it can bear. Read this before citing anything from the other files,
> including in an argument with yourself.

**The tiers:**

- **TIER 1 — Established.** Multiple independent lines of evidence, ideally from
  designs with different assumptions. Overturning it would require extraordinary
  evidence.
- **TIER 2 — Contested but tractable.** Serious people disagree; evidence and
  argument are relevant; progress is possible.
- **TIER 3 — Speculative.** Coherent, unfalsified, thinly evidenced. Hold loosely.
- **TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed.** No procedure could settle it.

**Standing caveats.**
1. Every number below was written from general knowledge with **no live
   verification**. Tier assignments reflect confidence in the *claim*, not in
   recall of the *citation*. Figures most worth checking are marked †.
2. **Developmental psychology has a serious replication problem.** Small samples,
   flexible measures, long lags, high policy demand. A field-wide replication
   project comparable to the Open Science Collaboration's has never been run.
   Adjust everything below downwards accordingly.
3. **Heritability claims here are population statistics in specific
   environments.** They say nothing about individual ceilings and nothing about
   causes of between-group differences. See nature-and-nurture.md.

## TIER 1 — Established

| Claim | Notes |
|---|---|
| **All psychological traits show non-trivial heritability** | Polderman et al. (2015): 14.5m twin pairs, 17,804 traits, mean h² ≈ 49%. Turkheimer's first law |
| **Heritability is a variance ratio, not a measure of malleability** | Follows from the definition. PKU, myopia, iodine, height, schooling |
| **Within-group heritability places no constraint on between-group causes** | Lewontin (1970). Mathematical, not rhetorical |
| **Heritability of cognitive ability rises with age; shared environment falls to ~0** | The Wilson effect. Adoptive-sibling adult IQ correlation ≈ 0.00 |
| **Shared environment explains little variance in adult personality in typical Western samples** | Converging twin, adoption and family designs. **The most surprising well-replicated finding in the field** |
| **Shared environment DOES explain substantial variance in religion, politics, education, income, and substance-use initiation** | The "parents don't matter" headline generalised from two outcome families to all of them |
| **The observational parenting literature is confounded by passive and evocative gene-environment correlation** | Parents supply both genes and environment; child behaviour evokes parental behaviour. Reported effects are upper bounds |
| **Severe institutional deprivation causes lasting cognitive, social and neural harm** | ERA study (Rutter, from 1992); BEIP randomised trial (Nelson, Zeanah, Fox, from 2000) |
| **Earlier removal from deprivation produces substantially better recovery** | ERA: adopted before ~6 months, largely recovered; after, persistent deficits into the mid-twenties |
| **Recovery from deprivation is domain-specific** | IQ, attachment and growth respond well; inattention and disinhibited social engagement much less |
| **Critical periods are real for binocular vision and first-language acquisition** | Hubel & Wiesel; late sign-language exposure produces permanent morphosyntactic deficits |
| **There is no demonstrated critical period for most abilities** | The "0-3 determines everything" edifice does not survive Bruer (1999) |
| **Rodent "enrichment" studies are deprivation studies relabelled** | The comparison condition is solitary confinement in a bare cage. This substitution underpins much of the popular early-years literature |
| **Schooling causally raises measured intelligence** | Ritchie & Tucker-Drob (2018): ~1-5 IQ points per year†, durable |
| **Education research effect sizes are systematically inflated** | Researcher-made measures ~0.40 vs standardised ~0.23†; published vs unpublished ~0.35 vs 0.19†. Use Kraft's (2020) benchmarks: >0.20 SD is large |
| **Learning styles do not work** | Pashler et al. (2008). The required interaction has essentially never been demonstrated. Believed by most teachers |
| **Spacing and retrieval practice work** | Among the best-supported findings in education |
| **Systematic phonics beats whole-language for early reading** | National Reading Panel (2000); Rose Review (2006); Ehri's meta-analyses |
| **Aggregating high-risk adolescents makes outcomes worse** | Deviancy training (Dishion, McCord & Poulin, 1999); Scared Straight OR ≈ 1.7 (Cochrane); Cambridge-Somerville 30-year follow-up |
| **Resilience is the modal trajectory after adversity** | Bonanno's trajectory work; PTSD is a minority outcome after most traumatic events |
| **Most people with high ACE scores do not develop the predicted outcomes** | Arithmetic consequence of low base rates and moderate relative risks |
| **Prospective and retrospective measures of childhood maltreatment identify largely different people** | Baldwin et al. (2019): κ ≈ 0.19† |
| **Infants under ~2.5 learn much less from screens than from live interaction** | Kuhl, Tsao & Liu (2003) is the cleanest demonstration |
| **Psychological debriefing after trauma does not prevent PTSD and may harm** | Cochrane (Rose et al., 2002). Still widely practised |
| **Refrigerator-mother and double-bind theories were false** | Autism h² ≈ 64-91%; schizophrenia h² ≈ 79-81% |
| **Facilitated communication messages are authored by the facilitator** | Controlled message-passing, dozens of studies, uniform result |
| **Vaccines do not cause autism** | Madsen et al. (2002), N = 537,303; Hviid et al. (2019), N = 657,461 |
| **Suggestive interviewing produces confident false reports in children** | Ceci & Bruck; Loftus & Pickrell (1995): ~25% false-memory implantation† |
| **Psychology samples are ~96% WEIRD for ~12% of humanity** | Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan (2010); developmental psychology is worse than average |
| **Parental time with children has risen since the 1960s, not fallen** | Time-use data across 11 countries (Dotti Sani & Treas, 2016) |
| **Adolescent sleep phase delay is biological** | Melatonin timing shift at puberty; school start times conflict with it |
| **Adolescents estimate risk about as well as adults; peer presence is what changes behaviour** | Gardner & Steinberg (2005): peer presence roughly doubled adolescent risk-taking, no effect in adults |
| **Most adolescent offending desists** | Moffitt's adolescence-limited group. Dunedin cohort |
| **Birth order has negligible effects on personality** | Rohrer et al. (2015), N≈20,000; Damian & Roberts (2015), N≈377,000 |
| **Non-transmitted parental alleles predict children's educational attainment** | Kong et al. (2018): "genetic nurture", ~30% of the transmitted effect. A measured environmental effect |

## TIER 2 — Contested but tractable

| Question | State of play |
|---|---|
| **Is the equal environments assumption sound enough?** | Zygosity-misclassification tests support it; chorionicity, twin closeness and assortative mating cut against. SNP heritability comes in well below twin heritability, which is consistent with twin estimates being too high. **Directional conclusions survive; precise percentages should not be quoted as constants** |
| **Why is SNP heritability so much lower than twin heritability?** | Rare variants, non-additivity, or inflated twin estimates. The single most consequential open technical question in the field |
| **Does heritability drop under deprivation?** | Turkheimer et al. (2003): h² ≈ 0.10 and C ≈ 0.60 in poor families. Tucker-Drob & Bates (2016): replicates in US samples, **not** in Western Europe or Australia. The welfare-compression explanation is plausible and untested |
| **What is the non-shared environment?** | Measured non-shared variables explain only a few percent each (Turkheimer & Waldron, 2000). Candidates: measurement error, developmental stochasticity, chance events. **Calling it "peers" is an assertion** |
| **Is Harris's group socialisation theory right?** | Her critique of the parenting literature is TIER 1. Her positive theory is TIER 3 and has produced little confirmatory evidence in thirty years |
| **How much of the ACE-outcome association survives genetic confounding?** | Sibling and twin designs shrink it, sometimes substantially. Not resolved |
| **Do early-intervention life-outcome effects persist while test scores fade?** | The pattern appears in Perry, Head Start long-run designs and STAR. But Tennessee VPK (Lipsey et al., 2018) found **third-grade harm** in a well-designed randomised study of a scaled programme |
| **Does universal pre-K work?** | Perry (N=123, 1962, no-preschool counterfactual) is the flagship. Tennessee is the best test of a scaled programme and it is negative. **The policy consensus rests on a much thinner evidential base than its confidence implies** |
| **Teacher value-added** | Chetty, Friedman & Rockoff (2014) effects are real in aggregate; Rothstein's sorting critique is unresolved; year-to-year reliability of ~0.3-0.5 makes individual ratings unusable |
| **Class size** | Randomised effect real (STAR, ~0.15-0.20 SD†); statewide scaling in California produced little benefit because it degraded teacher quality |
| **Attachment classification predictive validity** | d ≈ 0.15-0.35 across meta-analyses. Real population signal; individually uninformative |
| **Are attachment classifications cross-culturally equivalent?** | Distribution differences documented since 1988; instrument non-equivalence not excluded |
| **Did social media cause the post-2010 adolescent mental health decline?** | **Genuinely open.** Trends real and roughly synchronous across countries. Orben & Przybylski (2019): ~0.4% of variance†. Quasi-experiments (Braghieri et al. 2022; Allcott et al. 2020) find ~0.08-0.09 SD†. Small effects, alarming trends, unresolved causation |
| **The word gap** | Hart & Risley's 42 families and the 30-million figure do not survive as stated (Sperry et al., 2019). Language input matters; conversational turns predict better than word counts |
| **The dual-systems model of adolescence** | Behavioural pattern replicates cross-nationally; the neural interpretation is contested |
| **Growth mindset** | Sisk et al. (2018): d ≈ 0.08 on achievement. Yeager et al. (2019): ~0.10 GPA points for lower achievers. Real, tiny, marketed as large |
| **Marshmallow test predictive power** | Watts, Duncan & Quan (2018): association roughly halved and largely non-significant after controls for family background |
| **Nurse-Family Partnership** | Strong early US results; the UK Building Blocks trial (2015) found no effect on primary outcomes |
| **Does place cause outcomes?** | Chetty, Hendren & Katz (2016): MTO movers before age 13 earned ~31% more†; teen movers gained nothing. Strong design, one programme |

## TIER 3 — Speculative

| Claim | Why it is here |
|---|---|
| **Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance of experience in humans** | Blocked mechanically by two germline reprogramming waves. Dutch Hunger Winter: ~60 sibling pairs, one locus, no demonstrated phenotype. Överkalix: tens of individuals, no molecular measurement, effects that flip by sex |
| **"Toxic stress" biomarkers in humans** | Cortisol findings are heterogeneous with hyper- and hypo-cortisolism both reported; telomere effects small and publication-biased |
| **Candidate-gene × environment interactions** | Caspi et al. (2002, 2003) were enormously influential and have failed to replicate (Border et al., 2019). Treat single-gene GxE claims as unsupported |
| **Peer groups as the mechanism filling the non-shared residual** | Harris's proposal. Measured peer variables fill little of it |
| **Erikson's stages and Marcia's identity statuses** | Useful vocabulary, weak measurement, little predictive validity |
| **Emerging adulthood as a developmental stage** | Describes educated Westerners in a specific era; presented as developmental |
| **Evolutionary accounts of adolescent risk-taking** | Coherent, cross-species-suggestive, largely unfalsifiable as stated |
| **Attachment "internal working models"** | The theoretical core of attachment theory and the hardest part to measure |
| **Specific "trauma-informed" organisational practices** | Sensible orientation; specific-outcome evidence thin |
| **Birth-order niche theory** | The theory outlived the effect |
| **Most named parenting styles** (authoritative/authoritarian/permissive, Baumrind) | Correlational, genetically confounded, culturally specific. The authoritative advantage shrinks or reverses in several non-Western samples |

## TIER 4 — Unanswerable or malformed

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Is it nature or nurture?"** | Malformed. Neither operates without the other; the partition is a property of a population, not of a person or a trait |
| **"How much of *this child's* intelligence is genetic?"** | Category error. Heritability has no individual-level interpretation |
| **"What is the best way to raise a child?"** | Contains an unstated value premise about what "best" optimises. Empirically underdetermined, and see practices.md on why the framing is wrong |
| **"Was my childhood the cause of who I am?"** | Not answerable for an individual. Counterfactuals about a single life are unobservable |
| **"What would this person have been like with different parents?"** | Unobservable. Adoption designs answer a population version, not this |
| **"Is a happy childhood or a successful adulthood more valuable?"** | Value question. No study design bears on it |
| **"Do parents matter?"** as usually posed | Conflates five distinct questions. See the-questions.md |

## The big table — what people most commonly get wrong

| Belief | Status | The correction |
|---|---|---|
| Heritability means fixed | **False** | Variance ratio. Independent of malleability |
| High heritability means environment failed | **Backwards** | Heritability rises as environments improve and equalise |
| Within-group heritability implies between-group genetic causes | **False and dangerous** | Lewontin's seed argument. The historically load-bearing error in race science |
| Parents don't matter | **Misstated** | Differences among adequate Western parenting styles explain little variance in adult personality and IQ. Shared environment matters substantially for education, religion, politics, income and substance use — and none of this touches the child's actual experience |
| The first three years determine everything | **False** | Bruer (1999). Real sensitive periods exist for vision and first language; the general claim does not survive |
| Nursery care damages attachment | **False** | NICHD study; family factors predict far more |
| Insecure attachment predicts a damaged adult | **Overstated** | d ≈ 0.15-0.35 |
| A high ACE score predicts your outcomes | **False at the individual level** | Original authors warned against individual screening. Most people with ACE ≥ 4 do not have the outcomes |
| Trauma permanently rewires the brain | **Overstated** | Severe deprivation, yes. The general claim rests on a weak biomarker literature |
| Most trauma survivors get PTSD | **False** | Resilience is the modal trajectory |
| Your grandmother's trauma is in your DNA | **Not established** | Two germline reprogramming waves; the famous human studies are tiny and mechanism-free |
| Birth order shapes personality | **False** | Near-zero in well-powered within-family designs |
| Teenagers' brains aren't done until 25 | **Misstated** | No threshold at 25; the number is a sampling artefact of early MRI studies |
| Teenagers don't understand risk | **False** | They estimate it about as well as adults and deliberate more |
| Educational videos teach infants | **False** | DeLoache et al. (2010); the video deficit effect |
| Mozart makes babies smarter | **False** | 1993 study was college students, ~15 minutes, ~1.4 IQ points after meta-analysis |
| Learning styles | **False** | Pashler et al. (2008) |
| Self-esteem drives achievement | **Backwards** | Achievement drives self-esteem |
| Poor parents don't talk to their children | **Overstated and deficit-framed** | 42 families, extrapolated, primary caregiver only, contested by direct replication |
| Parents used to spend more time with children | **False** | Time-use data show the reverse |
| Group programmes help troubled teens | **Often harmful** | Deviancy training; Scared Straight; Cambridge-Somerville |
| Intensive parenting is evidence-based | **False** | Class-specific ideology; the modern intensification tracks admissions competition |
| Autism is caused by parenting | **False, and this belief did real harm** | h² ≈ 64-91% |

## The strongest convergences

Findings supported by designs with **different assumptions and different failure
modes** — the only kind worth real confidence:

1. **Genes matter substantially for psychological variation.** Twin studies,
   adoption studies, twins reared apart, SNP heritability and within-family
   polygenic prediction agree on direction despite sharing no assumptions.
2. **Severe deprivation causes lasting harm, and timing matters.** A natural
   experiment (ERA) and a randomised trial (BEIP) agree, with animal models
   giving a mechanism.
3. **Resilience is normal.** Trajectory analyses, cohort studies (Kauai) and
   base-rate arithmetic on the ACE data all converge.
4. **Aggregating troubled adolescents harms them.** Randomised trials
   (Cambridge-Somerville, Scared Straight) plus observational mechanism work
   (deviancy training).
5. **Environment moves cognitive ability at population scale.** The Flynn effect,
   schooling natural experiments, iodine and lead — four independent literatures.
6. **Determinism is not supported.** Post-deprivation recovery, adult turning
   points (Sampson & Laub), the size of the residual, and the fade of
   shared-environment effects all point the same way.

## Where to spend attention

| Priority | Why |
|---|---|
| **1. Avoid the catastrophic tail** | Severe deprivation, maltreatment, malnutrition, lead, untreated illness, prenatal alcohol and tobacco. **This is where the largest measured effects in the notebook are** |
| **2. Environment selection** | Place, school, peer group. Chetty's MTO result and Harris's actual practical implication point at the same lever |
| **3. Material security** | Poverty reduction has better evidence than any parenting technique |
| **4. Language and reading exposure, especially interactive** | Conversational turns, not word counts |
| **5. Sleep** | One of the few free, well-evidenced, universally applicable levers |
| **6. The relationship itself** | Not because it moves adult outcome variance. Because it is the child's life |
| **Last. Parenting style, enrichment products, technique optimisation** | Small measured effects, large anxiety costs, and the graveyard in the-control-group.md is full of confident advice from this category |
