# The Control Group — What Was Confidently Wrong

> **Status: TIER 1** on the facts of each case and on the current verdict.
> **TIER 2** on the mechanism attributions — why a field held a false belief is
> always a reconstruction.

> **Written from general knowledge with no live source verification.**

> **Why this file exists.** A notebook that only records what a field currently
> believes has no way to estimate how much that belief is worth. This file is
> the calibration set. **Every item below was mainstream, published,
> professionally endorsed and often government-funded.** Several caused
> quantifiable harm to real children and real parents.

> **The pattern that runs through almost every case: a plausible mechanism, a
> sympathetic audience, weak or absent controls, and an outcome measure supplied
> by the person delivering the intervention.**

## Refrigerator mother

**The claim.** Autism is caused by cold, emotionally withholding mothers.

Leo Kanner's founding 1943 paper described parents in whom he perceived "a
genuine lack of maternal warmth". **Bruno Bettelheim**, in *The Empty Fortress*
(1967), made it explicit and drew an analogy between autistic children and
concentration camp inmates, with the mother in the role of guard.

**The refutation.** Bernard Rimland — a psychologist and the father of an
autistic son — published *Infantile Autism* (1964) arguing for a neurobiological
basis. Twin and family studies followed. Current estimates place autism
heritability at roughly **64-91%** (Tick et al., 2016 meta-analysis; Sandin et
al., 2017, Swedish population cohort, ~83%), with substantial contributions from
de novo mutations and paternal age. Kanner himself later retracted the parental
causation claim.

**Bettelheim** additionally misrepresented his academic credentials, and former
residents of his Orthogenic School later described physical and emotional abuse.

**The harm.** Roughly twenty-five years in which mothers were told, by the
highest professional authority available, that their child's disability was
their fault. Some were advised to institutionalise their children or undergo
psychoanalysis. **This is the reference case for why this notebook does not
write blame into causal accounts of childhood difficulty.**

## Schizophrenogenic mothers and the double bind

**The claims.** Frieda Fromm-Reichmann (1948) coined "schizophrenogenic mother".
Bateson, Jackson, Haley & Weakland (1956) proposed the **double bind**:
schizophrenia arises from repeated exposure to contradictory injunctions from a
caregiver that cannot be escaped or commented on.

**The verdict.** No support. Schizophrenia heritability is estimated at roughly
**79-81%** (Sullivan et al., 2003 meta-analysis; Hilker et al., 2018, Danish twin
registry), with additional established risk factors including obstetric
complications, paternal age, cannabis use, urbanicity and migration.

**The one thing nearby that is real, and is constantly misread.** *Expressed
emotion* research (Brown, Vaughn & Leff) found that high criticism,
hostility and emotional over-involvement in a household predict **relapse rates
in already-diagnosed patients**. This is a finding about the course of an
existing illness, not its cause — and family-intervention programmes that reduce
EE reduce relapse. It is routinely reported as though it vindicated
family-causation theories. It does not.

**Steelman, briefly:** family environment does interact with genetic risk in
adoption studies (the Finnish Adoptive Family Study found elevated risk in
high-genetic-risk adoptees placed in disturbed families). That is a real
interaction effect on top of a predominantly genetic liability. It is not the
double bind.

## Recovered memory therapy and the satanic panic

**The claim.** Traumatic memories are repressed intact and can be recovered
through hypnosis, guided imagery and suggestive interviewing, and a network of
organised satanic ritual abuse in daycare centres was uncovering itself through
children's testimony.

**What happened.** The **McMartin Preschool** case (1983-1990) became the longest
and most expensive criminal trial in US history and produced **no convictions**.
Kern County, Wenatchee, Bakersfield and the Little Rascals case followed a
similar arc. In the UK, Rochdale and the Orkney cases produced child removals
later found unjustified.

**The evidence that dismantled it:**
- **Elizabeth Loftus** on misinformation and memory construction; Loftus &
  Pickrell (1995): roughly **25%** of participants came to report a false memory
  of being lost in a shopping mall as a child after suggestive interviewing.
- **Ceci & Bruck** on child suggestibility, including the "Sam Stone" studies:
  repeated suggestive interviewing produced confident, detailed, false reports
  from preschoolers that trained professionals could not distinguish from true
  ones.
- **Kenneth Lanning's FBI analysis (1992)** found no corroborated evidence of
  organised satanic ritual abuse networks despite extensive investigation.

**The two errors, and both matter.** Suggestive interviewing produces false
reports — that is established. **It does not follow that child sexual abuse is
rare, or that children generally lie about it.** Both errors — believing every
suggested report, and dismissing children's disclosures — have caused enormous
harm. The correct conclusion is narrow and procedural: **interview technique
determines evidentiary value**, which is why forensic interview protocols
(NICHD, Achieving Best Evidence) exist.

## Facilitated communication

**The claim.** Non-speaking autistic people can communicate by typing, with a
"facilitator" supporting their hand or arm. Douglas Biklen promoted it from
1990; institutions adopted it rapidly; it produced fluent, sophisticated
messages from people previously assessed as profoundly disabled.

**The test.** Controlled message-passing: show the client a picture the
facilitator cannot see, and see what gets typed. Wheeler et al. (1993), Montee
et al. (1995) and dozens of subsequent studies found the same result:
**the facilitator was the author**, almost always unknowingly. The American
Psychological Association, American Academy of Pediatrics, ASHA and others issued
formal statements against it.

**The harm.** Multiple families were accused of sexual abuse via facilitated
messages; some were prosecuted; some children were removed. **The method has
returned under new names — Rapid Prompting Method, Spelling to Communicate — with
the same absence of controlled authorship evidence.**

**Necessary clarification.** This is *not* a claim that non-speaking autistic
people lack inner lives, intelligence or things to say. It is a claim about
**authorship**, which is empirically testable and was tested. Genuine augmentative
and alternative communication — which does not involve a facilitator touching the
user — is well supported and is what the evidence recommends.

## Prevention programmes that did nothing, and ones that did harm

| Programme | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **DARE** (Drug Abuse Resistance Education, from 1983) | Police-delivered classroom drug education; reached ~75% of US school districts | Ennett et al. (1994) meta-analysis of 8 evaluations: effect on drug use ≈ **0.06 SD**, indistinguishable from zero. Lynam et al. (1999), 10-year follow-up: **no effects on any measure**. The programme continued and was eventually redesigned |
| **Scared Straight** (from 1978, Rahway State Prison) | Bring at-risk youth into prisons for confrontation with inmates | **Petrosino et al., Cochrane review (2002, updated 2013), 9 randomised trials: the intervention *increased* subsequent delinquency, odds ratio ≈ 1.68-1.72.** One of the clearest iatrogenic findings in criminology. Still televised and still run |
| **Cambridge-Somerville Youth Study** (1939-1945) | 506 boys randomised to intensive multi-year support — mentoring, tutoring, camps, medical care — or control | **Joan McCord's 30-year follow-up (1978): treated men had worse outcomes on alcoholism, serious mental illness, occupational status, and mortality.** Subjective reports from participants were positive. **The best-intentioned, best-designed harm in the history of the field, and the reason randomised evaluation matters** |
| **Boot camps for juvenile offenders** | Military-style discipline | Meta-analyses find no reduction in recidivism; some show increases |
| **Zero-tolerance school discipline** | Automatic exclusion for defined infractions | Associated with worse academic and behavioural outcomes and no improvement in school safety |

**The unifying mechanism for the harmful ones is deviancy training** (Dishion,
McCord & Poulin, 1999): aggregating high-risk adolescents lets them reinforce
each other. **Any intervention whose delivery mechanism is "put the troubled kids
in a room together" should be assumed harmful until a randomised trial says
otherwise.**

## The self-esteem movement

**The claim.** Low self-esteem causes academic failure, delinquency, drug use and
teen pregnancy; raising it fixes them.

**The institutional history.** The California Task Force to Promote Self-Esteem
and Personal and Social Responsibility (established 1986) commissioned academic
reviews. **The commissioned research largely failed to find the predicted
relationships. The report was published and promoted anyway**, and the movement
spread through American schools.

**The verdict.** Baumeister, Campbell, Krueger & Vohs (2003, *Psychological
Science in the Public Interest*) reviewed the literature: self-esteem does not
cause academic achievement — the causal arrow, where identifiable, runs from
achievement to self-esteem. It does not reduce delinquency, smoking, drinking or
early sexual activity. High self-esteem correlates with happiness and initiative,
and with some forms of aggression. **TIER 1 that the programme's premise was
false.**

Related: praising children for ability rather than effort has been shown in
several experiments to reduce persistence after failure (Mueller & Dweck, 1998) —
the mechanism runs opposite to the movement's practice.

## Baby Einstein, the Mozart effect, and screens for infants

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Baby Einstein / educational infant videos** | Zimmerman, Christakis & Meltzoff (2007): each daily hour of baby-DVD viewing was associated with roughly **6-8 fewer vocabulary words**† in 8-16-month-olds. DeLoache et al. (2010), randomised: infants learned **nothing** from the video relative to controls; parents who liked the video believed their children had learned. Disney offered refunds in 2009 |
| **The video deficit effect** | Infants under roughly 2-2.5 learn markedly less from screens than from equivalent live interaction. Kuhl, Tsao & Liu (2003) is the cleanest demonstration: 9-month-old English-learning infants exposed to Mandarin by a **live tutor** retained Mandarin phonetic contrasts; infants given **identical audio or video** exposure learned nothing. **TIER 1** |
| **The Mozart effect** | Rauscher, Shaw & Ky (1993): college students showed a **temporary, ~10-15 minute** improvement on a spatial task after listening to Mozart. It was never about babies. Chabris's (1999) meta-analysis put the true effect at roughly **1.4 IQ points**†, explained by arousal and mood. Georgia and Tennessee nonetheless distributed classical CDs to newborns at public expense |

## The "first three years" and critical-period overreach

The 1997 White House conference and the *I Am Your Child* campaign promoted the
claim that brain development in the first three years fixes lifelong capacity,
citing synaptic density curves and rodent enrichment studies.

**John Bruer's *The Myth of the First Three Years* (1999)** identified the errors,
and they hold: pruning schedules differ by region and continue into adolescence
and do not map onto learning capacity; "enriched" rodent environments are
normal-ish cages compared against solitary bare boxes, making them **deprivation
studies relabelled**; and adult plasticity is substantial.

**The harm is subtle but real:** it produces fatalism about children over three,
misallocates attention away from adolescence and adulthood, and adds a
neuroscientific gloss to parental anxiety. See attachment-and-early-years.md.

## Birth order

**The claim.** Sulloway's *Born to Rebel* (1996): firstborns are conscientious
and conformist, later-borns rebellious and open to experience, driven by
competition for parental investment niches.

**The verdict.** Well-powered studies find essentially nothing.

| Study | N | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Rohrer, Egloff & Schmukle (2015, *PNAS*) | ~20,000 across three national panels (US, UK, Germany) | **No effect on extraversion, agreeableness, conscientiousness, neuroticism or emotional stability.** A small effect on intellect/IQ: roughly **1.5 IQ points**† for firstborns |
| Damian & Roberts (2015) | ~377,000 US high-school students | Effects near zero, d ≈ 0.02, and confounded with family size and SES |

**The methodological lesson is general.** Between-family comparisons confound
birth order with family size, parental age and SES. **Within-family designs kill
the effect.** Any developmental claim supported only by between-family
comparisons should be treated as unestablished.

## Two more that matter

**Crack babies.** From the mid-1980s, US media and some researchers predicted a
generation of permanently damaged children — a "biologic underclass" —
from prenatal cocaine exposure. **Hallam Hurt's** long-running Philadelphia
cohort, following exposed and non-exposed children from poor neighbourhoods,
found **no significant cognitive differences attributable to cocaine exposure;
poverty was the dominant variable.** The panic drove prosecutions, child removals
and lasting stigma. Note the contrast with **fetal alcohol spectrum disorder and
prenatal tobacco exposure**, which are real, dose-related and well documented —
the field got one substance badly wrong in each direction.

**Vaccines and autism.** Wakefield's 1998 *Lancet* paper reported on **12
children**, was retracted in 2010, and its lead author was struck off the UK
medical register. Subsequent cohorts include Madsen et al. (2002), N = 537,303
Danish children, and Hviid et al. (2019), N = 657,461 — no association.
**TIER 1 that there is no link.** Measles has resurged in several
high-income countries as a result. This belongs in a child-development notebook
because it is the clearest modern case of a single small study, amplified by
sympathetic parental narrative, outrunning an enormous body of contrary evidence.

**And the marshmallow test**, briefly, since it is cited constantly: Mischel's
delay-of-gratification studies used small samples of Stanford-affiliated
preschoolers. Watts, Duncan & Quan (2018), with a larger and more diverse sample
and controls for family background and early cognitive ability, found the
predictive association **roughly halved and largely non-significant** after
controls. Delay of gratification partly measures whether a child has reason to
trust that promises are kept. **TIER 2 that the original claim was substantially
overstated.**

## Where the popular story is wrong

| Popular story | Reality |
|---|---|
| "These were fringe ideas" | Every one was mainstream. Bettelheim was a celebrated public intellectual; DARE was in three-quarters of US school districts; self-esteem had a state task force |
| "Science self-corrected quickly" | Refrigerator mother lasted ~25 years. Scared Straight is still running four decades after the first negative trials. Facilitated communication returns every decade under a new name |
| "Well-intentioned programmes are at worst useless" | Cambridge-Somerville and Scared Straight made outcomes measurably worse. **Absence of evaluation is not safety** |
| "Anecdotes from participants show it works" | Cambridge-Somerville participants reported the programme had helped them. It had harmed them. Subjective testimony and outcome data pointed in opposite directions |
| "We wouldn't make these mistakes now" | The ACEs screening rollout, the marshmallow-test literature and much of the "toxic stress" biomarker field have the same structure: plausible mechanism, weak measurement, high policy demand |

## The pattern, and what it should do to your priors

1. **Plausible mechanism plus sympathetic story plus no control group.** Present
   in every case above.
2. **The outcome measure was supplied by the person delivering the
   intervention.** Bettelheim, facilitated communication, self-esteem, Baby
   Einstein.
3. **Someone was blamed, and it was usually the mother.** Autism, schizophrenia,
   the word gap, crack babies.
4. **Between-family or uncontrolled comparisons.** Birth order, marshmallow test,
   nearly all observational parenting research.
5. **Scale before evaluation.** DARE, self-esteem, Scared Straight, ACEs
   screening.
6. **The correction is slower than the adoption by an order of magnitude.**

**Practical prior:** when you encounter a confident developmental claim, ask what
the control group was, who measured the outcome, whether the design was
within-family, and whether anyone is being blamed. Four questions, and they would
have caught every item in this file.
