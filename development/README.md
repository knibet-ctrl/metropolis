WHAT THIS IS
===============================================================================
An honest map of what is actually known about how humans turn out the way they
do, and how much of it anyone controls.

  What makes people differ from each other? What do all children need?
  What can a parent actually change? What predicts a life going well?
  Which famous developmental findings survived contact with better methods?

Written in the same discipline as leaders/, existence/, knowledge/ and risk/:
positions given fairly, disputes flagged as disputes, confidence marked, and a
standing refusal to pretend something is settled when it isn't.

===============================================================================
THREE WARNINGS, AND THE SECOND IS THE IMPORTANT ONE
===============================================================================
**1. No live sources were used.** This was written from general knowledge with
no verification against primary documents. Names, dates, sample sizes and
effect sizes are given because vague claims are unfalsifiable and therefore
useless — but **every specific number here should be checked before you cite
it.** Several are from memory and will be off. Where a figure is load-bearing,
I say so.

**2. This field has a documented history of scientific misuse, and the misuse
was not fringe.** Eugenics was mainstream, funded, peer-reviewed science with
Nobel laureates attached; roughly 60,000 people were sterilised in the United
States under laws upheld by the Supreme Court in *Buck v. Bell* (1927). The
refrigerator-mother theory blamed mothers for their children's autism for
roughly twenty-five years. The schizophrenogenic-mother and double-bind
theories did the same for schizophrenia. Race science has repeatedly attached
itself to genuine findings about heritability by committing a specific,
identifiable logical error — see nature-and-nurture.md, where the error is set
out at length rather than gestured at.

  **The rule this notebook follows:** every heritability claim is stated
  together with what heritability does *not* mean. Every group difference in
  outcomes is presented with the environmental, structural and measurement
  explanations given properly rather than as an afterthought. Nothing here
  states or implies that any group is inherently superior or inferior, because
  the evidence does not support that and the claim has a body count.

  **And a second rule.** Nothing in this notebook is written as blame directed
  at parents. Not at parents of autistic children, not at parents of children
  with mental illness or disability or difficulty, not at parents whose children
  turned out unhappy. The historical record on this is unambiguous: the field
  has caused more harm through misdirected blame than through any other error.

**3. Developmental psychology has a serious replication problem** and is one of
the areas hit hardest. Small samples, flexible outcome measures, long lags
between measurement and publication, and enormous public appetite for actionable
findings. Several results in this notebook that are still taught, still cited,
and still shaping policy do not survive well-powered replication. They are in
the-control-group.md rather than buried.

THE ONE IDEA THAT ORGANISES EVERYTHING
===============================================================================
**Most confusion in this field comes from silently switching between five
different questions.** They have different answers and the answers are not in
conflict.

  1. **What makes people DIFFER from each other?** — a variance question.
     Answer: genes a lot, family environment less than anyone expects,
     unsystematic chance a great deal.

  2. **What do all humans NEED?** — a level question, invisible to variance
     studies because almost everyone in the sample gets it. Answer: much more
     than question 1 implies.

  3. **What can a parent actually INFLUENCE?** — a causal-intervention question,
     and not the same as either of the above.

  4. **What PREDICTS life outcomes?** — a prediction question, mostly answered
     by structural variables (place, money, schooling, cohort) rather than by
     anything in the household.

  5. **What makes a childhood GOOD in itself?** — a value question. No amount of
     variance decomposition touches it.

**"Parenting has small effects on adult personality" and "how you treat your
child matters enormously" are both true.** The first is about variance between
people in a narrow range of environments. The second is about a person's actual
life. Confusing them is the single most common error in the popular literature
and in the backlash against it. -> the-questions.md, read it first.

TEN HEADLINE FINDINGS, COMPRESSED
===============================================================================
 1. **Everything psychological is heritable, and heritability is not
    destiny.** Polderman et al. (2015) meta-analysed 14.5m twin pairs across
    17,804 traits: mean heritability ~49%. This is a statement about variance
    in a population, not about any individual's ceiling, and it says **nothing
    whatsoever** about causes of differences between groups. **TIER 1** for the
    finding; **TIER 1** for the caveat.

 2. **Shared family environment explains close to zero variance in adult
    personality and adult cognitive ability in typical Western samples.**
    Adoptive siblings raised together correlate ~0 on IQ as adults. This is the
    most surprising well-replicated result in the field and the most abused.
    **TIER 1** for the finding, **TIER 2** for what it means.

 3. **But shared environment matters a great deal for things people care
    about**: religion, politics, smoking and drinking initiation, educational
    attainment, income, criminal onset, language, health behaviours. The
    "parents don't matter" headline generalised a result about personality to
    domains it never covered. **TIER 1**.

 4. **Deprivation changes the whole picture.** Turkheimer et al. (2003): in
    impoverished families, heritability of child IQ was ~10% and shared
    environment ~60% — the standard result inverted. Heritability is high when
    environments are good and similar. **TIER 2** — the interaction replicates
    in US samples but not consistently in Western Europe.

 5. **Severe early deprivation causes lasting harm and timing matters.** The
    Romanian adoptee studies are the clearest causal evidence in the notebook:
    children adopted out of institutions before ~6 months largely recovered;
    those adopted later showed persistent deficits into their mid-twenties.
    **TIER 1**.

 6. **Almost nothing else has a demonstrated critical period.** Vision does.
    First-language acquisition does. Most of the rest of the "0-3 determines
    everything" edifice was built on synaptic-pruning graphs and does not
    survive scrutiny (Bruer, 1999). **TIER 1** for the negative claim.

 7. **Early-intervention test-score gains reliably fade; life outcomes
    sometimes don't.** Head Start's cognitive advantage is gone by third grade,
    yet several long-run designs find real effects on education, earnings and
    health. The active ingredient is probably not the test score. **TIER 2**.

 8. **Schooling itself raises measured intelligence.** Ritchie & Tucker-Drob
    (2018) meta-analysed quasi-experimental designs: roughly **1-5 IQ points per
    additional year of education**, durable. One of the few large, causally
    clean environmental effects on cognitive ability. **TIER 1-2**.

 9. **Resilience is the normal response to adversity, not the exception.**
    Most people with high ACE scores do not develop the predicted outcomes.
    Bonanno's trajectory work finds stable low distress is the modal path after
    potentially traumatic events. **TIER 1**.

10. **Place is a stronger and cleaner lever than parenting style.** Chetty,
    Hendren & Katz (2016) on Moving to Opportunity: children who moved to
    lower-poverty neighbourhoods **before age 13** earned ~31% more as adults;
    those who moved as teenagers gained nothing. **TIER 2**, quasi-experimental
    with a randomised backbone.

HOW TO READ THIS
===============================================================================
Confidence tiers appear at the top of every file and in what-we-know.md:

  TIER 1  Established. Multiple independent lines of evidence.
  TIER 2  Contested but tractable. Serious people disagree; evidence bears.
  TIER 3  Speculative. Coherent, unfalsified, thinly evidenced.
  TIER 4  Unanswerable in principle, or the question is malformed.

If you read three files, read the-questions.md, the-control-group.md and
what-we-know.md. The control group is where the field's actual error rate is
visible, and it is the best available calibration for how much to trust the
current consensus.
