# Practices — What Actually Follows

> **Status: mixed and marked per item.** Ranked by evidence strength, not by how
> good they sound. Several widely promoted practices are **TIER 3** and are
> labelled as such rather than smuggled in beside the solid ones.

> **Written from general knowledge with no live source verification.**

> **This file is not advice to parents about how to be adequate.** It is a
> summary of what the evidence supports and, more importantly, what it does not
> support — which turns out to be most of what parents are told. **If you are
> reading this anxiously, read the last two sections first.**

---

## The honest summary before you start

**Almost all of the measured effect is in the tails.** Avoiding severe
deprivation, maltreatment, malnutrition, lead and untreated illness accounts for
more variance than every technique in the parenting literature combined. Above
that floor, the differences between reasonable approaches are small, hard to
measure, and mostly not what anyone is worrying about.

**The three things with the best evidence-to-effort ratio are: keep the child
safe and fed, talk and read with them interactively, and choose the environment
— place, school, peer group — as deliberately as your circumstances allow.**
Everything else is a rounding error by comparison, including nearly everything
sold as a parenting method.

## Ranked by evidence strength

| Rank | Practice | Tier | Why it's ranked here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Avoid severe deprivation, neglect and maltreatment** | **1** | The largest measured effects in the notebook, by a wide margin. ERA and BEIP |
| 2 | **Physical safety: car seats, water, firearms, medicines, safe sleep position** | **1** | Accidents are a leading cause of childhood death. This dwarfs every psychological variable in expected-value terms |
| 3 | **Do not smoke or drink alcohol in pregnancy** | **1** | FASD and prenatal tobacco effects are real, dose-related and well documented. *Note the contrast with the crack-baby panic, which was not* |
| 4 | **Adequate nutrition; treat deficiencies (iron, iodine, vitamin D where indicated)** | **1** | Iodine supplementation in deficient populations: ~10-13 IQ points |
| 5 | **Remove environmental lead** | **1** | Population-level IQ effects; one of the clearest environmental causal chains known |
| 6 | **Vaccinate** | **1** | No autism link (N > 1 million across cohorts). Measles is resurging where uptake fell |
| 7 | **Talk and read with the child, interactively** | **1-2** | Conversational turns predict better than word counts (Romeo et al., 2018). **Back-and-forth, not volume** |
| 8 | **Protect sleep — duration, regularity, and later school start times in adolescence** | **1-2** | Pubertal phase delay is biological; start-time trials show measurable benefits |
| 9 | **Respond to the child's signals** | **2** | This is what attachment security actually tracks — accurate reading and response, not any particular practice list |
| 10 | **Choose the environment: neighbourhood, school, peer group** | **2** | Chetty et al. (2016): moving before 13 raised adult earnings ~31%†. **This is also Harris's actual practical implication, and it is usually missed** |
| 11 | **Systematic phonics for early reading** | **1** | Converging evidence; a case where the profession was confidently wrong for thirty years |
| 12 | **Spacing and retrieval practice for anything being learned** | **1** | Best-supported findings in education. Applies to homework more than to parenting |
| 13 | **Small-group tutoring if a child is behind** | **1-2** | ~0.2-0.4 SD in good trials; among the largest reliable education effects |
| 14 | **Keep adolescents out of programmes that group them with other high-risk adolescents** | **1** | Deviancy training; Scared Straight OR ≈ 1.7; Cambridge-Somerville. **An avoid-harm item, not a do-good one** |
| 15 | **Limit screen media for under-2s in place of live interaction** | **2** | Video deficit effect. The evidence is about *substitution for interaction*, not about screens being toxic |
| 16 | **Reduce material insecurity where you can influence it** | **2** | Cash-transfer trials show mental-health benefits; poverty reduction removes several ACE categories directly |
| 17 | **Praise effort and strategy rather than fixed ability** | **3** | Mueller & Dweck (1998) and related work. Free, plausible, and the growth-mindset literature it belongs to has small effects. Do not oversell it |
| 18 | **"Authoritative" parenting — warm, clear, explains reasons** | **3** | Correlational, genetically confounded, culturally specific. Reasonable; not established |
| 19 | **Enrichment activities, classes, educational products** | **3, mostly no evidence** | See the-control-group.md. The burden of proof has not been discharged for essentially any of them |

## What the evidence does not support, however confidently it is asserted

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| A specific sleep-training method is necessary or harmful | Neither has been shown. Both camps overclaim |
| Breastfeeding produces large cognitive gains | Sibling-comparison designs shrink the effect substantially. The health benefits in low-resource settings are real and large; the IQ claims in high-income settings are small and confounded |
| Baby signing, Baby Einstein, infant flashcards, "brain training" | No demonstrated effects |
| Music lessons raise IQ | Small, inconsistent, and confounded by selection. Do them because music is worth doing |
| Attachment-parenting practice lists produce secure attachment | Not demonstrated. Security tracks responsiveness, which is compatible with many arrangements |
| Educational apps for preschoolers | Mostly untested; the tested ones are mostly null |
| Any single parenting book's system | No parenting book has been evaluated in a randomised trial with long-term outcomes |
| ACE screening tells you about your child's future | The original authors warned against individual-level use |
| Polygenic embryo screening for traits | Within-family predictive power is single-digit percentages and does not transport across ancestries. It is being sold anyway |

## Choosing the environment: the most underrated implication

Judith Rich Harris's argument is usually reported as "parents don't matter." Her
own practical conclusion was almost the opposite, and it is the most actionable
thing in the notebook:

**If peer groups and contexts do much of the socialising, then the parental
decisions that matter most are the ones that determine which peers and which
contexts.** Where you live, which school, which activities, which children are
routinely in the house, whether the child has a stable group where they are not
at the bottom of the status order.

This converges with Chetty's causal evidence on neighbourhoods and with the
deviancy-training literature on the harm of bad groupings. **Three independent
lines pointing at the same lever is unusual in this field.**

**Two honest caveats.** These decisions are heavily constrained by money and
circumstance, which makes this an argument about policy at least as much as
about parenting. And a child moved away from friends bears a real cost that
nothing in the outcome data captures.

## Reject the optimisation framing, and here is why the evidence supports rejecting it

The dominant modern framing treats a child as a project with an output metric,
and parenting as a set of levers to maximise it. **The evidence does not support
this framing, on its own terms.**

| Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **The levers are small** | Differences among adequate parenting styles explain little variance in adult personality or cognitive outcomes. You are optimising a low-variance parameter |
| **The measured variance is mostly not yours** | Genes, place, cohort, chance. The residual after those is where technique lives, and it is small |
| **Chance is a real developmental cause** | Genetically identical mice in a shared enriched environment diverge markedly (Freund et al., 2013). Some of who a person becomes is noise nobody could have controlled — which also means it is not anyone's fault |
| **The optimisation target is unstable** | The traits rewarded by the current admissions and labour market are not the ones that will be rewarded in thirty years. Optimising to a moving target is a forecasting error, and see risk/forecasting.md on how that goes |
| **The confident advice has a bad track record** | the-control-group.md is a list of things parents were told with authority that were false, several of which caused harm |
| **Intensive parenting is positional, not developmental** | Ramey & Ramey (2010): the modern intensification tracks admissions competition, not new knowledge. It is rational individually and zero-sum collectively |
| **The costs are real and measured** | Parental anxiety, exhaustion, reduced child autonomy and unstructured play, and a documented rise in parental time investment with no corresponding evidence of benefit |
| **It answers the wrong question** | Adult outcome variance is Question 1. A childhood is Question 5. See the-questions.md |

**The strongest version of the point:** a child is not a means of production for
the adult they will become. Sixteen years of a person's actual life are not an
input to year seventeen. If a practice makes those years worse in order to move
an outcome metric that the evidence says it probably will not move, the trade is
bad on both sides of the ledger.

## The relationship is worth having on its own terms

This is the part that variance decomposition cannot reach and that this notebook
would be dishonest to omit.

Behavioural genetics answers: *how much of the difference between adults tracks
differences in how they were raised?* The answer for personality and IQ is: not
much, in this range of environments. **It does not follow that how you treat your
child is unimportant**, for three reasons, all of which are stated elsewhere in
the notebook and belong here together:

1. **Variance is not level.** If every family in the sample provides warmth, the
   effect of warmth shows up as zero variance. Absence from a decomposition is
   not evidence of unimportance.
2. **The outcome measured is not the thing you care about.** A personality
   inventory at 30 is not a measure of whether a childhood was good.
3. **The relationship is a good in itself, and it is the child's actual life.**
   Whether a person felt safe, known and liked at home is not a proxy for
   something else. It is the thing.

**Both of these are true at once:** the evidence says you cannot engineer a
person, and the evidence gives you no reason at all to be cold, distant or harsh.
The people who read the behavioural genetics literature as licence for the second
have made a straightforward logical error, and the people who deny the first have
made a different one.

**What this permits, which is the practical payoff:** you can stop treating
ordinary parenting decisions as high-stakes. Most of them are not. The evidence
supports a floor — safety, food, warmth, attention, language, sleep, a stable
adult — and above that floor supports very little about technique. **That is
permission, not indifference.**

## And a note on blame

Nothing in this notebook licenses blaming parents for how a child turned out.

- Autism and schizophrenia are not caused by parenting. That theory did enormous
  damage and it was wrong.
- Most of the measured variance in adult outcomes is not attributable to
  differences in parenting.
- Some of it is irreducible chance.
- Children shape parents at least as much as the reverse, and much of the
  observed "parenting effect" in the literature is that arrow pointing backwards.
- Structural factors — money, place, health, cohort — dominate the variables
  available to any individual family.

**If your child is struggling, the field's own history is the best evidence
available that the explanation is unlikely to be you.**

## Where the popular story is wrong

| Popular story | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Every choice you make shapes your child's future" | Most measured effects of ordinary parenting choices are small. The tails dominate |
| "The science tells parents what to do" | The science mostly tells parents what not to worry about. That is the more useful finding and it does not sell books |
| "Doing more is better" | Time investment has risen sharply with no corresponding evidence of benefit, and with measurable costs to parents and to children's autonomy |
| "If parenting doesn't move outcomes, nothing matters" | Category error. Adult outcome variance is one question among five |
| "Behavioural genetics justifies detachment" | It does not. It bounds a claim about variance; it says nothing about how a child should be treated |
| "Good parents optimise" | The evidence supports a floor and very little above it. Optimisation is a positional strategy dressed as a developmental one |

## Open questions

- [ ] Has any parenting programme been evaluated with a randomised design and
      long-term outcomes, other than the intensive early-childhood programmes and
      home-visiting trials? Very few, and NFP failed to replicate in the UK.
- [ ] What is the actual cost of intensive parenting norms — to parental mental
      health, fertility, and children's autonomy? Barely measured against its
      claimed benefits.
- [ ] Does telling parents that most technique doesn't matter reduce anxiety or
      produce disengagement? Untested, and it is the key question for this file.
- [ ] Is there a defensible measure of "a good childhood" from the child's point
      of view, and why is it used so much less than adult outcome measures?
- [ ] How much of the environment-selection lever is available to families
      without money? If the answer is "little", this is a policy file, not a
      parenting file.
