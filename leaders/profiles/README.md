# Profiles — Index

35 deep profiles. Each follows the same template:

**What they inherited → Stories worth knowing → How they differed from named
contemporaries → Mechanisms used → The price → Where the popular story is wrong
→ The pattern**

The "where the popular story is wrong" section is in every file deliberately.
Most leadership writing repeats polished myths; these note what is disputed.

## Recurring findings across all 35

1. **Almost none were the most talented person available.** They were the best-positioned and the most persistent.
2. **The durable ones built systems.** The ones who relied on personal genius left ruins.
3. **Every one paid a specific, named price their peers declined to pay.** Obscurity, prison, unpopularity, monotony, danger, decades.
4. **An error-correction channel separates the two samples more reliably than talent.** It appears in nearly every success and is absent or destroyed in nearly every failure.
5. **New-medium capture recurs in every era** — Caesar's dispatches, Ashoka's edicts, the printing press, FDR's radio, King's television, Zelenskyy's phone.
6. **Timing and luck are doing more work than any of these profiles can show.** FitzRoy is the proof.

## Reliability

These profiles were written from general knowledge without live source
verification. Dates, quotations and figures carry the same caveats as the rest
of the notebook — see the flagged-claims table in
[sources-and-caveats.md](../sources-and-caveats.md).

Where a claim is disputed, the profile says so inline. Treat "where the popular
story is wrong" as the most reliable section in each file and everything else as
a starting hypothesis.
