# Volodymyr Zelenskyy (b. 1978)

*President of Ukraine since 2019. The notebook's case study in a single costly signal at a decisive moment.*

*Note: a living leader in an ongoing war. Assessment here is necessarily provisional, and the historical verdict cannot be written yet.*

---

## What he inherited

An unusual background for the notebook: **a career in comedy**.

Born in Kryvyi Rih to a Jewish family — his grandfather fought in the Red Army
and his great-grandparents were killed in the Holocaust. He trained as a lawyer,
never practised, and instead built **Kvartal 95**, a successful production
company.

He starred in **"Servant of the People"** (2015), a sitcom in which a
schoolteacher accidentally becomes president of Ukraine and fights corruption.
He then formed a real political party with the same name and won the 2019
presidential election with roughly 73% of the vote in the run-off.

He was widely regarded as an unserious figure — a comedian who had played a
president and then become one. His early presidency was mixed: an
anti-corruption agenda with limited results, and approval ratings that had
fallen substantially by late 2021.

Politically: a country that had lost Crimea in 2014 and had been fighting a
grinding war in the Donbas since then, with deep corruption problems and a
much larger hostile neighbour.

## Stories worth knowing

**25 February 2022 — the selfie video.** This is the moment the notebook is
interested in.

Russia invaded on 24 February. Widespread expectations — including in Western
intelligence assessments reported at the time — were that Kyiv would fall
within days and the government would collapse or flee. The US offered to
evacuate him.

He declined. The response widely reported and repeated afterwards was that he
needed ammunition, not a ride.

*(Caveat: this exact wording comes via intermediaries and is not confirmed
first-hand. Treat it as reported rather than verified.)*

Then, that night, he did the thing that mattered. He filmed a video **on a
phone, on a street in central Kyiv**, standing with his senior officials —
Prime Minister Shmyhal, adviser Podolyak, and others — naming each of them,
and stating that they were all there, in Kyiv, defending Ukraine's
independence.

*(The often-quoted "I'm here" is a paraphrase; the reported wording is closer
to "We are all here.")*

**Why this worked, mechanically:**

1. **It was unfakeable.** Recognisable Kyiv landmarks, live, at night, on a phone — no studio, no green screen, no possibility of pre-recording elsewhere. It answered the Russian claim that the government had fled with evidence rather than assertion.
2. **It was a costly, irreversible signal.** Staying in Kyiv while an armoured column advanced on it was a genuine risk of death. Under mechanism #9, a signal only carries information if it would be irrational to send while lying. This one qualified absolutely.
3. **It collapsed the expected-collapse narrative in one stroke.** Both domestically and internationally, the assumption of imminent capitulation was the single most important variable, and thirty seconds of phone video destroyed it.
4. **It converted his career into an asset.** A decade in television meant he knew exactly how to use the camera — framing, tone, brevity, no lectern, no notes.

**The daily video addresses.** From that point he recorded near-daily messages —
usually in a plain olive T-shirt or fleece, often filmed simply, always in the
same register. He deliberately abandoned suits. It became a uniform, and a
signal that the country remained on a war footing.

**Addressing foreign parliaments — tailored, not templated.** He spoke by video
link to a long list of legislatures, and crucially **rewrote the argument for
each audience**:

- To the **UK Parliament**, he echoed Churchill's "we shall fight" construction and Shakespeare's "to be or not to be."
- To the **US Congress**, he invoked Pearl Harbor and 11 September, and showed a video montage.
- To the **German Bundestag**, he invoked the Berlin Wall and the phrase "never again."
- To the **Israeli Knesset**, he made comparisons to the Holocaust — which was controversial and drew criticism there.

Whatever one thinks of individual choices, the method is mechanism #5 executed
at industrial pace: **argue from each audience's own history and values, not
from your own grievance.** It is the same move as King anchoring civil rights
in the Declaration of Independence.

**Presence as policy.** He travelled repeatedly to front-line areas — Bakhmut,
Kherson after its recapture, Izium — and made those visits visible. He made
unannounced trips abroad, including to Washington in December 2022, leaving the
country during wartime.

## How he differed from his contemporaries

Compare with **Ashraf Ghani** in Afghanistan, six months earlier. Ghani was, on
paper, vastly more qualified: a World Bank economist, an academic who had
literally co-authored a book on fixing failed states. When Kabul fell in August
2021, he **left the country**.

The state collapsed immediately. Whatever else was true about the Afghan
government's capacity, the departure of the president removed the last reason
for anyone to keep fighting.

**Same category of moment, opposite decision, opposite outcome. This is the
sharpest available natural experiment for mechanism #9 in the modern era.**

Compare with **Viktor Yanukovych**, Ukraine's own president in 2014, who fled to
Russia when the Maidan protests turned against him.

Compare with the **expectations of the professionals**. Intelligence services,
analysts and much of the commentariat modelled the military balance correctly
and the **will to resist** incorrectly. The notebook's recurring point: models
that omit legitimacy and morale systematically mispredict.

## The price

Ongoing and enormous — not primarily his own. Ukrainian civilian and military
casualties, millions displaced, cities destroyed, infrastructure attacked
through successive winters.

Personally: his family was moved to safety; he has described assassination
attempts; and he has been in continuous crisis leadership for years, with the
attendant costs.

**Politically, the risks of his approach are real and should be stated.**
Wartime concentration of power, the suspension of elections under martial law,
restrictions on some media and opposition parties, and the persistent corruption
scandals — including dismissals of officials over procurement — are legitimate
subjects of criticism. Whether wartime necessity justifies them is exactly the
argument that surrounds Lee Kuan Yew and Lincoln in this notebook, and it is not
resolved.

## Where the popular story is wrong

- **"I need ammunition, not a ride"** is reported second-hand and is not verified. It may well be substantially accurate; it is not a documented quote.
- **"He was a nobody comedian."** He ran a large, successful media business — meaning he had genuine executive experience in a hard commercial industry.
- **"Ukraine united spontaneously behind him."** Ukrainian identity had been consolidating since 2014, and Russia's invasion did more to unify the country than any leader could have. He channelled an existing shift.
- **"His communication won the war."** It won *support* — weapons, money, sanctions. The fighting was done by the Ukrainian military and population, and the outcome depends on factors far outside his control.
- **Beware of hagiography generally.** He is a living wartime leader whose reputation is an active instrument in an ongoing conflict, and the notebook's caution about narrative bias applies here more strongly than anywhere else in the collection.

## The pattern

**Zelenskyy contributes the cleanest modern demonstration of mechanism #9: in a
legitimacy crisis, one unambiguous act of shared risk does more than a hundred
statements.**

The general principle: **presence is a strategy.** When people are deciding
whether to commit to something costly, the single most informative signal is
whether the person asking them has committed first, visibly, in a way that
cannot be reversed.

The transferable version for anyone without a war: the moment your team,
customers or allies are deciding whether you are serious, **statements are
discounted to zero and irreversible actions are not.** Talk is free, which is
exactly why nobody believes it.

The honest caveat: mechanism #9 also appears in the failure sample. Costly
signals are what make commitment credible, and they are also what make
escalation impossible to stop. The verdict on this case is not yet available.
