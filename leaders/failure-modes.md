# Failure Modes — Every Mechanism Has an Evil Twin

The same traits that produced the outcomes in these files also produced
catastrophe, often in the *same person*. This file exists so the notebook
isn't a motivational poster.

## The three recurring destroyers

### 1. No stopping rule
Napoleon had no definition of "enough". Neither did Alexander, Hitler, or most
over-extended empires and companies. **Define the victory condition *before*
you start, in writing.** Success without a stopping rule reliably becomes failure.

### 2. Information starvation at the top
This is the most common killer of previously-effective leaders. Success →
deference → nobody delivers bad news → decisions made on fiction.
Every long-lived effective leader had a deliberate counter-mechanism:
- Lincoln kept rivals in the room.
- Toyota gave the line worker a cord that stops the factory.
- Amazon's "disagree and commit" legitimises visible dissent before the decision.
- Genghis promoted the man who had shot at him.

**If nobody has told you you're wrong this month, your information supply is broken.**

### 3. Confusing effectiveness with virtue
Genghis Khan, Caesar, Napoleon, Mao and Stalin were *effective*. Several were
responsible for mass death. Studying their methods is legitimate; admiring
them is a category error. Method and morality are separate axes, and the
biography industry constantly blurs them.

## The sequence of decline

Decline is not random. Across the profiles it runs in the same order, and each
stage is invisible from inside the one before it.

**Stage 1 — Success.** The mechanisms work. Real achievement, correctly
attributed.

**Stage 2 — Attribution error.** The success is attributed to *judgement* rather
to judgement plus circumstance plus luck. Nobody corrects this, because
correcting it looks like envy.

**Stage 3 — Deference.** People stop arguing. Not from fear yet — from
reasonableness. *They've been right before; who am I?* **This is the stage where
intervention is still cheap and nobody intervenes.**

**Stage 4 — Information decay.** Bad news arrives later, softer, and pre-filtered.
The leader still feels well-informed, because the volume of information has not
dropped — only its accuracy.

**Stage 5 — The first uncorrected error.** A decision is made on degraded
information. It is survivable, so it teaches the wrong lesson: that the
deference was fine.

**Stage 6 — Dissent becomes costly.** Someone objects and is punished, sidelined
or humiliated. **Everyone else observes this and updates.** Ford humiliated
Edsel. Mao purged Peng Dehuai. Holmes litigated against Tyler Shultz.

**Stage 7 — Fiction.** The reporting system now produces what the leader wants
to hear. Communes reporting fictitious harvests. Demonstrations run on hidden
commercial analysers.

**Stage 8 — Catastrophe**, whose size is proportional to the concentration
(#3) and the time horizon (#2) that were previously assets.

**Where to intervene:** Stage 3. It is the last stage that costs nothing and
the last stage the leader can still perceive. By Stage 6 the correction channel
is structurally gone and the system will not repair itself.

**The diagnostic for Stage 3–4, usable on yourself:** not "am I open to
feedback" — everyone answers yes. Ask instead: **when did someone last tell me
I was wrong, what specifically did they say, and what happened to them
afterwards?** If you cannot answer all three with a concrete instance from the
last month, you are already past Stage 3.

## Traits that look like the mechanisms but aren't

| Looks like | Actually is | How to tell |
|---|---|---|
| Conviction | Stubbornness | Conviction updates on evidence; stubbornness reinterprets evidence |
| Long-term thinking | Procrastination | Long-term thinking ships something every month |
| Focus | Avoidance | Focus says no to good things; avoidance says no to hard things |
| Contrarian insight | Contrarian for its own sake | Real insight is *specific* and *falsifiable*, and you'd bet on it |
| Costly signal | Self-harm as performance | A costly signal changes someone's behaviour; performance just costs you |
| Charisma | Nothing | Charisma is a correlate of success, not a cause. It's what we *remember* about winners |

## The single hardest question in this notebook

Churchill was ignored for a decade and was right.
Ten thousand other people were ignored for a decade and were simply wrong.

**From the inside, in year 4, these two feel identical.**

Partial defences — not solutions:
- Make your prediction **specific, dated, and public**. Vague predictions can't be wrong, which means they can't be right either.
- Define in advance **what evidence would make you abandon it**. Write it down. If you can't name any, you're not holding a thesis, you're holding an identity.
- Check whether your position is **cheap or expensive to hold**. If being contrarian is winning you attention and community, it's a cheap position and probably not insight.
- Distinguish "consensus hasn't got here yet" from "consensus considered this and rejected it for reasons I can't rebut."
