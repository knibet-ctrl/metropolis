# What We Actually Know — The Confidence Ledger

> **The key file.** Everything in this notebook, sorted by how much weight it
> can bear. Read this before acting on anything else here, including this
> sentence.

**The tiers:**

- **TIER 1 — Established.** Multiple independent lines of evidence, or it is
  close to definitional. Overturning it would require extraordinary evidence.
- **TIER 2 — Contested but tractable.** Serious people disagree; evidence is
  relevant; progress is possible.
- **TIER 3 — Speculative.** Coherent, unfalsified, weakly evidenced. Hold
  loosely.
- **TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed.** No procedure could
  settle it.

**Standing caveat: none of this was verified against sources. It was written
from general knowledge. Directions of effect are more reliable than magnitudes,
and magnitudes are more reliable than the decimal places. Historians dispute
essentially every single-cause technology story in this notebook.**

## TIER 1 — Established

### The structural claims

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **The same technology produces different outcomes in different societies** | Printing: Europe vs China vs Korea. The pill: US vs Japan (approved 1999) vs Ireland. Firearms: Europe vs Tokugawa Japan. **This is the central finding of the field** |
| **Societies can and do decline or reverse technologies they possess** | Tokugawa firearm restriction; Japan's 39-year delay on the pill; the Montreal Protocol; leaded petrol elimination; the US overland supersonic ban; germline editing moratoria |
| **Technologies change relative costs; they do not dictate outcomes** | The general form of the above |
| **Material constraints are real and non-negotiable** | Coal is heavy; character-set size determines movable type economics; sonic booms carry; thermodynamic limits exist |
| **Complementary innovation and organisational adaptation lag the technology by decades** | Electrification: motors from the 1880s, productivity from the 1910s–20s (David 1990). Firm-level IT returns depend on organisational investment (Brynjolfsson & Hitt) |
| **The inventor almost never captures the value** | Gutenberg (bankrupt), Tantlinger (royalty-free), Berners-Lee (gave it away), Engelbart (patent expired), Sasson/Kodak |
| **Whoever controls the standard captures durable value** | Qin script and measures (221 BCE, still operative); ISO container dimensions; TCP/IP; GSM |
| **Displacement costs are concentrated and immediate; gains are diffuse and delayed** | Handloom weavers; longshoremen; the general asymmetry that explains all technology politics |

### Empirical findings

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Rebound effects are real and non-zero** | Jevons 1865; direct rebound ~10–30% for household energy services (UKERC 2007) |
| **Lighting shows outright backfire** | Nordhaus (1996) on the price of light; consumption rose by far more than efficiency |
| **Road capacity expansion induces near-proportional traffic** | Duranton & Turner (2011): VMT elasticity ≈ 1.0 for US metropolitan interstates |
| **Computerisation substituted for routine tasks and polarised employment** | Autor, Levy & Murnane (2003); Goos & Manning (2007); Autor & Dorn (2013). Replicated across Western Europe |
| **Lead is a potent neurotoxin that lowers IQ and impairs executive function, with no safe threshold** | Needleman and a very large subsequent literature |
| **British real wages stagnated for roughly four decades after output per worker began rising** | Feinstein (1998); Allen's "Engels' pause"; heights stagnated or fell for cohorts born c. 1820–50 |
| **The Luddites targeted specific machines used to break wage rates and trade rules, not machinery as such** | Their own petitions and letters; Thompson (1963); Binfield |
| **AC diffusion caused a large fall in heat-related mortality in the US** | Barreca, Clay, Deschênes, Greenstone & Shapiro (2016) |
| **US polarisation rose fastest among the least-online demographics** | Boxell, Gentzkow & Shapiro (2017) |
| **Three months of feed-algorithm change produced ~no effect on polarisation** | 2023 Meta election studies (*Science*/*Nature*) |
| **US productivity growth accelerated 1995–2004 and fell back after 2005** | Standard BLS series; Jorgenson, Oliner & Sichel on the acceleration |
| **GDP mismeasurement of free digital goods cannot explain the post-2005 slowdown** | Syverson (2017) |
| **Patents are the primary appropriability mechanism in very few industries** | Levin et al. (1987); Cohen, Nelson & Walsh (2000). Pharma and chemicals yes; most others rely on lead time and secrecy |
| **Local newspaper closure raises municipal borrowing costs** | Gao, Lee & Murphy (2020) |
| **AI task-level RCT gains concentrate in the least-experienced workers** | Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (2023); Noy & Zhang (2023); Dell'Acqua et al. (2023). Replicates across studies |
| **US nuclear construction costs rose with cumulative experience; South Korea's fell** | Lovering, Yip & Nordhaus (2016) |
| **Adoption ceilings and rates track profitability, not information** | Griliches (1957) on hybrid corn |
| **The Montreal Protocol worked; the ozone layer is recovering** | Universal ratification; WMO/UNEP assessments |

## TIER 2 — Contested but tractable

| Question | State of play |
|---|---|
| **Why Britain, why then?** | Coal geology (Wrigley), high wages (Allen), New World land (Pomeranz), agrarian institutions (Brenner), property rights (North/Acemoglu), useful-knowledge culture (Mokyr), rhetoric (McCloskey). **Nobody has won.** Allen's wage data is under attack from Stephenson and Humphries |
| **When did Europe and China diverge?** | Pomeranz says ~1750; Broadberry's GDP reconstructions say much earlier. A factual dispute about reconstructed data |
| **Did printing cause the Scientific Revolution?** | Necessary condition at most. The diagram/table reproduction channel is the strongest part; Eisenstein's broader claims are contested by Johns |
| **Was fixity a property of print or an institutional achievement?** | Eisenstein vs Johns. Possibly a dispute about emphasis dressed as one about fact |
| **How large is economy-wide rebound?** | Direct rebound is measured; economy-wide is not, and it matters enormously for energy and AI policy |
| **Is risk compensation large or small?** | Real (ABS taxi evidence); usually partial; Peltzman's strong version did not survive Robertson and Crandall & Graham. Depends on salience and who bears the risk |
| **Net employment effect of robots** | Acemoglu & Restrepo (2020) find losses; Graetz & Michaels (2018) find none in aggregate. Both respectable, different data |
| **Do patents promote or retard innovation?** | Industry-dependent. Strong for pharma. Moser's evidence that patent-free countries innovated comparably but *specialised differently* is the most interesting finding |
| **How much of the pill's measured effect is the pill?** | Goldin & Katz vs Myers on abortion access, plus enormous confounding from the women's movement, Title IX, no-fault divorce and labour demand shifts |
| **AC's share of Sun Belt growth** | One cause among several (highways, air travel, defence spending, retirement migration). Not decomposed |
| **Smartphones and adolescent mental health** | Haidt vs Odgers/Orben/Przybylski. Timing coincidence striking; effect sizes in the best studies small; causal evidence weak. **Do not resolve this** |
| **Why did productivity growth fall after 2005?** | The most important unanswered question in modern economics. Candidates: idea-generation getting harder, mismeasurement (largely refuted), diffusion lag, market concentration, demand weakness |
| **Are energy transitions necessarily slow?** | Smil's historical prior vs recent solar and battery deployment rates. Live, and resolvable by about 2035 |
| **Did the container do more than trade policy for globalisation?** | Bernhofen et al. suggest yes. Not decomposed against Chinese domestic reform |
| **How much of nuclear's stall is cost vs politics?** | The Korean cost trajectory suggests a large institutional component. Not settled |
| **Scope of network effects** | Global (Facebook) vs local (delivery apps, messaging by country). No predictive theory of which |

## TIER 3 — Speculative

| Claim | Why it stays here |
|---|---|
| **Any specific forecast of AI's economic effect** | Acemoglu's ~0.5–0.7% TFP over a decade and the transformative projections are both extrapolations from assumptions. **Boosters and dismissers are equally unevidenced** |
| **Whether task reinstatement will continue** | The historical base rate is strong and base rates fail exactly at regime changes. Unfalsifiable in advance |
| **Lead explains a large share of the 20th-century crime decline** | Higney, Hanley & Moro (2022) found strong publication bias; the corrected effect is much smaller. Lead is a catastrophe for other, better reasons |
| **Economy-wide rebound produces backfire for general-purpose technologies** | Plausible, argued, not established |
| **Print-capitalism created national consciousness** | Anderson (1983). Elegant, influential, weakly evidenced as causation |
| **The ~40-year GPT adaptation lag is a constant** | Two or three cases fitted to a line. Might be right; is not established |
| **The Moses bridges were designed to exclude buses** | Rests on a single Caro interview; contradicted by the existing legal ban on commercial traffic. **Repeated as fact for forty years** |
| **Ellul's autonomous technique** | Predicts a real, observable pattern (technical fix generates next problem) but is unfalsifiable as stated, and Montreal is a counterexample |
| **Mumford's authoritarian/democratic technics correlation** | Vaccines, sewers and the grid are large, centralised and liberating. The correlation is much weaker than claimed |
| **Whether the current AI capability trend continues, plateaus, or requires new architecture** | Unknown to everyone, including the builders |

## TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Was technology X net good for humanity?"** | Requires aggregating welfare across incommensurable people and generations against an unobservable counterfactual. Not a question with a fact of the matter |
| **"Would the Industrial Revolution have happened without coal?"** | Counterfactual over a 200-year system with no held-out cases |
| **"Is technological progress inevitable?"** | Unfalsifiable as posed. Any non-occurrence is reinterpreted as delay |
| **"When will we reach AGI?"** | "AGI" has no agreed operational definition. **Malformed, not merely uncertain** |
| **"Would we be happier with less technology?"** | Preference formation is endogenous to the technology. There is no external vantage point |
| **"Does technology have an inherent direction?"** | Metaphysics wearing empirical clothes |
| **"Who really invented X?"** | Almost always malformed. Simultaneous independent invention is the norm (Ogburn & Thomas catalogued 148 cases), and "invention" is a legal and rhetorical category, not a natural one |

## Things people most commonly get wrong

**The highest-value table in the notebook.**

| Widely believed | Actually |
|---|---|
| The printing press spread knowledge and caused progress | It made copies cheap. It amplified the *Malleus Maleficarum* as efficiently as Luther. Reproduction cost falling does not select for truth |
| China had printing but did nothing with it | Chinese print output was vast. Korea had metal movable type by 1377. **They lacked a Reformation to have, not a press to have it with** |
| Watt's engine caused the Industrial Revolution | Water power dominated early factories; steam's productivity contribution was modest before ~1830. Transport mattered more than the factory |
| Industrialisation quickly raised living standards | ~40 years of flat real wages, falling heights, catastrophic urban mortality first |
| The Luddites hated technology | They objected to specific uses of machines to break wage rates and trade rules, after Parliament removed their legal remedies. They were correct about themselves |
| Efficiency reduces consumption | Frequently the opposite, and reliably less than the engineering estimate |
| Building more roads reduces congestion | Elasticity ≈ 1.0. One of the best-identified results in urban economics, ignored in essentially every business case |
| Social media caused political polarisation | Polarisation rose fastest among the least-online. Large field experiments find ~no effect |
| Algorithms trap people in filter bubbles | Personal choice does more than the algorithm; online media diets are more diverse than offline for most people |
| QWERTY proves markets lock into bad standards | The flagship case is largely myth (Liebowitz & Margolis). **Lock-in is real — use mains voltage or railway gauge** |
| Betamax was better and lost | Worse on recording length and licensing, which were the deciding attributes |
| Kodak ignored digital | Kodak invented it and invested heavily. **The business model died, not the management** |
| Concorde was killed by the 2000 crash | Killed by fuel economics and the 1973 US overland supersonic ban |
| "Too cheap to meter" was a promise about nuclear power stations | Misquoted and misattributed, and it has damaged nuclear's credibility for seventy years |
| The internet made geography irrelevant | Agglomeration intensified. Confidently predicted, cleanly falsified |
| The stirrup created feudalism | Chronology doesn't work. Cited constantly anyway |
| The Moses bridges prove artefacts have politics | Weakest part of Winner's case. Use the tomato harvester instead |
| Montreal proves we can solve climate | Montreal was easy: few producers, patentable substitutes, a vivid image, rich-country self-interest. Climate has none of those |
| Data is the new oil | Returns to data diminish quickly in most applications |
| China has a unified social credit score | Largely a Western misconception. Fragmented systems, no unified citizen score |
| The inventor gets rich | Almost never |
| The best technology wins | See the-control-group.md, all of it |
| AI will replace X% of jobs by year Y | No such forecast has a defensible method |
| AI is just autocomplete and nothing will change | Equally unsupported. Task-level effects are measured and real |

## The strongest convergences

**Where independent lines of evidence agree, which is the closest thing to
knowledge in this domain:**

1. **Determinism fails on comparative evidence.** Printing (Europe/China/Korea),
   firearms (Europe/Japan), the pill (US/Japan/Ireland), nuclear power
   (France/US/Germany). Four independent domains, same finding. **This is the
   most robust result in the notebook.**
2. **Adaptation lags the technology by decades**, and the adaptation is the
   actual change. Electrification, IT, containerisation, printing. Different
   centuries, same shape.
3. **Behavioural response erodes engineering estimates.** Rebound, induced
   demand, risk compensation, moral hazard. Different literatures, same
   mechanism: people re-optimise when a constraint moves.
4. **Value accrues to the scarce complement, not to the invention.** Every case
   in this notebook.
5. **Costs are concentrated and immediate; benefits are diffuse and delayed.**
   This one asymmetry explains the politics of every case here, from the
   croppers to the dockers to the present.
6. **The flagship example of a good idea is frequently its weakest evidence.**
   QWERTY, the Moses bridges, the stirrup, "too cheap to meter." **Check the
   canonical case before repeating it.**

## Where to spend attention

| Priority | Why |
|---|---|
| **1. Ask what became cheap, in orders of magnitude** | The single most predictive question available, and the most skipped |
| **2. Ask what became scarce as a result** | Where the value and the pathology both go |
| **3. Ask who bears the concentrated cost, and whether any of the gain reaches them** | Determines the politics, and it is forecastable in advance, unlike most things here |
| **4. Assume a decades-long organisational adaptation lag** | Consistent with every case in the notebook |
| **5. Model the behavioural response before believing the engineering estimate** | Rebound, induced demand, compensation |
| **6. Check the canonical example** | It is wrong more often than chance would suggest |
| **7. Build for robustness rather than forecast accuracy** | Social consequences at 20+ year horizons are close to unforecastable. Cross-ref `risk/risk-management.md` |
| **8. Notice the boring infrastructure** | The container has no ideology and restructured the world. Ask what is currently boring and ubiquitous |
