# Reading

> **STATUS: recommendations, with caveats.** Everything here is listed from
> general knowledge; titles, dates and publishers have **not** been verified.
> Check before citing. Where a work is influential *and* unreliable, both are
> stated — that combination is common in this field.

> Selection bias warning: this list is overwhelmingly Anglophone and reflects
> what circulates in the English-language debate. That is itself a distortion.

---

## If you read only three things

| Work | Why | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Hayek, "The Use of Knowledge in Society"** (*AER*, September 1945) | The best short piece of reasoning in economics. Explains why prices work *and*, by implication, exactly where they fail | ~12 pages |
| **Bank of England, "Money creation in the modern economy"** (*Quarterly Bulletin*, 2014 Q1) | The central bank correcting the textbooks on how money is created. Free, plain English | ~14 pages |
| **Keynes, *General Theory*, Chapter 12** ("The State of Long-Term Expectation," 1936) | On uncertainty, convention and speculation. The beauty-contest passage. Better than most of what has been written since | ~15 pages |

## What money is

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **David Graeber, *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011)** | The reason most people know the barter story is wrong. **Caveats required:** specific factual errors (the Apple/IBM passage is simply wrong), disputed readings of Smith and of some Assyriological material, and an explicit political programme. **The core anthropology is right and predates the book by decades** — do not let the book's errors discredit Humphrey's finding |
| **Caroline Humphrey, "Barter and Economic Disintegration" (*Man*, 1985)** | The actual source of the negative result. Less quoted, more reliable |
| **Carl Menger, "On the Origin of Money" (*Economic Journal*, 1892)** | The best statement of the commodity theory. Historically wrong, analytically excellent. Read it to see what a good argument for a false conclusion looks like |
| **G.F. Knapp, *The State Theory of Money* (1905)** | The chartalist source. Heavy going |
| **A. Mitchell Innes, "What Is Money?" (*Banking Law Journal*, 1913)** | Short, sharp, ahead of its time. Keynes reviewed it favourably |
| **Felix Martin, *Money: The Unauthorised Biography* (2013)** | The best single-volume synthesis of the credit view. More careful than Graeber and less known |
| **R.A. Radford, "The Economic Organisation of a P.O.W. Camp" (*Economica*, 1945)** | Six pages. Money, prices, inflation and Gresham's law emerging in a prison camp. A classic, and note the limit of what it proves |
| **Milton Friedman, "The Island of Stone Money" (1991)** | Yap, and the Bank of France's earmarked gold. Friedman arriving at the ledger view from the other direction |
| **Nick Rowe's blog / Perry Mehrling's lectures** | For the hierarchy-of-money framing. Mehrling's *The New Lombard Street* (2011) is the book version |

## How money and banking actually work

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Bank of England (2014) and Bundesbank (April 2017) money creation papers** | The primary sources. Free. Read these before any secondary account |
| **Basil Moore, *Horizontalists and Verticalists* (1988)** | The Post-Keynesian endogenous money argument, made before the central banks conceded it |
| **Walter Bagehot, *Lombard Street* (1873)** | Still the framework for lender-of-last-resort. Note Thornton (1802) got there first |
| **Perry Mehrling, *The New Lombard Street* (2011)** | The modern update. Short and clear |
| **Stephanie Kelton, *The Deficit Myth* (2020)** | The accessible MMT statement. **Read alongside a critic** — Rogoff, Krugman's exchanges with Kelton, or Doug Henwood's critique from the left, which is more interesting than the critiques from the right |
| **Abba Lerner, "Functional Finance and the Federal Debt" (1943)** | MMT's intellectual ancestor, and clearer than most of its descendants |
| **Wynne Godley and Marc Lavoie, *Monetary Economics* (2007)** | Stock-flow consistent modelling. Technical. The source of the sectoral balances apparatus |

## Inflation

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Milton Friedman, "The Role of Monetary Policy" (*AER*, 1968)** | The presidential address that predicted the collapse of the Phillips trade-off before it happened. **One of the best-scored predictions in the discipline** |
| **Thomas Sargent, "The Ends of Four Big Inflations" (1982)** | Why hyperinflations stop abruptly when the fiscal regime changes. The strongest evidence for the fiscal reading |
| **Adam Fergusson, *When Money Dies* (1975)** | Weimar, narrative. Vivid and widely read; **often used to support a monetarist moral the book's own detail undercuts** — the reparations and Ruhr occupation are right there in the text |
| **Rudiger Dornbusch and Stanley Fischer on high inflation** | The technical literature on stabilisation |
| **Bernanke and Blanchard, "What Caused the US Pandemic-Era Inflation?" (2023)** | The most careful post-mortem. Mostly supply first, demand later |
| **Isabella Weber's work on sellers' inflation (2021-)** | The strongest statement of the profit-inflation position. Read with the identity/causation objection in mind |
| **The Boskin Commission Report (1996)** | On CPI measurement bias. Dry, and the foundation of every argument about real wage stagnation |

## Crises

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Charles Kindleberger, *Manias, Panics, and Crashes* (1978)** | The historian's account. Endlessly readable. **Explains everything after the fact and predicts nothing**, which is the genre's limitation |
| **Hyman Minsky, *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* (1986)** | The primary source. Harder going than the secondary literature suggests. Same virtue and same weakness as Kindleberger |
| **Reinhart and Rogoff, *This Time Is Different* (2009)** | The eight-centuries dataset. **Score this separately from their 2010 austerity paper**, which contained a spreadsheet error. Conflating the two is now itself a common mistake |
| **Herndon, Ash and Pollin (2013)** | The replication that found the error. Read as an epistemics case study, not as a refutation of the book |
| **Friedman and Schwartz, *A Monetary History of the United States, 1867-1960* (1963)** | Monumental. The Depression chapters reshaped central banking |
| **Bernanke, "Non-Monetary Effects of the Financial Crisis" (*AER*, 1983)** | The credit-channel complement to Friedman-Schwartz |
| **Barry Eichengreen, *Golden Fetters* (1992)** | The gold standard's role in the Depression. **Among the strongest empirical findings in macro history** |
| **Gary Gorton, *Slapped by the Invisible Hand* (2010)** | 2008 as a run on repo. The most illuminating short account of the mechanism |
| **The FCIC Report (2011)** | The official US inquiry. **Read the dissents** — the commission split 6-4 and could not agree on causes, which is the most honest thing about it |
| **Adam Tooze, *Crashed* (2018)** | The global and political history of 2008 and after. The best single narrative |
| **Admati and Hellwig, *The Bankers' New Clothes* (2013)** | On bank capital. Polemical, and the finance is correct |

## Value and markets

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Hayek (1945)** | See above. Read it |
| **Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), Book I** | Read the actual text, not the slogans. Then read *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) to see what the "Adam Smith problem" is about |
| **Marx, *Capital* Vol. I (1867)** | Chapters 1, 6, 7 and 10 for the core argument. Dense, and the analysis of the working day is powerful regardless of what you think of the price theory |
| **Alfred Marshall, *Principles of Economics* (1890)** | The scissors. Also the source of most of the apparatus in an intro course |
| **Akerlof, "The Market for Lemons" (*QJE*, 1970)** | Rejected three times, then a Nobel. Short |
| **Coase, "The Problem of Social Cost" (1960)** | **Read the whole thing, because everyone quotes the part that isn't his point** |
| **Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990)** | The empirical correction to both market and state orthodoxy. Genuinely surprising |
| **Kahneman and Tversky, "Prospect Theory" (*Econometrica*, 1979)** | The core behavioural result, and one that has replicated |
| **Michael Sandel, *What Money Can't Buy* (2012)** | The best statement of the moral-limits objection. Read with Al Roth's work on repugnant markets as the reply |

## Growth and inequality

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Acemoglu and Robinson, *Why Nations Fail* (2012)** | The institutional thesis, accessibly. **Read Albouy's (2012) critique of the settler-mortality instrument alongside it**, and note that China is a standing anomaly |
| **Jared Diamond, *Guns, Germs, and Steel* (1997)** | The geographic thesis. Excellent on 1500, weak on 2000. Heavily criticised by historians for determinism |
| **Joel Mokyr, *A Culture of Growth* (2017)** | The serious version of the cultural explanation. Far better than the Weber-derived popular versions |
| **Robert Allen, *The British Industrial Revolution in Global Perspective* (2009)** | The high-wage-economy argument. The best price-and-incentive account of why mechanisation happened where it did |
| **Thomas Piketty, *Capital in the Twenty-First Century* (2014)** | **Score the data and the theory separately.** The WID series is a major contribution. Read **Rognlie (2015)** on housing — the strongest critique — and note that "the FT debunked it" is a myth |
| **Branko Milanović, *Global Inequality* (2016)** | The elephant curve and the between-vs-within distinction. Careful about its own limits |
| **Ha-Joon Chang, *Kicking Away the Ladder* (2002)** | On what rich countries actually did while industrialising. Polemical and historically substantive |
| **Dani Rodrik, *Economics Rules* (2015)** | The best account of what economic models are for and how they should be used. Should be required reading before any of the above |

## Correctives — read these against the rest

**This section exists because every book above has a critic worth hearing.**

| For | Read against it |
|---|---|
| Graeber, *Debt* | Reviews by economists and Assyriologists documenting specific errors. Then keep Humphrey's finding, which stands independently |
| Reinhart & Rogoff (2010) | Herndon, Ash and Pollin (2013). Then note the causation is *still* unresolved |
| Piketty | Rognlie (2015) on housing; Acemoglu-Robinson (2015) on institutions. **Not** the FT episode, which he largely rebutted |
| Diamond | Historians' critiques of geographic determinism |
| *The Spirit Level* (Wilkinson & Pickett) | Critiques of country selection and causal inference. Suggestive, overclaimed |
| Any behavioural-economics popular book | The replication literature. **Ego depletion and social priming largely failed; nudge field-trial effects are much smaller than published;** and the 2021-23 data fabrication findings in the honesty subfield were serious |
| Friedman on money supply targeting | The 1979-82 experiment, which the Fed abandoned |
| Austrian business cycle theory | Its own methodological stance, which exempts it from testing |
| MMT | Rogoff and Krugman from the mainstream; Henwood from the left |
| Anyone confident about 2021-23 inflation | The fact that the profession was wrong in both directions within thirteen years |
| **This notebook** | **Everything. None of it was source-verified** |

## Free and high-quality

- **Central bank quarterly bulletins and working papers** — often the clearest
  writing in the field, and free.
- **BIS Annual Economic Report** — the most consistently sceptical
  institutional voice; Borio and White warned before 2008.
- **IMF World Economic Outlook analytical chapters** — the chapters, not the
  forecasts.
- **NBER and CEPR working papers** — pre-publication, so treat as unrefereed.
- **Our World in Data** — for the long-run series, with sources shown.
- **World Inequality Database (WID.world)** — the Piketty-Saez-Zucman data,
  independent of the theoretical apparatus.
