# What We Actually Know — The Confidence Ledger

> **The key file.** Every significant claim in this notebook, sorted by how much
> weight it can bear. Read this before citing anything from the other files,
> including in an argument with yourself.

**The tiers:**

- **TIER 1 — Established.** Multiple independent lines of evidence. Overturning it would require extraordinary evidence.
- **TIER 2 — Contested but tractable.** Serious people disagree; evidence and argument are relevant; progress is possible.
- **TIER 3 — Speculative.** Coherent, unfalsified, unevidenced. Hold loosely.
- **TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed.** No procedure could settle it.

**Standing caveat:** every number below was written from general knowledge with
no live verification. Tier assignments reflect my confidence in the *claim*, not
in my recall of the *citation*. The specific figures most worth checking are
flagged with a dagger (†).

## TIER 1 — Established

| Claim | Notes |
|---|---|
| **No demarcation criterion separates science from non-science** | Falsificationism fails on at least five independent grounds. Laudan (1983) is the standard reference; the field largely agreed |
| **You cannot falsify a hypothesis in isolation** | Duhem (1906), Quine (1951). A failed prediction refutes a conjunction and doesn't tell you which conjunct |
| **Scientists do not follow "the scientific method"** | No historian or sociologist of science defends the schoolroom version. Practice is heterogeneous across and within fields |
| **Publication bias is severe and quantified** | Turner et al. (2008): 22 of 36 negative antidepressant trials unpublished; 11 more published as positive.† The best single measurement in the notebook |
| **Positive results dominate the literature** | Sterling (1959): 97% in psychology journals. Sterling et al. (1995): unchanged 35 years later. Fanelli (2010): 85-90% across fields |
| **Statistical power in the social/behavioural sciences is low and did not improve for 50 years** | Cohen (1962) through Smaldino & McElreath (2016). Median neuroscience power ~21% (Button et al., 2013)† |
| **Analytic flexibility alone produces false positives at high rates** | Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn (2011): four ordinary degrees of freedom push false positives above 60% |
| **The same data analysed by different competent teams yields different conclusions** | Silberzahn et al. (2018): 29 teams, effect estimates 0.89-2.93, 20 of 29 significant. Botvinik-Nezer et al. (2020): 70 fMRI teams, no two identical pipelines |
| **Preregistration dramatically reduces positive-result rates** | Kaplan & Irvin (2015): NHLBI trials 57%→8% around mandatory registration.† Scheel et al. (2021): Registered Reports ~44% positive vs ~96%† |
| **Psychology's replication rate is low** | OSC (2015): 36% of 100. Many Labs 2 (2018): 14 of 28. Camerer (2018): 13 of 21 in Nature/Science social science |
| **Preclinical biomedicine replicates worse than psychology** | RP:CB (2021): effect sizes ~85% smaller; **not one of 193 experiments was repeatable from published methods alone** |
| **Peer review detects deliberately inserted errors poorly** | Multiple error-insertion studies: reviewers find roughly a quarter to a third† |
| **Peer review does not detect fraud** | Zero counterexamples among the major cases. All were caught by outsiders doing forensic work |
| **Reviewer agreement is near chance** | Rothwell & Martyn (2000); Cicchetti (1991) κ ≈ 0.2-0.3; Peters & Ceci (1982): 8 of 9 already-published papers rejected on resubmission |
| **Grant panels do not agree with each other** | Pier et al. (2018), PNAS: no better-than-chance concordance on the same 25 R01 applications |
| **Modern peer review is a post-1970 institution** | *Nature* 1973, *Lancet* 1976. Einstein's 1905 papers were not externally refereed |
| **Most retractions are misconduct, not error** | Fang, Steen & Casadevall (2012): 67.4% of 2,047 retracted biomedical papers attributable to misconduct |
| **Humans reason toward preferred conclusions** | Kunda (1990), Haidt (2001), plus confabulation and choice-blindness evidence. Multiple independent paradigms |
| **Experts are systematically overconfident in prediction** | Tetlock (2005): events called impossible occurred ~15% of the time |
| **Mechanical prediction equals or beats clinical judgement** | Meehl (1954); Grove et al. (2000): ~94% of 136 comparisons. Robust for seventy years and still ignored |
| **Expertise requires valid cues plus fast feedback** | Kahneman & Klein (2009), reached by two researchers with opposite priors |
| **Metrics under pressure become targets and degrade** | Goodhart (1975), Campbell (1979). As close to a law as social science offers |
| **Domain expertise does not transfer** | The Nobel disease. Pauling, Mullis, Montagnier, Shockley, Josephson |
| **Base rate neglect is real and severe in professionals** | Casscells et al. (1978): ~18% of Harvard medical staff got the standard problem right† |
| **Presenting probabilities as natural frequencies substantially improves reasoning** | Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995). **The best debiasing result in the notebook** |
| **Political capture can destroy a scientific field** | Lysenkoism, 1930s-1964. Genetics banned in 1948; Vavilov died in prison, 1943 |
| **Industry funding shaped the dietary fat consensus, undisclosed** | Kearns, Schmidt & Glantz (2016): recovered Sugar Research Foundation correspondence funding the 1967 NEJM review by Hegsted, McGandy and Stare |
| **Negative trial data sat unpublished for decades in nutrition** | Ramsden et al. recovered the Minnesota Coronary Experiment (BMJ, 2016) and Sydney Diet Heart data. Cholesterol fell as predicted; mortality did not |
| **Eugenics was mainstream, funded, peer-reviewed science** | Cold Spring Harbor ERO, Carnegie and Rockefeller funding, Pearson and Fisher, *Buck v. Bell* (1927), ~60,000 US sterilisations |
| **The 1949 Nobel for lobotomy rested on uncontrolled self-assessed outcomes** | Freeman's follow-ups were unblinded, self-conducted, and on self-selected criteria. Never rescinded |

## TIER 2 — Contested but tractable

| Question | State of play |
|---|---|
| **What replaces falsificationism?** | Mayo's severe testing is the strongest live candidate. Lakatos's progressive/degenerating distinction is the best retrospective tool and gives no prospective guidance |
| **Does "science advances one funeral at a time" hold?** | **Split by mechanism.** Hull, Tessner & Diamond (1978): age barely predicted acceptance of Darwinism. Azoulay et al. (2019): outsider entry rises sharply after a star's premature death. Structural gatekeeping, not stubborn belief |
| **How bad is the replication problem outside psychology and preclinical biology?** | **Unmeasured in physics, chemistry, geology, engineering.** Assumed better. Not tested |
| **Is the Open Science Collaboration's 36% an underestimate?** | Gilbert et al. (2016) argued yes, on power and fidelity grounds. Convergence with independent projects argues no. **The criticism has been dismissed more than answered** |
| **Does peer review improve papers?** | Plausible and weakly evidenced. Reporting quality improves; error detection is poor; the cost is ~63 million hours a year† |
| **Is scientific productivity declining?** | Bloom et al. (2020) on ideas-per-researcher is fairly robust; the *interpretation* is not. Park et al. (2023) on disruptiveness is contested as a citation-practice artefact |
| **Do patents promote or retard innovation?** | Genuinely mixed by industry. Strongest case in pharma; weakest in software |
| **Does intelligence protect against myside bias?** | Stanovich says no, and the evidence is decent. This is the most consequential claim in bias-and-reasoning.md |
| **Does motivated numeracy replicate?** | Kahan (2017) is the flagship; replication has been mixed. Direction supported, magnitude uncertain |
| **Which classic biases survive?** | Anchoring, framing, base-rate neglect, sunk cost, overconfidence: yes. Priming, ego depletion, facial feedback, warmth effects, IAT prediction: no or barely |
| **Is Dunning-Kruger a real effect?** | The published graphs are substantially reproducible from random data plus regression to the mean. **Contested** |
| **Is science epistemically privileged over other traditions?** | Defensible on track record; not defensible on method, because there is no method to point at |
| **Are current theories true, or merely empirically adequate?** | Pessimistic meta-induction vs no-miracles argument. Structural realism is the best attempted reconciliation. Unresolved |
| **Did Piltdown actually delay African-origins acceptance?** | Widely stated. Possibly a just-so story constructed after 1953 |
| **Did dietary guidelines cause the obesity epidemic?** | An ecological inference, not a demonstrated one. The revisionist case (Taubes, Teicholz) is overstated — the "Keys cherry-picked 7 of 22 countries" claim misrepresents the Seven Countries Study's design |
| **What should replace saturated fat in the diet?** | Polyunsaturated fat has reasonable trial support; refined carbohydrate does not. **Nutritional epidemiology remains the least reliable major field in this notebook** |
| **What are the COVID-19 origins?** | **Unresolved, and this notebook takes no position.** The *process* failure — a live hypothesis stigmatised via an undisclosed conflict of interest — is much better established than any origin claim |
| **Do open/signed peer review models improve quality?** | *BMJ* has data; results mixed on quality, negative on reviewer willingness |

## TIER 3 — Speculative

Held by serious people. Nobody should be confident.

| Claim | Note |
|---|---|
| **Funding lotteries above a quality bar would outperform panels** | Follows logically from Pier (2018) and Fang (2016). Two small national programmes. **No adequate evaluation exists.** The highest-value untested reform here |
| **Automated forensics (statcheck, GRIM/SPRITE, image analysis) can replace what referees cannot do** | Early results promising, adoption slow, no controlled comparison |
| **Machine-learning research has a large undiagnosed replication problem** | Enormous analytic flexibility, benchmark chasing, no preregistration culture. **The prediction is obvious; the audit has not been run** |
| **The physics preprint model generalises to other fields** | Thirty years of evidence in one field with unusual properties (strong theory, small expert pools, severe reputational cost) |
| **Any metric can be made Goodhart-resistant** | The pessimistic alternative — that rotation of metrics is the only defence — may simply be correct |
| **Reasoning evolved for argument rather than accuracy** | Mercier & Sperber. Explanatorily powerful; hard to distinguish empirically from rival accounts |
| **The undetected fraud rate is around 2%** | Self-report on a career-ending question. Treat as a floor, not an estimate |
| **LLMs will worsen reasoning on identity-loaded questions and improve it on technical ones** | My own guess. **No evidence.** Recorded so it can be checked later |
| **Adversarial collaboration would improve contested literatures if adopted widely** | The successful cases (Kahneman-Klein) are a tiny self-selected sample |

## TIER 4 — Unanswerable in principle, or malformed

| Question | Why it can't be settled |
|---|---|
| **"What fraction of undetected fraud exists?"** | Undetected by construction. Every estimate extrapolates from the caught population, which is a biased sample by definition |
| **"Which of today's consensus beliefs are wrong?"** | If identifiable, they wouldn't be consensus. History says several, and not the ones you would guess |
| **"Which rejected maverick is right?"** | No reliable ex-ante marker exists. The four heuristics in how-theories-die.md are weak and I offer them without confidence |
| **"Is science objective?"** | "Objective" carries at least four incompatible meanings — mind-independent, value-free, intersubjectively checkable, procedurally fair. The answer differs per sense; the question as asked has none |
| **"What is the probability this paper is correct?"** | Requires a prior nobody has. Ioannidis established the *dependence*, not the value |
| **"Would this field have discovered X sooner under different institutions?"** | No counterfactual is observable. All such claims are narrative |
| **"How much has publication bias cost us?"** | Requires knowing the contents of the file drawer |

## The things people most commonly get wrong

Sorted roughly by how often the error appears in public discussion.

| Belief | Reality |
|---|---|
| **"Peer-reviewed means verified"** | Two or three unpaid people read a narrative once, without the data. It is triage, not proof |
| **"The scientific method"** | A schoolroom fiction no historian defends. Astronomy, palaeontology, particle physics and ecology are different epistemic enterprises sharing an ethos |
| **"Science is self-correcting"** | It corrects eventually, under external pressure, unevenly. The candidate-gene literature ran ~20 years. Self-correction is a long-horizon property of the system, never a guarantee about any paper |
| **"Correlation isn't causation, so observational studies are worthless"** | Smoking and lung cancer was established without an RCT, over Fisher's objections. Design carries causal inference, not study type |
| **"The replication crisis was discovered in 2011"** | Documented in 1959 and every decade since. What changed was the incentive to care |
| **"It's a psychology problem"** | Preclinical cancer biology and nutrition epidemiology are worse. Psychology looks bad because it measured itself |
| **"Failed replication means fraud"** | Almost never. Low power plus flexible analysis plus selective publication does it with everyone acting in good faith |
| **"Michelson-Morley disproved the aether"** | It produced a null result absorbed by an auxiliary hypothesis for 18 years |
| **"Wegener was right and everyone else was blind"** | Right about drift, wrong about mechanism; his critics' physics was correct. The field converted within ~5 years of a workable mechanism |
| **"Marshall drank the bacteria and won"** | Memorable, not decisive. Randomised eradication trials were. And Lykoudis had done the therapeutic work twenty years earlier to no effect |
| **"Science advances one funeral at a time"** | Half true, wrong mechanism. Funerals free up positions and funding, not minds |
| **"Phlogiston was stupid"** | It unified combustion, calcination and respiration for eighty years. Judging superseded theories as obviously foolish is the commonest error in popular history of science |
| **"They laughed at Galileo"** | Carries no evidential weight. The base rate for rejected-and-right is low and the denominator is unrecorded |
| **"90% of papers are never cited"** | Myth. In medicine roughly 10-15% are uncited at five years. The real finding is skew, not silence |
| **"Educating people resolves disagreement"** | Kahan: more science literacy produced *more* polarisation on identity-loaded questions |
| **"Smart people are less biased"** | For most biases yes; for myside bias, roughly not at all |
| **"*Thinking, Fast and Slow* is reliable throughout"** | Kahneman conceded the priming chapter rested on underpowered studies |
| **"Implicit bias tests reveal prejudice that drives behaviour"** | r ≈ 0.1 for behaviour; changing scores doesn't change behaviour; the creators say it is unsuitable for individual diagnosis |
| **"Following the science"** | Science gives conditional descriptions. Policy requires value trade-offs. The phrase launders the second as the first |
| **"Rosalind Franklin's data were stolen"** | The archival account (Cobb & Comfort, 2023) is more complicated and better evidenced than the myth |
| **"Open access fixed publishing"** | It moved payment from reader to author, creating an incentive to accept more. Paper mills followed |

## Where the strongest convergences are

When independent methods reach the same conclusion, that is the best signal
available. There are four here.

**1. Incentives, not intentions, determine output quality.** Smaldino &
McElreath's selection model (theory), Kaplan & Irvin's registration effect
(natural experiment), Turner et al.'s FDA comparison (complete-population
audit), and Scheel et al.'s Registered Reports comparison (institutional
change). **Four different methods, one conclusion: change what is rewarded and
the literature changes, without anyone's beliefs changing.**

**2. No individual-level fix works; every structural fix does.** Reviewer
training decayed. Bias education failed. Statistical exhortation failed for
fifty years. Preregistration, blinding, mandatory registration, genome-wide
significance thresholds and mechanical aggregation all worked. **The pattern has
no exceptions in this notebook.**

**3. Verification happens outside the official channel.** Every fraud was caught
by forensic outsiders. Every replication failure was found by consortia, not
referees. Theories died when new instruments arrived (seafloor magnetics,
molecular biology, LIGO), not when arguments improved. **The official
gatekeeping layer is not where error correction lives.**

**4. Expertise is conditional and narrow.** Kahneman and Klein reached it from
opposite priors; Tetlock reached it from forecasting tournaments; Meehl and
Grove reached it from clinical prediction; the Nobel disease demonstrates the
transfer failure. **Four routes, same destination.**

## Where to spend attention

If you have limited time, spend it here rather than there.

| Spend it on | Not on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The prior plausibility of the claim** | The p-value | Ioannidis's whole argument. The prior does most of the work |
| **Was it preregistered?** | Was it peer reviewed? | The evidence gap between these two is enormous and runs the wrong way from public perception |
| **Sample size and power** | Journal prestige | High-prestige journals select for surprise, which correlates with false positives |
| **Effect size and its confidence interval** | Statistical significance | "Significant" tells you almost nothing about magnitude or importance |
| **Has anyone independently replicated it?** | How many times it's been cited | Citation counts measure attention. Retracted papers keep accruing them |
| **Is the claim inside the speaker's domain?** | Their credentials | The single highest-yield check in expertise-and-authority.md |
| **Does the domain support expertise at all?** | The speaker's confidence | Kahneman-Klein conditions. Confidence is a fact about the person |
| **Their track record on scored predictions** | Their reputation | Tetlock: fame is inversely correlated with accuracy |
| **How the consensus formed** | That a consensus exists | Convergence-consensus and inheritance-consensus look identical from outside |
| **Absolute risk, natural frequencies** | Relative risk, percentages | The strongest debiasing result available, and it costs nothing |
| **What would change their mind** | How well they argue | Better reasoners produce better arguments for their existing position |

## The single most useful sentence in the notebook

**Wherever being wrong stops costing anything, knowledge production degrades —
regardless of the credentials of the people involved.**

Everything above is a corollary.
