# The Questions — Disaggregated

> **Status: mixed, by design.** This file contains no claims of its own. It
> sorts the question "how do we know things?" into parts that have different
> answers, different disciplines, and different tiers. **Start here.** Most
> arguments about science are two people answering different sub-questions.

The diagnostic that runs through everything:

**Which discipline could, in principle, settle this — and what would settling
it look like?** If no procedure exists, you are looking at a philosophical
commitment, not a factual dispute, and it matters enormously which.

---

## "What is science?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a criterion separating science from non-science? | Philosophy of science | **Tier 1 (negative).** No. Every proposed criterion fails. See what-is-science.md |
| Do scientists actually follow a method? | History, sociology of science | **Tier 1 (negative).** Not a single one. Practice is heterogeneous |
| Is there nonetheless something that reliably works? | Meta-science | **Tier 2.** Severe testing, prediction of novel facts, and independent replication are the best candidates |
| Is science epistemically privileged over other traditions? | Philosophy | **Tier 2.** Defensible on track record; not defensible on method |
| Are scientific theories *true*, or just useful? | Philosophy (realism debate) | **Tier 2, possibly Tier 4.** Pessimistic meta-induction vs no-miracles argument. Genuinely open |

**The failure mode:** using the absence of a demarcation criterion (row 1) to
argue that astrology and cosmology are epistemically equivalent. Row 3 is where
the actual difference lives.

## "How do wrong ideas die?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Does contrary evidence kill a theory directly? | History of science | **Tier 1 (negative).** Almost never. Auxiliary hypotheses absorb it |
| Can you falsify a single hypothesis in isolation? | Logic (Duhem-Quine) | **Tier 1 (negative).** No. You test a bundle |
| Do scientists change their minds, or just die? | Economics, scientometrics | **Tier 2.** Both. Weak individual-conversion effect; stronger gatekeeping effect |
| How long does a correct rejected idea take? | History | **Tier 2.** Wildly variable: ~12 years (H. pylori) to ~50 (drift) to never (Lykoudis) |
| Can you tell in advance which maverick is right? | — | **Tier 4 as usually asked.** No reliable ex-ante marker exists. See how-theories-die.md |

**The failure mode:** the last row. Every crank believes they are Semmelweis.
Semmelweis was Semmelweis roughly once.

## "How much of the published literature is false?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| What fraction of findings replicate? | Meta-science | **Tier 1 for specific fields.** Psychology ~36-50%; preclinical cancer biology ~10-45%; economics ~60% |
| What fraction are *false* (not merely unreplicated)? | — | **Tier 2, and much harder.** A failed replication is evidence, not proof |
| Why? | Statistics, sociology | **Tier 1.** Low power, publication bias, analytic flexibility, and incentives. Mechanisms are well identified |
| Is it getting better? | Meta-science | **Tier 2.** Yes in registered domains, unclear elsewhere. Volume of low-quality output is rising |
| Does this generalise to physics, chemistry, geology? | — | **Tier 2.** Probably not at the same rate. Tight theory and instrumental precision change everything |

**The failure mode:** "science is broken" as a global claim. The crisis is
concentrated in fields with weak theory, small effects, noisy measurement and
enormous analytic flexibility. That is a specific diagnosis, not a mood.

## "Should I trust this expert?"

Four different questions, routinely collapsed into one:

1. **Is this person competent in their domain?** — Usually checkable. Tier 1 in principle.
2. **Is the claim inside that domain?** — The question people skip. A virologist on virology and a virologist on school policy are not the same epistemic object.
3. **Does the domain support expertise at all?** — Kahneman & Klein's condition: valid cues plus fast feedback. Many prestigious domains fail it.
4. **Are their incentives aligned with telling you the truth?** — Separate from all of the above and often decisive.

**A person can be top of their field, making a claim in their field, in a
domain where expertise is real, and still be wrong because of (4).** All four
are necessary. See expertise-and-authority.md.

## "Why do I believe what I believe?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Do humans reason toward preferred conclusions? | Psychology | **Tier 1.** Robust across paradigms and cultures |
| Does intelligence reduce this? | Psychology | **Tier 2, leaning no.** Myside bias appears near-independent of cognitive ability (Stanovich) |
| Do the classic bias findings replicate? | Meta-science | **Tier 2, mixed.** Anchoring and framing survive; priming, ego depletion, facial feedback largely do not |
| Can you debias yourself? | Psychology | **Tier 2.** Format changes (natural frequencies) work well; willpower-based debiasing works poorly |
| Is reasoning *for* truth at all? | Cognitive science | **Tier 2.** Mercier & Sperber argue reasoning evolved for argument and justification, not accuracy. Serious position |

## "What are the institutions for?"

| Sub-question | Discipline | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Does peer review detect fraud? | Meta-science | **Tier 1 (negative).** It does not, and was never designed to |
| Does peer review improve papers? | Meta-science | **Tier 2, leaning yes, modestly.** Reporting quality improves; error detection is poor |
| Do metrics distort behaviour? | Economics, sociology | **Tier 1.** Goodhart's law is as close to a law as social science gets |
| Does grant peer review identify the best science? | Meta-science | **Tier 2, leaning no.** Panel agreement is near zero above the funding line (Pier 2018) |
| Would alternatives (lotteries, open review) work better? | — | **Tier 3.** Small trials only. Genuinely untested at scale |

## The questions that are actually Tier 4

Not hard. Malformed, or unsettleable in principle.

| Question | Why it can't be settled |
|---|---|
| **"Is science objective?"** | "Objective" is doing four jobs at once: mind-independent, value-free, intersubjectively checkable, procedurally fair. Answer differs per sense |
| **"Is our current physics true?"** | The pessimistic meta-induction (every past theory was superseded) and the no-miracles argument (why else would it work?) are both strong and neither wins |
| **"What is the probability this paper is correct?"** | Requires a prior you don't have. Ioannidis's model shows the *dependence*, not the value |
| **"How much undetected fraud is there?"** | Undetected by definition. All estimates are extrapolations from the caught population — a biased sample by construction |
| **"Which of today's consensus beliefs are wrong?"** | If we could identify them, they wouldn't be consensus. Historically the answer is "several, and not the ones you'd guess" |

## Where the popular story is wrong

| Popular belief | Reality |
|---|---|
| "The scientific method is: hypothesis, experiment, conclusion" | A schoolroom fiction. No historian of science defends it. Real practice is opportunistic, theory-laden and messy |
| "Science is self-correcting" | It corrects *eventually*, under external pressure, and unevenly. The candidate-gene literature ran for two decades. Self-correction is a property of the system over long horizons, not a guarantee about any paper |
| "Peer-reviewed means checked" | It means two or three busy people read it once, usually without the data |
| "Consensus means the question is closed" | Sometimes it means the question is closed. Sometimes it means the field has a shared blind spot. Distinguishing them requires looking at *how* the consensus formed |
| "Following the science" | Science produces conditional descriptions. Policy requires value trade-offs. The phrase smuggles the second past you disguised as the first |
| "Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence" | True as Bayesianism, but note it is a *conservative* rule: it protected the aether and delayed continental drift as reliably as it filters out nonsense |

## Open questions

- [ ] Is there any ex-ante marker distinguishing a correct heterodox claim from a crank one, better than base rate? Best candidates: makes risky novel predictions, engages the strongest counter-evidence, is proposed by someone who accepts the field's other results.
- [ ] Does the replication rate in a field predict anything about that field's practical output? Psychology replicates badly and produced usable results anyway; nutrition replicates badly and arguably did net harm.
- [ ] What is the actual base rate of scientific fraud? Every estimate is downstream of detection.
- [ ] Do the physical sciences have a replication problem that nobody has measured because nobody has run the audit?
- [ ] Is the decline in "disruptive" research (Park et al. 2023) real, or a citation-practice artefact? *(Genuinely contested; see institutions-of-knowledge.md.)*
- [ ] Would large-scale funding lotteries outperform panels? Two small national programmes exist; no adequate evaluation.
