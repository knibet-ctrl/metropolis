# What We Actually Know — The Confidence Ledger

> **The key file.** Every significant claim in this notebook, sorted by how much
> weight it can bear. Read this before citing anything from the other files,
> including in an argument with yourself.

> **Not medical advice.** A tier assignment is a statement about the strength of
> population evidence, not about what you should do. Personal decisions require
> a clinician.

**The tiers:**

- **TIER 1 — Established.** Multiple independent lines of evidence, ideally
  including randomised trials on hard outcomes. Overturning it would require
  extraordinary evidence.
- **TIER 2 — Contested but tractable.** Serious people disagree; evidence and
  argument are relevant; a decisive study is imaginable.
- **TIER 3 — Speculative.** Coherent, unfalsified, unevidenced in humans. Hold
  loosely. **This is where most of the health industry operates.**
- **TIER 4 — Unanswerable in practice, or malformed.** No feasible study could
  settle it, or the question is not an evidence question at all.

**Standing caveat:** every figure below was written from general knowledge with
no live verification, and **medical evidence has a shorter half-life than
anything else in this notebook series.** Tier assignments reflect my confidence
in the *claim*, not in my recall of the *citation*. Figures most worth checking
are flagged with a dagger (†).

## TIER 1 — Established

| Claim | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Smoking causes disease and costs roughly a decade of life** | Doll & Peto, British Doctors Study 1951–2001. Quitting at 40 recovers ~9 of those years; at 30, nearly all |
| **Treating hypertension reduces stroke, cardiovascular events and mortality** | Decades of RCTs. SPRINT (2015): ~25% reduction in the CV composite, ~27% in all-cause mortality vs standard target |
| **Lowering LDL reduces cardiovascular events, proportional to the reduction** | CTT meta-analyses: ~21–22% fewer major vascular events per 1 mmol/L per year†. Triangulated with Mendelian randomisation and PCSK9 trials |
| **Vaccination prevents infectious disease at population scale** | Smallpox eradicated 1980. The largest mortality reductions in history |
| **Physical activity is associated with 20–35% lower all-cause mortality** | Consistent across dozens of cohorts with dose-response. **Causal magnitude is TIER 2** |
| **The exercise dose-response curve flattens** | Arem et al. (2015): ~20% benefit below the guideline minimum, ~39% at 3–5×, no further gain to 10× |
| **Acute sleep restriction impairs cognition, and people underestimate their own impairment** | Van Dongen et al. (2003). Among the most practically important findings in the notebook |
| **CBT-I is the first-line evidence-based treatment for chronic insomnia** | ACP guideline (2016). Effects persist after treatment ends, unlike hypnotics. **Badly under-known** |
| **Antidepressants separate from placebo in randomised trials** | Cipriani et al. (2018): 522 trials, 116,477 participants, all 21 drugs superior. **Magnitude is TIER 2** |
| **Publication bias inflated the published antidepressant literature** | Turner et al. (2008): of 36 negative/questionable FDA trials, 22 unpublished and 11 published as positive |
| **CBT and exposure-based therapies work for depression and anxiety disorders** | Largest evidence base in clinical psychology, with effect sizes inflated by publication bias and waitlist comparators |
| **Named diets perform similarly when adherence is matched** | DIETFITS (2018), n=609: <1 kg difference, not significant. POUNDS LOST (2009), n=811: equivalent |
| **Supplements do not prevent disease in people without deficiency** | VITAL (n=25,871) null for cancer and CVD. USPSTF 2022 insufficient-evidence finding |
| **Beta-carotene supplementation increased lung cancer in smokers** | CARET (stopped early for harm) and ATBC. **The most important supplement result ever obtained** |
| **Antioxidant supplements are associated with increased mortality in several syntheses** | Bjelakovic et al., Cochrane |
| **Folic acid supplementation in pregnancy prevents neural tube defects** | MRC Vitamin Study (1991). **The clearest positive supplement result in existence** |
| **Overdiagnosis is real, large, and demonstrable** | South Korea: thyroid cancer incidence up ~15-fold under ultrasound screening, mortality flat (Ahn, Kim & Welch, *NEJM*, 2014) |
| **Lead-time, length and overdiagnosis bias make 5-year survival a corrupted metric** | Welch et al. (*JAMA*, 2000): no correlation with mortality across 20 tumour types |
| **Medicine reverses ~40% of the established practices it tests** | Prasad et al. (2013): 146 of 363 *NEJM* articles testing existing practice were reversals |
| **Surrogate endpoints have killed people** | CAST (1989): PVC suppression achieved; mortality ~7.7% vs 3.0% placebo†. Torcetrapib, ACCORD, rosiglitazone repeated the error |
| **Observational evidence on lifestyle rarely survives randomisation** | Young & Karr (2011): 52 claims tested in RCTs, **none confirmed**† |
| **Healthy-user and adherer bias are enormous** | Coronary Drug Project (1980): adherent *placebo* patients had roughly half the mortality of non-adherent placebo patients† |
| **Bed rest does not help and often harms** | Allen, Glasziou & Del Mar (*Lancet*, 1999): 39 trials, 15 conditions, no benefit anywhere |
| **Routine episiotomy causes net harm** | Argentine Episiotomy Trial (1993); Hartmann et al. (2005) |
| **Stents do not reduce death or MI in stable angina** | COURAGE (2007), ORBITA (sham-controlled, 2018), ISCHEMIA (2020). **Acute MI is entirely different** |
| **Arthroscopy for knee osteoarthritis is no better than sham surgery** | Moseley (2002), Sihvonen/FIDELITY (2013) |
| **HRT failed as cardiovascular prevention despite strong observational support** | WHI arm stopped 2002. The landmark demonstration of healthy-user bias |
| **Relative risk without a base rate is uninterpretable** | Processed meat: 18% relative increase ≈ 1 extra colorectal case per ~111 people at 50 g/day |
| **NNT depends on baseline risk, not on the drug** | Which is why "statins work" and "statins don't work" can both be true of different populations |
| **Social connection is strongly associated with survival** | Holt-Lunstad et al. (2010): 148 studies, ~50% greater odds. **Observational only** |
| **Alcohol is a Group 1 carcinogen with a monotonic cancer dose-response** | IARC. Independent of the cardiovascular question |
| **Nutritional epidemiology has severe measurement problems** | FFQ correlation with weighed records ~0.4–0.6†; differential under-reporting by weight and health-consciousness |
| **Injury prevention is a major and underweighted lever** | Seatbelts ~45% fatality reduction†; external causes dominate years-of-life-lost under 45 |
| **Resistance training reduces falls and preserves function in older adults** | RCTs. One of the strongest interventions in geriatrics |
| **Lung cancer screening in high-risk smokers reduces mortality** | NLST (2011): ~20% lung-cancer mortality reduction, ~6.7% all-cause. NELSON confirmed |

## TIER 2 — Contested but tractable

| Question | State of play |
|---|---|
| **How much of the exercise–mortality association is causal?** | No RCT is powered for mortality and none will be. Triangulation is good; the magnitude is not established |
| **Does raising VO2max move risk to match the already-fit?** | Mandsager's HR ≈ 0.20 for elite fitness is observational. **The HDL precedent is a warning** |
| **Where does the sodium curve bottom out?** | PURE (J-curve, spot-urine methodology) vs TOHP follow-up (linear benefit) vs SSaSS (~14% stroke reduction with potassium substitute). **Genuinely unresolved** |
| **Is ultra-processed food harmful beyond its calories?** | Hall (2019), n=20, is the only inpatient RCT and it is striking. NOVA may be a proxy bundle rather than a mechanism |
| **How large is the antidepressant effect, clinically?** | Kirsch (~2 HAM-D points, below an arbitrary threshold) vs the heterogeneity rebuttal. Superiority is TIER 1; magnitude is here |
| **Does exercise treat depression?** | Cochrane: SMD ~−0.62 overall, ~−0.18 in low-risk-of-bias trials†. Probably real, smaller than advertised |
| **Does mammography's benefit exceed its harm, with modern imaging and treatment?** | Cochrane vs the Canadian trial vs guideline bodies. **Competent people disagree** |
| **Does PSA screening produce net benefit?** | ERSPC positive with large NNS; PLCO contaminated. Active surveillance and pre-biopsy MRI change the calculus |
| **Does colonoscopy screening reduce mortality?** | NordICC (2022): incidence down 18%, mortality not significantly reduced at 10 years, 42% attendance. Follow-up pending |
| **Do screening programmes reduce all-cause mortality at all?** | Saquib et al. (2015): essentially none demonstrated. Power vs absence of effect is unresolved |
| **Is the sleep–mortality association causal?** | U-shaped; long sleep is almost certainly reverse causation; short sleep partly so |
| **Does the glymphatic clearance story hold?** | Xie (2013) vs a 2024 *Nature* challenge reporting reduced clearance during sleep.† Open |
| **Is sleep regularity more important than duration?** | Recent cohorts suggest yes. Never randomised |
| **Is vaping a net public health gain?** | Almost certainly far less harmful than combustion; plausibly effective for cessation; **no long-term outcome data.** Youth initiation is the live counter-consideration |
| **Does intentional weight loss reduce mortality in the mildly overweight?** | Look AHEAD (2013): no CV event reduction in diabetics despite real weight loss. GLP-1 outcome trials are changing the picture |
| **Is the constrained total energy expenditure model correct?** | Pontzer's Hadza data. **If right, it reframes the whole exercise-for-weight-loss literature** |
| **Is the hallmarks framework causal or descriptive?** | Senescence has the best mouse evidence. For most hallmarks, driver vs consequence is unknown |
| **Does any pharmacological agent slow human ageing?** | Rapamycin has the best animal case and no human lifespan data. Metformin's observational case does not survive MR |
| **Is HRT beneficial when started near menopause?** | The timing hypothesis. KEEPS and ELITE used surrogate endpoints. Symptom treatment is separately justified |
| **What is the population incidence and severity of antidepressant withdrawal?** | Davies & Read's ~56%† came from selected samples; the historical "mild and brief" claim was also unevidenced. **Both sides overstated** |
| **Do psychedelic and ketamine effects survive expectancy?** | Blinding fails almost completely. **Discount accordingly until someone solves it** |
| **Is the rise in reported youth mental illness real?** | Increase vs detection vs threshold change vs prevalence inflation (Foulkes & Andrews). Politically charged, empirically unresolved |
| **Does social media harm adolescent mental health?** | Haidt vs Odgers/Przybylski. Effect sizes in the existing data are small; the natural experiments are weak |
| **Did dietary guidelines contribute to the obesity epidemic?** | Ecological inference. Taubes and Teicholz make the strongest case and it is unproven |
| **What should replace saturated fat?** | Polyunsaturated fat has reasonable trial support; refined carbohydrate does not |
| **How much sedentary time matters independent of exercise?** | Ekelund (2016): ~60–75 min/day activity largely offsets it, except the TV-time residual |
| **Does CPAP reduce cardiovascular events?** | SAVE (2016) null. Adherence is the standard explanation and is not fully satisfying. Symptom benefit is clear |
| **Is there an upper limit where endurance exercise harms?** | Coronary calcification and atrial fibrillation signals in masters athletes; no mortality signal |

## TIER 3 — Speculative

Held by serious people, sold aggressively by others. **Nobody should be
confident, and money is changing hands regardless.**

| Claim | Note |
|---|---|
| **Senolytics benefit humans** | Excellent mouse data (Baker et al.: ~25% median lifespan extension†). Human evidence is a handful of biomarker pilots. **Sold at scale now** |
| **Rapamycin extends human healthspan** | Best animal evidence in the field, including late-life initiation. Zero human lifespan data. Real immunosuppressive risk |
| **Metformin is a geroprotector** | No RCT. TAME unfunded. MR unsupportive. May blunt exercise adaptation |
| **NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR) do anything clinically** | Trials raise the biomarker and show no outcome benefit. **A market built on a measurement** |
| **Resveratrol/sirtuin activation** | The original assays were a fluorophore artefact (Pacholec et al., 2010). GSK abandoned the programme. **Still sold** |
| **Blue Zones represent real longevity clusters with a transferable diet** | **Under serious challenge** (Newman): extreme-age regions correlate with absent birth records, poverty and pension-fraud incentives. Plus selection on the dependent variable in the dietary inference. **TIER 3 and falling** |
| **Biological age clocks are actionable** | Predictive above chronological age; poor test-retest reliability; **never validated as a surrogate endpoint.** Precisely the CAST error |
| **Caloric restriction extends human lifespan** | Primate results conflicted and were partly explained by control-group diet. CALERIE-2 showed biomarker change with real costs |
| **Intermittent fasting has benefits beyond energy restriction** | Trials mostly show equivalence at matched intake |
| **Cold exposure, sauna, breathwork, red light** | Sauna has the best observational data (Finnish cohorts) and no trial. The rest is mechanism and marketing |
| **Telomere lengthening would be beneficial** | MR says longer telomeres raise several cancer risks. **The popular story may have the sign wrong** |
| **Personalised nutrition based on glycaemic response works** | The leading candidate for real personalisation. Evaluated largely by parties selling it |
| **Microbiome modification improves general health** | Real biology, enormous claims, almost no hard-outcome evidence outside *C. difficile* faecal transplant, which genuinely works |
| **Prevalence inflation worsens youth mental health** | Foulkes & Andrews. Coherent, taken seriously, untested |
| **Loneliness is intervenable at scale** | The association is huge; **intervention trials have mostly disappointed**, which is evidence against the naive causal reading |
| **n=1 biohacking protocols produce anything** | No control arm, biomarker endpoints, confounded by wealth. **Cannot become evidence by accumulation** |

## TIER 4 — Unanswerable in practice, or not evidence questions

| Question | Why it doesn't resolve |
|---|---|
| **"What is the optimal diet for humans?"** | Requires a decades-long randomised feeding trial in a free-living population. **It will never be run and no observational design substitutes** |
| **"How long could I live if I did everything right?"** | Counterfactual about one person. No reference class of one |
| **"Is ageing a disease?"** | A definitional and political question wearing a scientific costume. The answer determines regulatory pathways, which is why it is argued about |
| **"How much health is worth how much life?"** | A values question. The quality-adjusted life year is an attempt to operationalise it and is contested precisely because it is a values judgement in arithmetic form |
| **"Is it worth knowing your genetic risk?"** | Depends on what you would do differently and how you handle the knowledge. Not settleable by data about other people |
| **"Should I be screened?"** | Depends on your risk, your values about overdiagnosis, and your tolerance for uncertainty. **This is why guidelines increasingly say "shared decision-making," which is honest and unsatisfying** |
| **"Which of today's practices will reverse?"** | Prasad's 40% is a base rate, not a forecast. Identifying *which* in advance is the whole difficulty |
| **"Is my personal experience with X evidence?"** | For you, it is what you have. As evidence about the world, n=1 unblinded uncontrolled observation with regression to the mean is not a weak study — **it is not a study** |

## Things people most commonly get wrong

**The largest table in the notebook, because the myth surface in this field is
larger than in any other.**

| Belief | Correction |
|---|---|
| "A study found X" is informative | Design determines everything. Observational lifestyle findings almost never survive randomisation |
| Correlation studies with millions of people are reliable | Size fixes noise, not bias. A 500,000-person food questionnaire is a precise estimate of a biased quantity |
| A 50% increase in risk is alarming | On a 0.2% baseline that is 1 extra case per 1,000. **Always ask for the base rate** |
| IARC "Group 1" means as dangerous as tobacco | It classifies **hazard**, not risk. The magnitudes differ by orders of magnitude |
| Dietary fat makes you fat | Refuted isocalorically |
| Dietary cholesterol raises blood cholesterol much | Guidelines dropped the limit in 2015. Most people still avoid yolks |
| Sugar is uniquely fattening beyond its calories | Not shown. Sugar-sweetened *beverages* are the better-supported target |
| There is a best diet | Trials find equivalence at matched adherence, including when personalised by genotype |
| Breakfast is essential | RCTs of adding breakfast found no weight benefit and slightly higher total intake (*BMJ*, 2019) |
| Superfoods, detoxes, cleanses, alkaline diets | No mechanism, no evidence. Marketing categories |
| Non-coeliac gluten sensitivity is widespread | Largely failed blinded re-challenge. Coeliac disease (~1%) is entirely real and entirely different |
| 10,000 steps is a target | The name of a 1965 Japanese pedometer. Benefit plateaus at 6,000–8,000 for older adults |
| The fat-burning zone burns more fat | Higher *proportion*, lower total. Irrelevant to fat loss |
| HIIT is dramatically superior | Comparable per unit of work. Adherence differentiates, not physiology |
| Exercise is how you lose weight | Modest effect alone; compensation is substantial. **It is how you improve health at any weight** |
| Sitting is the new smoking | Smoking costs ~10 years. The comparison is not close and activity largely offsets sitting |
| Older people should avoid lifting | Backwards. Progressive resistance training is among the best geriatric interventions |
| Lactic acid causes soreness | Lactate clears in an hour. DOMS is mechanical damage. Known since the 1980s |
| Everyone needs 8 hours | Mortality nadir near 7h; individual variation is wide; genuine short-sleep genotypes are rare |
| A bad night damages you | It is normal. **Worrying about it is the mechanism that turns acute insomnia chronic** |
| Blue light from phones is a major disruptor | Small at real-world brightness; blue-blocker trials mixed. The content matters more than the wavelength |
| Sleep trackers measure sleep stages | Poorly. Orthosomnia is a documented clinical consequence |
| Alcohol helps you sleep | Shortens onset, degrades architecture, fragments the second half |
| Moderate drinking protects your heart | The J-curve is largely sick-quitter bias. MR does not support cardioprotection |
| "Chemical imbalance" is psychiatry's theory | A marketing simplification. Academic psychiatry moved on and **should have said so publicly far sooner** |
| The serotonin review showed antidepressants don't work | It made no such claim. **Mechanism ≠ efficacy.** Cipriani (2018) stands |
| Antidepressants are placebos | They separate from placebo in the full regulatory dataset. The honest debate is about magnitude |
| Therapy has no side effects | Adverse effects occur and are systematically under-measured |
| Telomeres are your biological clock | Weak predictor; genetically longer telomeres raise several cancer risks |
| Blue Zones prove a longevity diet | The age data is under serious challenge, and the dietary inference selected on the outcome |
| Biological age tests tell you how you're ageing | Poor reliability, no validated responsiveness to intervention |
| Supplements are a safe bet | Beta-carotene increased lung cancer in smokers. **"Probably useless" is not the same as "harmless"** |
| Vitamin D deficiency causes everything | Reverse causation. VITAL (n=25,871) supplemented and found nothing for cancer or CVD |
| Caught early is always better | Only if treatment changes the outcome. For overdiagnosed disease, detection is pure harm |
| 5-year survival measures care quality | It measures diagnostic intensity. **No correlation with mortality across 20 cancers** |
| More screening is better | Screening has harms and several programmes have not demonstrated mortality benefit |
| Medical error is the third leading cause of death | A poorly supported extrapolation, endlessly repeated |
| Medicine is why we live longer | Sanitation, nutrition, housing, income and vaccination did most of the historical work |
| Genetics determines your lifespan | Twin studies attribute roughly 20–30% of lifespan variance to genetics†, possibly less with assortative-mating corrections |
| Reversals mean medicine can't be trusted | **Medicine found the reversals.** The problem is the adoption threshold, not the method |

## Where the strongest convergences are

**These are the places where independent methods agree, which is the only real
protection against method-specific bias:**

| Convergence | Lines of evidence |
|---|---|
| **LDL → cardiovascular disease** | RCTs (statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9) + Mendelian randomisation + familial hypercholesterolaemia natural experiment. **The best-triangulated causal claim in chronic disease** |
| **Blood pressure → stroke and CVD** | RCTs + MR + dose-response + mechanism |
| **Smoking → mortality** | Cohorts + dose-response + mechanism + quitter natural experiment + effect size no confounder could produce |
| **Alcohol → no cardioprotection** | Corrected meta-analysis + MR (ALDH2) + failure of the trial that would have tested it |
| **HDL → not a target** | MR + three failed CETP inhibitor programmes |
| **Vitamin D → not causal for most outcomes** | Large RCTs + MR + a clear reverse-causation mechanism |
| **Overdiagnosis is real** | Natural experiments (South Korea thyroid, Japanese neuroblastoma) + autopsy studies + incidence/mortality divergence |
| **Surrogate endpoints are unreliable** | CAST + torcetrapib + ACCORD + rosiglitazone. **Four independent replications of the same failure mode** |

## Where to spend attention

**The honest and unwelcome summary.**

1. **The TIER 1 list is about ten items long and you already know all of them.**
   Not smoking, movement, sleep, blood pressure, not crashing, vaccination,
   little or no alcohol, treating what needs treating, and other people.
2. **The marginal value of additional health information, past that list, is
   close to zero and possibly negative.** Past a point, more information
   produces more anxiety, more screening, more false positives, and more
   overtreatment — with no improvement in outcome.
3. **Almost the entire industry operates in Tiers 2 and 3.** Supplements,
   protocols, wearables, biological age tests, longevity clinics, personalised
   nutrition. **That is where the money is precisely because that is where the
   uncertainty is** — you cannot sell a certainty that is free.
4. **The highest-value single action for most people is not on any list here:
   have a clinician you can actually see.** Every item on the TIER 1 list that
   requires action — blood pressure, lipids, vaccination, treatable conditions,
   symptoms that need investigating — routes through that.
5. **Do not export this notebook's scepticism uniformly.** The correct posture
   toward a supplement advertisement and toward a vaccination schedule are
   opposite. **Calibration means updating in both directions, and a reader who
   takes only the scepticism away has been harmed by this file.**

**The tension I cannot resolve:** this notebook argues both that most health
claims are overstated and that a small set is extremely well established. Those
are consistent as evidence and in tension as tone. **If you find yourself more
sceptical of blood pressure treatment after reading it, I have failed and you
should discard the tone and keep the tiers.**

## Open questions across the whole notebook

- [ ] **What fraction of today's TIER 1 will be TIER 2 in twenty years?** Prasad's 40% reversal rate applies to tested practices. **The untested majority has no error bar at all**, and this governs how much weight anything above deserves.
- [ ] **Can nutritional epidemiology be repaired, or must it be replaced?** Ioannidis versus the field. The single most consequential methodological dispute here, because it determines whether a whole literature counts as evidence.
- [ ] **Has morbidity compressed or expanded?** Fries (1980) versus the expansion hypothesis. Determines whether medicine's successes have been net good for how people actually live.
- [ ] Is the physical-activity mortality association causal at the observed magnitude? The largest item on the TIER 1 list rests on evidence that will never be randomised.
- [ ] Can overdiagnosed cancers be distinguished from lethal ones at the point of diagnosis? **Solving this resolves most of the screening debate at once.**
- [ ] Why do average treatment effects in mental health stay small when clinical experience reports strong individual responses? Diagnostic heterogeneity is the leading hypothesis and is unproven.
- [ ] What is the current accepted surrogate endpoint that will turn out to be PVC suppression? Candidates: biological age clocks, amyloid burden, some inflammatory markers.
- [ ] Does sceptical health writing reduce trust in the well-evidenced core, causing net harm? **The strongest objection to this notebook, and I have no answer to it.**
- [ ] Would raising the evidentiary bar for adopting new practices cost more lives through delay than it saves through avoided reversals? Never quantified, and it is the best argument against the whole sceptical programme.
