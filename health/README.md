NOT MEDICAL ADVICE — READ THIS FIRST
===============================================================================
**This notebook is a map of evidence quality. It is not medical advice, it is
not a treatment plan, and it is not a substitute for a clinician who knows your
history.**

  - **No drug is recommended here. No dose is given here.**
  - **Nothing here should be used as a reason to stop, start, reduce or skip a
    prescribed treatment.** Stopping some medications abruptly is dangerous.
    That decision belongs with the person who prescribed it.
  - Population averages do not tell you what happens to you. A treatment with
    no average benefit can be the right treatment for a specific patient, and
    vice versa.
  - **If you are in immediate danger — chest pain, stroke symptoms, thoughts of
    suicide, an overdose — that is an emergency, not a research question.**
    Call your local emergency number. Crisis lines: 988 (US/Canada),
    116 123 Samaritans (UK/Ireland), 000/13 11 14 (Australia).

**Second warning, structural.** Medical evidence changes, and it changes in this
field faster than in any other notebook here. Several claims below were reversed
within the last twenty years, and several current claims will be reversed within
the next twenty. See the-control-group.md, which is the most important file for
calibrating how much anything here is worth.

===============================================================================
WHAT THIS IS
===============================================================================
An honest map of what is actually known about keeping a human body alive and
functioning well.

  What genuinely extends life? What genuinely improves how you feel?
  Which of those are the same thing, and where do they conflict?
  Why is nutrition science so bad? Why does medicine keep reversing itself?
  What is worth money, and what is a story with a price tag attached?

Written in the same discipline as leaders/, existence/, knowledge/, risk/ and
civilisations/: positions given fairly, disputes flagged as disputes, confidence
marked by tier, arithmetic shown, and a standing refusal to pretend something is
settled when it isn't.

**No live sources were used. This was written from general knowledge with no
verification against primary documents, and medical evidence has a short
half-life.** Names, dates, trial acronyms and effect sizes are given because
vague health claims are unfalsifiable and therefore useless — but every specific
number here should be checked before you cite it, and several are from memory
and may be off. Where a figure is load-bearing, I say so. **Figures I am least
confident about are flagged with a dagger (†).**

===============================================================================
THE ONE IDEA THAT ORGANISES EVERYTHING
===============================================================================
**Almost every health claim you will encounter is an observational correlation
dressed as a causal fact, and the base rate for such claims surviving a
randomised trial is close to zero.**

Young & Karr's 2011 review found **52 claims from observational studies that
were subsequently tested in randomised trials. None was confirmed in the
direction claimed. Some reversed.†** That is the single most useful number in
this notebook.

Three consequences:

  1. **SORT BY DESIGN, NOT BY CONCLUSION.** "A study found" is not information.
     Randomised, blinded, hard-outcome, pre-registered, adequately powered — or
     not. -> evidence-hierarchy.md

  2. **THE THINGS THAT SURVIVE ARE BORING, FEW, AND MOSTLY FREE.** Don't smoke.
     Move. Sleep. Control blood pressure. Don't get hit by cars. The complete
     TIER 1 list fits on a postcard. -> what-actually-works.md

  3. **THE INDUSTRY IS BUILT ALMOST ENTIRELY ON TIERS 2 AND 3.** Supplements,
     protocols, biohacking, superfoods, most wellness. That is not a conspiracy
     claim; it is an economic observation. **You cannot build a business on a
     postcard.** -> practices.md, what-we-know.md

HEADLINE FINDINGS (compressed; each contestable; tier marked)
===============================================================================
 1. **Not smoking is worth roughly ten years of life, and nothing else on the
    list is close.** TIER 1. Doll & Peto's British Doctors Study followed ~34,000
    doctors from 1951 to 2001. Lifelong smokers lost about a decade. **Quitting
    at 40 recovers about nine of those years; quitting at 30 recovers nearly all
    of them.** No supplement, diet or protocol has ever demonstrated anything of
    this magnitude.

 2. **Observational nutrition and lifestyle findings almost never survive a
    randomised trial.** TIER 1. Beta-carotene *increased* lung cancer in smokers
    (CARET, ATBC). Vitamin E did nothing. Vitamin D did nothing for cancer or
    cardiovascular events (VITAL, 25,871 people). HRT reversed outright. The
    pattern is not a series of accidents; it is what confounding does.

 3. **Cardiorespiratory fitness is one of the strongest mortality predictors
    ever measured, stronger than smoking status in some cohorts.** TIER 1 as
    association, TIER 2 as a causal claim about training. Mandsager et al.
    (2018), 122,007 treadmill tests — elite versus low fitness, adjusted hazard
    ratio around 0.20.† The observational-to-causal gap here is real and I flag
    it rather than eliding it.

 4. **The exercise dose-response curve is steepest at the start.** TIER 1.
    Going from nothing to a little buys most of the available benefit; going
    from a lot to more buys almost none. The marginal return on your first
    twenty minutes a week exceeds the marginal return on your two-hundredth.

 5. **Most named diets perform the same in trials once adherence is matched.**
    TIER 1 for weight. DIETFITS (Gardner, 2018): 609 people, healthy low-fat
    versus healthy low-carb, twelve months, mean difference under a kilogram
    and not significant. Genotype and insulin-secretion matching did not help.
    **The diet wars are largely a fight about a null result.**

 6. **Ultra-processed food has one genuinely strong piece of evidence and it is
    small.** TIER 2. Hall et al. (2019): twenty inpatients, metabolic ward,
    matched macronutrients, ad libitum eating. The ultra-processed arm ate about
    500 kcal/day more and gained weight; the whole-food arm lost it. n=20 is
    n=20 — but it is the only inpatient randomised trial in the entire debate.

 7. **Medicine reverses about 40% of the established practices it bothers to
    test.** TIER 1. Prasad et al. (2013) reviewed a decade of *NEJM*: of 363
    articles testing an existing practice, **146 (40.2%) were reversals.** The
    denominator is practices that got tested at all — most never are.

 8. **"Caught early" is not automatically better, and 5-year survival is a
    corrupted metric.** TIER 1. Lead-time bias, length bias and overdiagnosis
    each inflate survival without saving anyone. Welch et al. (2000) found **no
    correlation between changes in 5-year survival and changes in mortality
    across 20 tumour types.** South Korea's thyroid cancer incidence rose about
    fifteen-fold under ultrasound screening with **flat mortality.**

 9. **The alcohol J-curve is mostly an artefact.** TIER 2, moving toward TIER 1.
    "Abstainers" in old cohorts included sick people who had quit. Correcting
    for that (Stockwell et al., 2016) removes the protective effect, and
    Mendelian randomisation does not support cardioprotection either. Alcohol is
    an IARC Group 1 carcinogen. **The moderate-drinking-is-good-for-you result
    is the cleanest example of healthy-user bias in the notebook.**

10. **The highest-value interventions are free or nearly free, and this is
    commercially inconvenient.** TIER 1. Walking, sleep, not smoking, blood
    pressure measurement, seatbelts, vaccination, and other people. **There is
    no revenue model in a postcard, which is why the postcard is not what you
    hear about.**

===============================================================================
HONEST CAVEATS
===============================================================================
- **No sources were checked.** Trial names, years and effect sizes were
  reconstructed from memory. Treat every number as approximately right, verify
  before use, and be aware that some may be wrong in detail.
- **This field moves.** Anything described here as current was current as of my
  general knowledge, not as of the date on this file.
- **Selection bias in what I remember.** Famous trials are famous partly because
  their results were surprising, which means this notebook over-represents
  reversals and under-represents the boring confirmations that never made news.
  That cuts against the notebook's own thesis and I flag it.
- **Anglophone and rich-country bias throughout.** Almost every trial cited was
  run in North America or Western Europe. Applicability elsewhere is assumed,
  not demonstrated.
- **This notebook leans sceptical.** Scepticism is the right prior for wellness
  claims and the wrong prior for, say, vaccination or blood pressure treatment,
  where the evidence is excellent. **Do not export the tone from nutrition.md to
  what-actually-works.md.** Calibration means updating in both directions.
- **Population evidence does not settle individual cases.** This is the single
  most common misuse of a file like this.
- **Nothing here is a diagnosis, and no reader's situation is known to me.**
