# The Control Group — Medical Reversals

> **Status: TIER 1** on the facts of each case and on the existence of a high
> reversal rate. **TIER 2** on the mechanism attributions — these are
> reconstructions, and the histories are written by people with positions.

> **Not medical advice.** Several treatments below are still in appropriate use
> for appropriate indications. **A reversal usually narrows an indication rather
> than abolishing a drug.** Nothing here is a reason to change your treatment.

> **Why this file exists.** A notebook that catalogues only what medicine got
> right is a survivorship machine. **The reversals are the empirical proof of
> everything argued in evidence-hierarchy.md** — they are what happens when
> observational data, mechanistic reasoning and surrogate endpoints are trusted,
> and they have a body count.

> **The finding that runs through nearly every case: the practice was adopted on
> a mechanism that made sense, spread on observational data or a surrogate
> endpoint, and was overturned only when someone finally ran a randomised trial
> — often decades and many thousands of deaths later.**

---

## The base rate

**Prasad et al. (*Mayo Clinic Proceedings*, 2013):** reviewed **ten years of
the *New England Journal of Medicine*.** Of **363 articles testing an
established practice, 146 (40.2%) were reversals** — the trial showed the
existing practice was no better than, or worse than, a lesser alternative.

**Herrera-Perez et al. (*eLife*, 2019)** extended this across three journals and
identified **396 reversals.**

**The denominator matters enormously: this is 40% of the practices that someone
bothered to test.** Most established practice is never subjected to a randomised
trial at all, and there is no reason to think the untested majority is better
supported.

**Prasad & Cifu, *Ending Medical Reversal* (2015)**, is the standard reference.

## Hormone replacement therapy and the WHI — the landmark case

**The purest demonstration in medicine that observational data and randomised
data can disagree completely.**

**The observational era.** Through the 1980s and 1990s, cohort studies —
principally the **Nurses' Health Study** — reported that postmenopausal women
using hormone therapy had roughly **40–50% lower coronary heart disease
incidence.** The mechanism was plausible: oestrogen improves lipid profiles and
endothelial function. By the late 1990s HRT was being prescribed for
**cardiovascular prevention**, not only for symptoms.

**The trial.** The **Women's Health Initiative**, a large randomised
placebo-controlled trial. In **July 2002** the oestrogen-plus-progestin arm was
**stopped early** because harms exceeded benefits: increased breast cancer,
stroke, venous thromboembolism, and — the opposite of the entire rationale —
**increased coronary heart disease** (HR ≈ 1.29†).

**The mechanisms of error, in order of importance:**

| Mechanism | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Healthy-user bias** | Women prescribed HRT in the 1980s were wealthier, better educated, thinner, more likely to exercise, and had better healthcare access. **They were destined for lower CHD rates whatever they took** |
| **Adherent-user bias** | Compare the Coronary Drug Project placebo result in evidence-hierarchy.md. Adherence alone is worth a large mortality difference |
| **Prevalent-user bias** | Cohorts enrolled women already taking HRT — meaning women who had tolerated it. Those who had adverse events early had already stopped and were invisible |
| **Mechanistic seduction** | The lipid story was correct at the level of the biomarker and wrong at the level of the outcome |
| **Confusion of indications** | Symptom relief and disease prevention are different claims requiring different evidence |

**The complication that must be included, because the story is usually told too
simply.** The WHI enrolled women averaging around 63 years — a decade or more
past menopause. The **timing hypothesis** proposes that hormone therapy started
near menopause has a different risk profile from therapy started later, and
subsequent trials (**KEEPS**, **ELITE**) examined surrogate measures with more
favourable results.

**Consequently:** HRT for symptom control in appropriately selected women near
menopause is a legitimate, evidence-informed clinical decision today. **What was
reversed was HRT as cardiovascular prevention for the general postmenopausal
population.** Reporting the reversal as "HRT is dangerous" produced its own harm
— a generation of women denied effective symptom treatment. **The reversal was
over-corrected, and that is itself part of the lesson.**

## CAST — the surrogate endpoint case that should have ended the practice forever

**Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial**, *NEJM*, **1989**.

**The reasoning, which was excellent:** premature ventricular contractions after
myocardial infarction predict sudden cardiac death. Encainide and flecainide
suppress PVCs effectively. Therefore they should reduce sudden death.

**Every step of that chain was true except the conclusion.**

**The result:** the trial was stopped early. Mortality in the treated arms was
approximately **7.7% versus 3.0% on placebo**†. **The drugs suppressed the
arrhythmia and killed the patients.**

**Estimates of the death toll from the practice before the trial run into the
tens of thousands in the US alone.** Thomas Moore's *Deadly Medicine* (1995) is
the popular account.

**Why this is the most important case in the file:**

- The surrogate was **validated as a prognostic marker.** It genuinely predicted
  death. It was still the wrong target.
- **A marker of risk is not a mechanism of risk.** PVCs signalled a damaged
  myocardium; suppressing the signal did nothing about the damage and the drugs
  were themselves proarrhythmic.
- **Nobody had to guess.** The trial was run, over objections that withholding
  an obviously beneficial drug was unethical.

**Torcetrapib (raised HDL, increased mortality), rosiglitazone (improved HbA1c,
cardiovascular signal), and ACCORD's intensive glucose arm (better HbA1c,
increased mortality) are the same error repeated after CAST.** The lesson has to
be relearned every decade.

## The lobotomy Nobel

**António Egas Moniz was awarded the 1949 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine**
for prefrontal leucotomy.

**Walter Freeman** popularised the transorbital "ice pick" variant in the US,
performing thousands personally, sometimes in non-surgical settings and without
a surgical team. Estimates run to **40,000–50,000 US procedures.†**

**The mechanism of error:**

| Failure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Outcome assessed by the operator** | Freeman's follow-ups were unblinded, self-conducted, and used criteria he selected |
| **No control group** | Institutional psychiatric populations have variable courses; spontaneous improvement was attributed to surgery |
| **Outcome measure was institutional convenience** | "Improvement" frequently meant a patient became manageable — a real effect of the operation and not a benefit to the patient |
| **Irreversibility** | Unlike a drug, the intervention could not be withdrawn when the assessment changed |
| **Institutional incentives** | Overcrowded asylums with no alternatives, and enormous pressure for any intervention at all |

**The Nobel has never been rescinded.** See knowledge/expertise-and-authority.md.

## Thalidomide — the case that built the regulatory system

Marketed by Chemie Grünenthal from **1957** as a sedative, including for
morning sickness in pregnancy. Withdrawn **1961** after **William McBride** and
**Widukind Lenz** independently linked it to phocomelia. **Roughly 10,000
affected infants across ~46 countries.**

**Frances Oldham Kelsey**, a reviewer at the FDA, **repeatedly refused US
approval** on the grounds of inadequate safety data. She received the President's
Award for Distinguished Federal Civilian Service in 1962.

**Consequences:** the **Kefauver-Harris Amendments (1962)** required
manufacturers to demonstrate **efficacy** as well as safety, and established the
modern trial-based approval architecture.

**The mechanism of error:** no requirement for pre-market efficacy or
teratogenicity testing; animal testing that did not use species susceptible to
the effect; and marketing that outran the evidence base by years.

**The genuinely uncomfortable epilogue:** thalidomide is an effective treatment
for multiple myeloma and erythema nodosum leprosum, and is used today under
extremely strict pregnancy-prevention controls. **The drug was not evil; the
indication was catastrophic. This is what "reversal narrows an indication"
means.**

## Bed rest

**Allen, Glasziou & Del Mar (*The Lancet*, 1999)** systematically reviewed
**39 randomised trials of bed rest across 15 conditions**, including back pain,
uncomplicated myocardial infarction, hepatitis, pregnancy complications, and
post-lumbar-puncture headache.

**Bed rest improved outcomes in none of them, and worsened outcomes in
several.**

**The mechanism of error is pure mechanistic reasoning:** rest allows healing;
therefore more rest allows more healing. **It was standard practice for most of
a century without anyone testing it**, and the harms — deconditioning, venous
thromboembolism, pneumonia, pressure injury, delirium, loss of independence —
were invisible because they were attributed to the underlying illness.

**Early mobilisation is now standard in most of these conditions.** The
reversal was complete and is rarely taught as a reversal.

## Routine episiotomy

Routine surgical incision during vaginal delivery, on the theory that a
controlled cut prevents uncontrolled severe tearing and protects pelvic floor
function.

**The Argentine Episiotomy Trial (1993)** and later syntheses — including
**Hartmann et al. (*JAMA*, 2005)** — found **routine episiotomy produced no
benefit and increased severe perineal trauma, pain and healing complications.**

**Rates fell dramatically in most high-income countries.** Selective episiotomy
for specific indications remains appropriate.

**The mechanism of error:** surgical mechanistic reasoning, tradition, and an
absence of any randomised comparison for roughly seventy years in a procedure
performed on a majority of deliveries in some settings. **Obstetric practice is
disproportionately represented among reversals, and the historical reasons for
that are worth their own file.**

## Vioxx

**Rofecoxib**, a COX-2 selective anti-inflammatory, approved **1999**, withdrawn
**September 2004**.

| Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| **VIGOR (2000)** | Rofecoxib versus naproxen. Myocardial infarction rate roughly **five-fold higher** on rofecoxib. **Interpreted publicly as naproxen being cardioprotective**, rather than rofecoxib being harmful |
| **APPROVe (2004)** | Placebo-controlled trial for adenoma prevention confirmed increased cardiovascular events. Withdrawal followed |
| **Scale** | **Graham et al. (*The Lancet*, 2005)** estimated on the order of **88,000–140,000 excess serious coronary events in the US** |
| **Litigation** | Merck settled for approximately $4.85bn in 2007 |

**Mechanisms of error:** a plausible alternative interpretation preferred over
the unfavourable one; a mechanistic rationale (COX-2 selectivity sparing the
stomach) that distracted from an unmeasured pathway (prostacyclin/thromboxane
imbalance); trial data disclosure controversies; and enormous direct-to-consumer
marketing that built a user base before the safety question resolved.

## Opioids and "pain as the fifth vital sign"

**The most consequential reversal in this file, because the mechanism was a
metric.**

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1980** | **Porter & Jick**, a **one-paragraph letter** to the *NEJM* reporting low addiction rates among **hospitalised** patients given opioids in a monitored setting |
| **The citation cascade** | **Leung et al. (*NEJM*, 2017)** analysed its citation history: cited **hundreds of times**, overwhelmingly uncritically, and repeatedly characterised as evidence that opioids were rarely addictive **in chronic outpatient use** — a population it did not study |
| **1996** | Purdue launches OxyContin, marketed on a delayed-release formulation claimed to reduce abuse potential |
| **1996–2001** | The American Pain Society promotes **"pain as the fifth vital sign"**; adopted by the VA (1999) and reinforced by Joint Commission standards (2001) |
| **The Goodhart step** | Pain scores became a **measured, audited, and eventually reimbursement-linked target.** Institutions optimised the score. **Prescribing followed the metric** |
| **Consequences** | **More than 500,000 US overdose deaths involving opioids between 1999 and 2019**; a transition to heroin and then illicit fentanyl |
| **Accountability** | Purdue pleaded guilty to misbranding in 2007 and to federal charges in 2020; Sackler-related settlements followed |
| **The over-correction** | Abrupt prescribing restrictions and forced tapers caused documented harm to existing chronic pain patients, including suicides. **The correction produced its own casualties** |

**This case belongs equally in risk/ and knowledge/.** It is Goodhart's law
(risk/risk-management.md), a citation cascade (knowledge/how-theories-die.md),
and regulatory capture, operating together on a population scale.

## Stents for stable angina

| Trial | Result |
|---|---|
| **COURAGE (2007)** | PCI plus optimal medical therapy versus optimal medical therapy alone in stable coronary disease: **no reduction in death or myocardial infarction** |
| **ORBITA (Al-Lamee et al., *The Lancet*, 2018)** | **Sham-controlled.** 200 patients, all received the catheterisation procedure; only some received the stent. **No significant difference in exercise-time improvement.** The first sham-controlled trial of PCI |
| **ISCHEMIA (2020)** | 5,179 patients with moderate-to-severe ischaemia: **no reduction in the primary composite** with an invasive strategy; some angina-symptom benefit |

**Mechanism of error:** an overwhelmingly persuasive mechanical story — a
narrowed pipe, widened. Symptom improvement was real and was attributed entirely
to the mechanism, with **no sham control** to separate the procedure from the
placebo response and natural history. **ORBITA was ethically controversial
precisely because everyone knew the answer, which is the standard condition
under which reversals are discovered.**

**Essential distinction: PCI in acute myocardial infarction is genuinely
lifesaving and is not part of this reversal.** Applying the stable-angina result
to acute care would kill people.

## Arthroscopic surgery for knee osteoarthritis

| Trial | Result |
|---|---|
| **Moseley et al. (*NEJM*, 2002)** | 180 patients, **sham surgery control** including skin incisions. Arthroscopic lavage and debridement were **no better than placebo surgery** at any point over two years |
| **Sihvonen et al. (FIDELITY, *NEJM*, 2013)** | **Sham-controlled** partial meniscectomy for degenerative meniscal tear without osteoarthritis: **no benefit over sham** |
| **Adoption of the finding** | Slow. The procedure continued at high volume for years afterwards in many countries |

**Mechanism of error:** visible pathology on imaging assumed to be the pain
generator (degenerative meniscal tears are extremely common in **asymptomatic**
people), plus surgery's exceptionally strong placebo response, plus the absence
of any regulatory requirement for procedures equivalent to drug approval.

**The structural point:** **a new drug requires randomised evidence of efficacy;
a new surgical procedure generally does not.** When sham-controlled surgical
trials are run, they fail at a striking rate — arthroscopy, vertebroplasty
(Buchbinder and Kallmes, two independent sham trials, *NEJM*, 2009), and stents.
**There is no reason to expect the untested procedures are different.**

## What the reversals have in common

| Mechanism | Cases |
|---|---|
| **Surrogate endpoint trusted** | CAST, torcetrapib, ACCORD, rosiglitazone |
| **Observational data trusted (healthy-user bias)** | HRT, antioxidants, vitamin D, moderate alcohol |
| **Mechanistic plausibility trusted** | Bed rest, episiotomy, dietary cholesterol, stents |
| **No sham control in a procedure** | Arthroscopy, vertebroplasty, PCI for stable angina |
| **Outcome assessed by the enthusiast** | Lobotomy |
| **Metric became a target** | Opioids/fifth vital sign |
| **Financial incentive aligned with adoption** | Vioxx, opioids, stents, HRT |
| **Correction over-shot and caused new harm** | HRT (symptom treatment withdrawn), opioids (forced tapers) |

**And the one that is not a reversal but belongs here as a control on the
control:** **Barry Marshall and Robin Warren** on *Helicobacter pylori* and
peptic ulcer. The establishment was confidently wrong, an outsider was right,
Marshall drank the culture himself, and they received the Nobel in 2005.
**Sometimes the heterodox position is correct.** This is the case that stops the
file being an argument for reflexive contrarianism — which fails far more often
than it succeeds, and whose failures are simply less memorable.

## Where the popular story is wrong

| Popular belief | Reality |
|---|---|
| Medicine is self-correcting | It corrects, slowly, when someone funds a trial. **Most established practice is never tested at all** |
| Reversals prove medicine can't be trusted | They prove the *method* works and that the *adoption threshold* is too low. The reversals were found by medicine, not by its critics |
| A reversal means the treatment is useless | Usually it narrows an indication. Thalidomide treats myeloma. HRT treats symptoms. PCI saves lives in acute MI |
| Big scandals are about fraud | Mostly they are about **honest reasoning from plausible mechanisms and bad endpoints.** CAST involved no villains |
| Surgery is held to a high evidential standard | Drugs require randomised efficacy evidence. **Procedures largely do not.** When sham trials are run they fail often |
| The WHI showed HRT is dangerous | It showed HRT failed as cardiovascular *prevention* in older postmenopausal women. **The over-correction harmed a generation of symptomatic women** |
| Doctors ignored obvious evidence | The evidence usually did not exist. Someone had to run the trial, and running it was frequently called unethical because the answer was "obvious" |
| We've learned the lesson about surrogate endpoints | CAST was 1989. Torcetrapib was 2006. ACCORD was 2008. **Biological-age clocks are being used as endpoints right now** |

## Open questions

- [ ] Why does adoption of reversal findings take so long? Arthroscopy continued for a decade after Moseley. See knowledge/how-theories-die.md.
- [ ] Should new surgical procedures require sham-controlled trials before adoption? The ethical objections are real and the failure rate when they are run is very high.
- [ ] Would raising the evidentiary bar for adoption cost more lives than it saves through delay? **The strongest argument against everything in this file, and it has never been quantified.**
- [ ] How do you tell Marshall and Warren from a crank in advance? **The demarcation problem, in clinical clothing.** See knowledge/what-is-science.md.
